**1. PulteGroup Q1 2026: Florida new orders +18% but margins compressed to 24.4%**
- **Source:** [PulteGroup Q1 2026 Earnings Call Transcript — Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/23/pultegroup-phm-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- **What happened:** PulteGroup reported an 18% surge in Florida new orders in Q1 2026, but gross margin fell from 27.5% to 24.4% as builder incentives reached 10.9% of gross sales price; overall revenue fell 12% and EPS dropped from $2.57 to $1.79.
- **Hartley Capital implication:** Validates the Florida community-pipeline thesis — demand is real and Brand 9 signage volumes should track community openings — but margin compression signals that builders are tightening ancillary spend, so pricing discipline and contract lock-in with homebuilder clients matters more now; watch whether D.R. Horton (which bucked the trend with solid April results) expands its Florida footprint faster than peers.

**2. Allegra Marketing Print Mail acquires Image360 franchise unit (March 2026)**
- **Source:** [Allegra Baltimore Acquires Image360 Baltimore Downtown — ASI Central](https://members.asicentral.com/news/industry-news/march-2026/allegra-marketing-print-mail-baltimore-acquires-image360-baltimore-downtown/)
- **What happened:** Allegra Marketing Print Mail Baltimore absorbed the Image360 Baltimore Downtown franchise, consolidating print, mail, marketing, signage, and visual communications under one roof — another data point in the slow-rolling franchise-level roll-up pattern across sign/print/marketing verticals.
- **Hartley Capital implication:** Franchise-unit consolidation is compressing the independent owner-operator layer that Brand 9 competes with and potentially sells to; the PE-backed umbrella brands (Allegra parent Alliance Franchise Brands, Image360 parent) are building density, which both raises the competitive bar and makes a Brand 9 tuck-in or bolt-on more attractive to a strategic acquirer; surfaces a wedge for a roll-up thesis at the franchise-unit level before valuations reset upward.

**3. Distyl AI raises $175M at $1.8B to make Fortune 500 firms "AI-native" via workflow automation**
- **Source:** [Top AI Agent Startups 2026 — AI Funding Tracker](https://aifundingtracker.com/top-ai-agent-startups/)
- **What happened:** Distyl AI closed a $175M round valuing it at $1.8B, targeting workflow automation and outcome measurement for Fortune 500 clients across telecom, insurance, manufacturing, and finance — positioning itself as the infrastructure layer for enterprise AI-agent deployment.
- **Hartley Capital implication:** Distyl's sector targeting (manufacturing, finance, insurance) overlaps directly with The Construct's B2B agent-ops wedge; a well-capitalized incumbent entering SMB-adjacent verticals compresses The Construct's runway to establish category ownership before enterprise tooling commoditizes; the $1.8B valuation at this stage suggests the market is pricing the winner-take-most dynamic aggressively — The Construct needs a defensible vertical moat (e.g., signage-ops, homebuilder workflow) rather than a horizontal agent pitch.
