## 1. Florida Real Estate — Inventory Tightening Across Core Markets

**Source:** [Redfin Florida Housing Market](https://www.redfin.com/state/Florida/housing-market) · [Heart Mortgage Blog](https://blog.heartmortgage.com/post/2026-florida-housing-market-trends-2025-2026-median-home-price-inventory-days-on-market)

**What happened:** Florida statewide active inventory fell 8.8% YoY to 202,021 homes (April 2026); statewide median hit $393,701 (+2.3% YoY). Jacksonville, a Brand 9 core market, tightened to ~4-month supply by mid-May — the first sub-5-month reading in over a year.

**Hartley Capital implication:** Validates the new-home demand thesis for Brand 9's homebuilder clients. Supply-constrained Jacksonville and Tampa Bay (median $380K–$440K) support continued community pipeline expansion, which translates directly into sustained signage and wayfinding project volume. Watch for builder land acquisition announcements in H2 2026 as a leading indicator.

## 2. Signage Roll-Up — Allegra Absorbs Image360 Baltimore Downtown

**Source:** [ASI Central](https://members.asicentral.com/news/industry-news/march-2026/allegra-marketing-print-mail-baltimore-acquires-image360-baltimore-downtown/) · [WhatTheyThink](https://whattheythink.com/news/129784-allegra-marketing-print-mail-baltimore-expands-capabilities-acquisition-image360-baltimore-downtown/)

**What happened:** In March 2026, Allegra Marketing Print Mail Baltimore (print/mail franchise) acquired Image360 Baltimore Downtown (signage franchise) — both locally owned franchisees under the Alliance Franchise Brands umbrella — consolidating them into a single full-service shop at 314 N. Charles St.

**Hartley Capital implication:** This franchisee-level roll-up within Alliance Franchise Brands (which controls Allegra, Image360, and print-marketing verticals) is a likely precursor to a platform-level M&A move. If Alliance pursues intra-system consolidation at scale, it creates a multi-service competitor to independents like Brand 9 in local markets. A potential new wedge: evaluate whether Brand 9's model of builder-specialist signage gives it defensible positioning *outside* the franchise system, or whether an Alliance roll-up creates a buyer for Brand 9's footprint.

## 3. AI Agent Platforms — Wonderful Closes $150M Series B at $2B Valuation

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/12/wonderful-raises-150m-series-b-at-2b-valuation/) · [EU Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/03/amsterdam-based-enterprise-ai-agent-platform-wonderful-raises-e129-8-million-series-b-at-e1-7-billion-valuation/)

**What happened:** Amsterdam-based Wonderful — an enterprise AI agent platform that builds, deploys, monitors, and optimizes human-grade agents across complex enterprise workflows — raised $150M (€129.8M) Series B at a $2B valuation in March 2026. Founded 2025 by Bar Winkler (CEO) and Roey Lalazar (CTO), it competes directly in the B2B agent-ops layer.

**Hartley Capital implication:** Well-capitalized generalist agent platforms are compressing the market that The Construct is entering. At $2B, Wonderful is well-funded enough to land enterprise anchor clients before The Construct reaches scale. Thesis pressure: The Construct must accelerate vertical specialization (e.g., builder/franchise workflows, signage asset management) rather than competing on generalist agent infrastructure, or risk being priced out of the enterprise tier entirely.
