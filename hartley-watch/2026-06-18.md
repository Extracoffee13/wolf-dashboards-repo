## 1. Florida Inventory Drawdown — Supply Tightens Despite Sticky Rates

**Source:** [Redfin Florida Housing Market](https://www.redfin.com/state/Florida/housing-market)

**What happened:** Florida statewide for-sale inventory fell 9.89% YoY to ~200,524 homes in May 2026, median price up 1.7% YoY to $395,595, while 30-year rates hold in the 6.0–6.3% band. Jacksonville (~4 months supply) and Orlando (+1.2% price gain expected) remain resilient; Tampa suburban submarkets are the outlier, flagged for potential 8.9% price corrections in overbuilt pockets.

**Hartley Capital implication:** Validates the constructive FL homebuilder thesis for Jacksonville/Orlando/Miami exposure — demand is absorbing inventory even with rates sticky in the 6s. Tampa suburban land positions warrant scrutiny; any deal diligence touching Tampa MSA should underwrite the oversupply pocket explicitly.

## 2. Taylor Morrison Acquired by Berkshire Hathaway for $6.8B

**Source:** [Taylor Morrison Form DEFA14A — SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562476/000119312526252061/d10100ddefa14a.htm)

**What happened:** Taylor Morrison (TMHC) agreed to be acquired by Berkshire Hathaway for $6.8 billion; deal expected to close H2 2026. Q1 2026 results showed 2,268 closings at an avg. price of $578K and a backlog of 3,465 homes ($2.3B value). Meanwhile Lennar cut its FY2026 delivery target to 82–83K homes (down from prior guidance) with margins compressing to ~16%; D.R. Horton posted stronger results with communities up 11% and net orders up 11%.

**Hartley Capital implication:** A Brand 9 homebuilder client moving under Berkshire private ownership changes procurement dynamics — signage/wayfinding master service agreements typically get renegotiated or centralized post-close. Relationship should be proactively reset before H2 close. Separately, the divergence between DHI's strong order volume and LEN's margin compression surfaces DHI as the more durable client / investment candidate in the current rate environment.

## 3. AI Agent Infrastructure Capturing 20.7% of 2026 YTD VC Deal Flow

**Source:** [Angel Investors Network — Enterprise AI Agent Series B Infrastructure Wins 2026](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/venture-capital/enterprise-ai-agent-series-b-why-infrastructure-wins) · [AI Funding Tracker — Top AI Agent Startups 2026](https://aifundingtracker.com/top-ai-agent-startups/)

**What happened:** Capital in the agentic AI stack is bifurcating: vertical AI agents command 48.3% of 2026 YTD deals and 54.6% of capital, while agent execution infrastructure (runtimes, sandboxes, observability, security, identity) accelerated to 20.7% of deal flow. Wonderful (Amsterdam) closed a $150M Series B led by Insight Partners in March 2026 as a representative enterprise agent platform round.

**Hartley Capital implication:** Infra layer is commoditizing fast under sustained VC pressure — The Construct's defensible position is NOT generalized runtime plumbing but vertical orchestration for specific high-value domains (e.g., homebuilder ops, signage procurement workflows). If The Construct is currently competing on infra features, that is a wedge risk; if it has staked out a vertical, this funding wave validates the domain-specific approach.
