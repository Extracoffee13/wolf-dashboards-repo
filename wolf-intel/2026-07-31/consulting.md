## 1. Agentic AI Strategy for CIOs and CTOs in 2026 (BCG)

**Firm:** BCG
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/agentic-ai-strategy-cio-cto

**Executive summary:** BCG's early-July 2026 practitioner interview series (published under the working title "The Agentic Leadership Playbook") reports that roughly 5% of companies have become truly "agent-first," while the rest are clustered below 30% adoption — a bimodal split rather than a smooth adoption curve. The leaders are cutting costs 15–20% across functions they've re-architected around agents, not the ones that bolted agents onto existing workflows. BCG's practitioners are blunt that the gating factor isn't model capability: success is roughly 70% people and change management, 30% algorithms. The piece pairs with a companion ("How CIOs Can Govern Agentic AI at Scale") arguing that agent governance is now a core leadership responsibility spanning the C-suite, not a technical hygiene task owned by IT.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is a direct data point for Hartley Capital's agent-AI-at-scale thesis — the bottleneck the market is converging on is organizational (workflow redesign, governance, change management), not model quality, which is exactly the wedge an agent-ops consultancy/operator should be selling into rather than another model wrapper.

**Score: 4** — sharpens a thesis we hold (agent ops at scale is an org-design problem, not a tooling problem).

## 2. Global Private Equity Report 2026 / Midyear PE Report (Bain & Company)

**Firm:** Bain & Company
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/welcome-to-a-new-era-global-private-equity-report-2026/ ; https://www.bain.com/globalassets/noindex/2026/bain-report_global-private-equity-report-2026.pdf ; midyear update: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/winning-firms-will-focus-on-what-they-can-control-weather-the-rest-as-triple-shock-brakes-private-equitys-latest-revival-bain--company-2026-midyear-pe-report-302793107.html

**Executive summary:** Bain's flagship 2026 PE report (with a June 2026 midyear update) finds the global PE recovery has stalled under three overlapping shocks that dampened dealmaking, fundraising, and exits in H1 2026. Inside that soft macro, one structural trend keeps compounding: add-ons now account for roughly three-quarters of US buyout deal count, up from ~60% a decade ago. Bain's read is that "financial engineering alone" no longer works — winners are the firms with disciplined value-creation plans, proactive AI adoption inside portfolio companies, and a genuine operational "right to win," concentrating fewer, better bets rather than spraying capital.

**What it means for The Construct: **Validates and sharpens Hartley Capital's core roll-up thesis at the macro level — buy-and-build isn't a side strategy anymore, it's ~75% of how buyouts get done, which means the differentiation has to come from operating-model discipline (including AI-enabled portfolio ops) rather than deal sourcing or leverage alone.

**Score: 4** — sharpens a thesis we hold (roll-ups as the dominant PE mechanism, operational excellence as the differentiator).

## 3. The Intelligence Layer: How Agentic AI Can Connect the Infrastructure Industry (EY, with University of Cambridge / NVIDIA / Futurity Systems)

**Firm:** EY
**Source:** https://www.ey.com/en_cn/newsroom/2026/07/agentic-ai-poised-to-boost-global-infrastructure-productivity-new-ey-report-finds

**Executive summary:** EY's infrastructure-sector report (China-market release dated July 2026, following earlier regional releases in May) argues global infrastructure productivity is capped not by a technology gap but by fragmentation — segmented data, systems, and stakeholders that force manual coordination and periodic reporting instead of continuous, real-time visibility. Citing a review of 100+ construction productivity studies from Cambridge's Laing O'Rourke Centre, the report notes rework alone can eat up to 15% of total project costs. Against a backdrop of a $64T global infrastructure funding gap (and ~$140T of needed investment through 2050), EY frames agentic AI as a coordination layer that continuously routes data across systems while humans retain judgment and accountability.

**What it means for The Construct:** Background-tier relevance to Brand 9 — the "rework = 15% of project cost from coordination failure" framing is a reusable talking point for pitching monument-signage/wayfinding as a coordination-and-visibility problem to homebuilders and GCs, but there's no direct signage or FL-market angle in this report.

**Score: 3** — useful background (reusable pitch framing, not a thesis-moving finding).
