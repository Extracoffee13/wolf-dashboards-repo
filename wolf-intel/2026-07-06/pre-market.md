Chips are leading a relief rally back into US futures after the late-June semiconductor slump, and Europe is grinding to fresh highs — but the overnight tape is not one clean risk-on signal. ## Macro Backdrop — the real story this week

1. **Warsh vs. the jobs number.** New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh told the ECB's Sintra forum on Wed Jul 1 that "prices are too high" — hawkish framing that pushed the 10Y to ~4.481%, 2Y to ~4.176%, 30Y to ~4.973%. Markets are pricing ~73% odds of a hold at the July FOMC and ~65% odds of at least a quarter-point **hike** by September — a genuine regime shift from the cut-cycle narrative that dominated earlier in 2026 (some outlets are calling this the "Warsh Shock"). Then, one day later (Thu Jul 2), the June jobs report came in soft: nonfarm payrolls +57,000 vs. 115,000 consensus, unemployment rate down to 4.2% only because labor force participation fell to 61.5% (lowest since March 2021), and April/May were revised down a combined 74,000. Kiplinger's take: the weak print "quiets the rate-hike conversation." Those two signals are in direct tension.
2. **FOMC minutes, Wednesday Jul 8** — the June meeting was Warsh's first as chair. This is the week's key catalyst: does the committee's internal language lean toward the hawkish Sintra rhetoric or toward accommodating the soft jobs data?
3. **Tariff cliff, July 24** — the 10% Section 122 global tariff hits its 150-day statutory limit and expires unless Congress acts. USTR is running public comment hearings in early July on a proposed 10-12.5% Section 301 replacement targeting ~60 countries under a forced-labor investigation, with Annex A exemptions expected to mirror the current exclusion list. EU-origin goods are unaffected (locked into the 15% EU trade-deal rate since Jul 1).
4. **Pharma tariff deadline, July 31** — proposed 100% tariff on branded pharma imports, reducible to 20% for companies that commit to US onshoring by the deadline. Watch large-cap pharma for headline risk building through the month.
