## 1. Deloitte — "Agentic AI Is Scaling Faster Than Guardrails"

**Firm:** Deloitte Insights
**URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html
**Published:** Primary survey wave dated 2026-04-24, part of the 2026 *State of AI in the Enterprise* series (fielded Aug–Sep 2025, n=3,235 IT/business leaders across 24 countries); still driving active secondary coverage this week (TechRadar, WebProNews).

**Summary:** Deloitte's enterprise AI survey documents a widening gap between agent-adoption ambition and governance readiness. By 2027, 74% of respondents expect at least "moderate" agentic AI use, and 5% expect agents to be a core operating component of the business — yet only 21% currently have a mature governance model in place (clear agent boundaries, real-time monitoring, audit trails). Roughly 80% of enterprises are racing to deploy autonomy without the control plane to contain it, which Deloitte frames as the central operational risk of the next 18 months.

**What it means for The Construct:** WOLF's circuit-breaker / kill-criteria discipline (hard daily/weekly loss limits, published Sharpe and drawdown, a public 90-day shutdown criterion) is already the minority-case governance model Deloitte says 80% of enterprises lack — that gap is a credibility asset for Hartley Capital pitches, not just internal hygiene.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the thesis that governance/audit infrastructure is a moat for agent-heavy operators, not overhead.

## 2. KPMG — Global AI Pulse: Q2 2026

**Firm:** KPMG
**URL:** https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/q2-ai-pulse-2026.html
**Published:** 2026-06-24 (survey of 2,000+ business leaders across 20 countries)

**Summary:** KPMG's quarterly pulse shows agent deployment holding steady in aggregate but with rising pragmatism underneath: 42% of leaders report only partial visibility into AI/agent spend, and 49% have already scaled back agent deployments because costs outweighed the benefits delivered. The split is stark — leaders with real-time cost-monitoring dashboards and cost reviews embedded in the AI approval process are 5x more likely to report established ROI (15% vs. 3%) than those without. KPMG frames this quarter as the "cost governance" inflection point: model capability is no longer the binding constraint on scaling multi-agent systems — spend visibility is.

**What it means for The Construct:** Direct, quantified justification for building agent-cost telemetry (token spend, per-agent P&L attribution) into Hartley Capital's ops stack now, before scale — the 5x ROI-visibility gap KPMG measured is exactly the failure mode a token-spend dashboard on WOLF's own agent fleet would preempt.

**Score: 4** — actionable, quantified, and squarely in the agent-ops-at-scale lane.

## 3. Deloitte — 2026 Private Equity Outlook (Asia Pacific Almanac + global mid-year M&A Trends Pulse)

**Firm:** Deloitte
**URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/ap/en/perspectives/2026-asia-pacific-private-equity-almanac.html
**Published:** 2026 mid-year cycle (Almanac + M&A Trends Pulse survey wave, June 2026)

**Summary:** Deloitte's PE outlook shows the market bifurcating between a small number of mega-deals (twenty deals accounted for 33% of total 2025 US deal value) and a much larger, flat-volume population of mid-market activity. The 2026 playbook firms are converging on: bolt-on acquisitions over new platform deals (lower equity commitment, known industries), shared portfolio-operations / cross-portfolio service centers for efficiency, and deeper operational value creation replacing pure financial engineering as the primary return driver.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the institutional-grade version of what Hartley Capital is already betting on — bolt-on discipline plus a shared operating layer (read: a portfolio-wide agent stack) over paying up for platform deals. It's a tailwind for pitching the roll-up thesis to allocators who've just read this exact framing from a Big Four almanac.

**Score: 4** — validates the specific mechanics (bolt-on + shared ops center) of the roll-up engine, not just the general PE tailwind.
