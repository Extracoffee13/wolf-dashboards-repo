## PAPER 1 — Score: 4 (Sharpens a thesis)

**Title:** The AI-First Real Estate Company: An Opportunity for Structural Advantage  
**Firm:** BCG  
**Date:** May 2026 (approx)  
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-ai-first-real-estate-company-advantage  
**Companion PDF:** https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-ai-first-companies-real-estate.pdf  

### Executive Summary

BCG surveyed AI adoption across sectors and found real estate sitting near the bottom of the league table — only 25% of real estate firms qualify as "AI leaders" versus 40% across all industries, and the sector is investing roughly half the cross-industry average in AI, lagging even asset-heavy sectors like utilities. The structural causes are fragmented data landscapes and historically asset-centric operating models that resist horizontal process integration. Against that backdrop, BCG identifies an early-mover opportunity that is measurable and time-bounded: AI-first development companies (DevCos) are already compressing project timelines by approximately 30% and accelerating capital deployment cycles. The full-stack AI-first RE firm creates value across development, operations, and investment — but only with CEO-led transformation, explicit AI ownership at the top, and a willingness to question whether existing processes should exist at all rather than simply automating them.

### What It Means for The Construct

Real estate is the most AI-laggard major asset class precisely because its fragmented, asset-centric model makes horizontal AI deployment hard — which is exactly the friction point where Brand 9's systemized signage + wayfinding workflow can undercut incumbents on speed and cost while homebuilder clients are still debating vendor RFPs. For Hartley Capital, this is a PE roll-up diagnostic: acquire RE-adjacent operators, inject AI capability, and capture the 20–30% operational gain that 75% of incumbents will never achieve because their operating model prevents it.

## PAPER 2 — Score: 4 (Sharpens a thesis)

**Title:** From Digital Twin to Agentic Twin  
**Firm:** Roland Berger  
**Date:** April 27, 2026 (confirmed)  
**URL:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/From-digital-twin-to-agentic-twin.html  

### Executive Summary

Roland Berger introduces the "agentic twin" as the next evolution beyond digital twins: a zero-legacy, AI-native simulation of your own organization, built not to optimize existing operations but to stress-test what a fully autonomous competitor with no legacy infrastructure would do to your business. The framework is a diagnostic tool — build the AI-native version of yourself first, identify the gap between that model and your current state, then roadmap toward closing it before a new entrant does. The paper identifies three recurring failure modes: companies automate what exists rather than questioning whether it should exist; AI strategies are built around continuous improvement rather than process elimination; and management culture gravitates toward low-disruption pilots rather than the structural rewiring that agentic systems actually require. Organizations that begin this stress-test now will have more options; those that wait until competitive pressure is obvious will be making high-stakes decisions under time compression.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital's PE due diligence process, the agentic twin framework is the right analytical lens for portfolio company assessment — model the AI-native version of an acquisition target before the deal closes to quantify the transformation upside and identify the structural blockers. For The Construct's internal agent architecture (WOLF, Forge, Signal, etc.), this is a validation that the multi-agent operating model we're building is not a novelty but the correct terminal state for a lean, PE-scale family office.

## PAPER 3 — Score: 5 (Changes a thesis)

**Title:** The Self-Driving Portfolio: Agentic Architecture for Institutional Asset Management  
**Source:** arXiv (q-fin / cs.AI)  
**arXiv ID:** 2604.02279  
**Date:** April 2, 2026 (submitted); presented at context of AAMAS 2026 (May 25–29, Paphos, Cyprus — happening this week)  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02279  
**Authors:** Andrew Ang (Tau Balance / Columbia University), Nazym Azimbayev, Andrey Kim (Altbridge)  

### Executive Summary

This paper presents the first fully-specified, working blueprint for an agentic institutional portfolio management system. The architecture deploys approximately 50 specialized agents to produce capital market assumptions, construct portfolios via over 20 competing methods, and then critique and vote on each other's outputs. A researcher agent proposes portfolio construction methods not yet represented in the ensemble; a meta-agent continuously reviews past forecasts against realized returns and rewrites agent code and prompts autonomously to improve future performance — a self-improving loop operating within defined risk rails. The entire pipeline is governed by the Investment Policy Statement (IPS), the same legal/fiduciary document that guides human portfolio managers. The result shifts the portfolio manager's role from analytical execution to governance and oversight. Andrew Ang, the lead author, is a former BlackRock global head of factor investing and Columbia Business School professor — this is not a toy paper from a graduate student; it is a practitioner blueprint from someone who ran one of the world's largest systematic investment operations.

### What It Means for The Construct

This is the technical architecture for Hartley Capital's alpha engine, not a 2028 roadmap — a working 2026 implementation from credible institutional practitioners. The IPS-as-governance-document concept is the specific innovation: it solves the fiduciary compliance problem that has blocked family offices from deploying autonomous agents in portfolio management. This paper should move from the research queue to the build queue.
