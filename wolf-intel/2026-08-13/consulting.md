## 1. Which Companies Will Capture Value From AI in 2026

**Firm:** BCG
**Published:** August 13, 2026 (today)
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/which-companies-capture-value-from-ai

**Summary:** BCG's latest AI-value-capture analysis argues the AI boom's spoils have been narrowly distributed so far — concentrated in the physical foundation of AI (chip designers, memory/equipment makers, gas-turbine and grid suppliers) rather than broadly across the economy, even as hyperscaler capex runs toward $750B in 2026 and 65%+ of S&P 500 companies now reference AI on earnings calls. Only 5% of companies qualify as "future-built" — systematically building AI capability across functions — and those firms see roughly double the revenue lift and 40% greater cost reduction versus laggards. Industry maturity is bifurcated: software, telecom, and fintech lead; fashion/luxury, chemicals, and **real estate and construction lag**. BCG frames the next phase as a value-pool reallocation fight, not a productivity story — the question isn't whether AI creates value, but who is structurally positioned to capture it.

**What it means for The Construct:** Real estate/construction sitting at the bottom of BCG's AI-maturity ranking is the whitespace confirmation for Brand 9's whole play — an under-digitized vertical is exactly where being the first credible AI-visibility mover (answer pages, GBP optimization, entity schema) compounds instead of getting arbitraged away by sophisticated incumbents. For Hartley, the "5% future-built" framing sharpens the agent-AI thesis toward infrastructure and operationally-embedded plays over generic AI-adoption stories — the moat is in the workflow, not the model.

**Score: 4** — sharpens both engines' theses with a specific, falsifiable industry-maturity data point.

## 2. Q2'26 Pulse of Private Equity — Global insights

**Firm:** KPMG
**Published:** August 2026 (KPMG Private Enterprise, dp.kpmg.com/en/insights/2026/08/)
**Source:** https://kpmg.com/dp/en/insights/2026/08/pulse-of-private-equity.html

**Summary:** KPMG's Q2'26 PE pulse shows global deal value at roughly $1 trillion across 9,294 deals in H1 2026, with rolling-twelve-month investment and volume both down modestly ($2.4T→$2.3T, 21,060→20,105 deals). The read is a market in consolidation, not retreat: IPO conditions in the US are improving, exit activity is picking up among large PE houses, and the second half looks constructive. The defining behavioral shift is that sponsors are increasingly **passing on smaller transactions** and concentrating dry powder in fewer, larger, high-conviction deals — the quarter's headline deals (KKR's $10B Helix Digital Infrastructure, Stonepeak/Bernhard's $4.5B Cleco buyout, KKR's $4.5B EDF renewables deal) are all AI-infrastructure- or energy-adjacent megadeals.

**What it means for The Construct:** If the largest sponsors are systematically vacating the small-deal end of the market to chase AI-infrastructure and energy megadeals, that's a competitive-density signal in Hartley's favor for disciplined, smaller-scale roll-ups in unglamorous verticals (signage, local trades, regional real estate services) — less capital chasing the same assets means better entry multiples, not worse. The risk is the inverse read: shrinking overall deal volume could just as easily mean financing and exit liquidity get tighter for smaller sponsors too, not only easier competitively.

**Score: 3** — useful background on capital-allocation behavior in PE, directly load-bearing for Hartley's roll-up entry-multiple assumptions but not thesis-changing on its own.
