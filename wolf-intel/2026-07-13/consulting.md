## 1. The Future of Opex in the Agent Economy
**Firm:** Bain & Company | **Published:** 2026-05-22
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/the-future-of-opex-in-the-agent-economy/

**Summary:** Bain argues that agent and token costs are on track to displace 20–30% of today's headcount operating expense inside technology-forward organizations, shifting the classic opex mix from ~95%+ headcount toward a 70–80% headcount / 20–30% token-and-agent-cost split. Crucially, Bain is explicit that there's no smooth glide path: the transition is "wildly nonlinear," with cost overlaps (you pay for both the agent and the human it's replacing during the handoff period), organizational whiplash, and quarters where the unit economics simply don't pencil out. A side data point: even at only 1–2% of total opex today, token spend is already unbudgeted and concentrated — the top 5% of users consume more tokens than the other 95% combined, meaning token cost is a power-law problem, not an average-case one.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is close to a direct model of what Hartley Capital needs underwritten into every portfolio-company deal model — agent-driven opex reduction is real but arrives in lumpy, overlapping waves, not a steady glide path, so roll-up integration timelines and EBITDA-ramp assumptions should build in a "double-run" cost period rather than assuming clean headcount-for-agent substitution.

**Score: 5** — sharpens (and partially validates) a thesis we already hold about agent-driven opex transformation in PE-owned businesses.

## 2. Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest
**Firm:** Bain & Company | **Published:** ~2026-06
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/

**Summary:** Bain's midyear check-in says the PE recovery that was building into 2026 has stalled under a "triple shock" — tech deal value alone fell 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 as AI-valuation anxiety froze large software transactions. The headline framework, "12 is the new 5": a deal that needed only ~5% annual EBITDA growth to hit a 2.5x return a decade ago now needs roughly 10–12%, because cheap debt and easy multiple expansion — the return drivers that bailed out mediocre operators for fifteen years — are gone for the foreseeable future. Winning firms are responding by treating AI not as a cost-cutting bolt-on but as a genuine value-creation lever: new products, new revenue lines, sharper decision-making at the firm level, not just headcount reduction.

**What it means for The Construct: ** Multiple arbitrage is dead as a roll-up thesis — Hartley Capital's underwriting has to assume real double-digit organic EBITDA growth per asset, which raises the bar on how much of that growth needs to come from agent-driven operating leverage (tying directly back to paper #1) rather than financial engineering.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the roll-up thesis by quantifying exactly how much harder organic value creation now has to work.

## 3. AI Investment and Agent Deployment Hold Steady Amid Growing Focus on Pragmatism (Q2 2026 AI Pulse)
**Firm:** KPMG | **Published:** 2026-06-24
**Source:** https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/q2-ai-pulse-2026.html

**Summary:** KPMG's quarterly pulse survey shows agent deployment holding roughly flat quarter over quarter (53% vs. 55%), but the share of organizations orchestrating multiple agents across workflows doubled from 9% to 18% — the shift is from single-agent point solutions to coordinated, enterprise-wide multi-agent systems. The catch: while two-thirds of organizations now have monitoring dashboards (66%) and approval workflows (61%) for their agents, only 26% report full, real-time visibility into what their AI systems actually cost to operate. The report frames this as the emerging fault line — deployment is outpacing cost observability.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is a direct read on where the market sits on "agent ops at scale" — multi-agent orchestration is becoming normal practice (validating WOLF/PRAXIS's own architecture), but the cost-observability gap (74% of orgs flying blind on agent spend) is exactly the failure mode Hartley Capital-backed operators need to avoid before they lean on agent-driven margin expansion in deal models.

**Score: 4** — useful, current market calibration for exactly the "agent ops at scale" watch item on our list; not thesis-changing but a hard data point worth tracking quarter to quarter.
