## Scored papers (≥3, top 3)

### 1. Bain & Company — Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest"

- **Firm:** Bain & Company
- **Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/ (press release: https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/winning-firms-will-focus-on-what-they-can-control-weather-the-rest-as-triple-shock-brakes-private-equitys-latest-revival-bain--company-2026-midyear-pe-report/)
- **Published:** ~June 8, 2026 (~7 weeks old — outside the strict window but the most substantive recent PE-strategy piece found)
- **Score: 5**

Bain describes a "triple shock" — macro uncertainty, valuation compression, and liquidity constraints — braking PE's 2025 recovery. Tech deal value fell roughly 70% from Q4'25 to Q1'26 on AI-valuation anxiety. The report's central claim: winning firms in this cycle aren't distinguished by the size of their AI budget, but by whether they've closed the gap between *talking about* AI and actually *booking it to EBITDA*. It pushes hard on Day-1 execution of value-creation plans over prolonged diligence cycles.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is close to a direct validation of the Hartley Capital roll-up thesis — the operative distinction Bain draws (AI-narrative vs. AI-booked-to-EBITDA) is exactly the discipline The Construct is trying to operationalize across portfolio companies like Brand 9. Use this as the external citation when justifying Day-1 agent deployment over a slow diligence-first rollout.

### 2. BCG — "AI-First Enterprise Operations: Reinventing the Operating System of Work"

- **Firm:** BCG / BCG Henderson Institute
- **Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/reinventing-the-operating-system-of-work-with-ai
- **Published:** ~July 8, 2026 (~16 days old)
- **Score: 5**

BCG argues AI value capture requires end-to-end process redesign built around agents, not automation bolted onto an unchanged workflow. Firms that redesigned control flow and orchestration around "outcome-managed agents" reportedly saw ~60% cost reductions, versus under 20% for firms that just automated existing steps. Frames agent deployment explicitly as an operating-model problem, not a tooling problem.

**What it means for The Construct:** Gives a concrete benchmark (60% vs. under 20%) to argue that shallow agent adoption inside portfolio companies won't move EBITDA — only redesigning the operating model around agents will. Directly applicable language for how The Construct pitches its own C-Suite-of-agents model to acquisition targets.

### 3. KPMG — Q2 2026 AI Pulse: Asset Management & Private Equity

- **Firm:** KPMG
- **Source:** https://kpmg.com/us/en/media/news/q2-ai-pulse-2026.html (survey PDF: https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/corporate-communications/pdf/2026/AIPulseSurvey_Q2_FINAL.pdf)
- **Published:** ~June 16, 2026 (~5–6 weeks old)
- **Score: 4**

Survey-based: 24% of asset management/PE firms have AI agents in production today, 68% are piloting. Average planned AI spend is ~$101–103M over the next 12 months across respondents. Only 26–36% have real-time, token-level cost visibility into agent spend — governance and workforce readiness are lagging deployment speed. Firms that differentiate are using agents for cross-functional alignment and shared insight generation, not just point automation.

**What it means for The Construct:** A peer benchmark for what "agent maturity" looks like specifically in PE/AM. The cost-governance gap (only ~30% have real-time visibility into agent spend) is a gap The Construct should close ahead of scaling its own agent fleet — cheap insurance against an expensive blind spot other funds are currently running with.
