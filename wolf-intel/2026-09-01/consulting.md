## Methodology and a caveat up front

Direct fetch access to mckinsey.com, bcg.com, bain.com, and arxiv.org is blocked by this session's network egress policy — those domains returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED` on every attempt. Today's scan therefore ran entirely on web search rather than by paging through each firm's publications index directly. That's a materially weaker method for confirming exact publish dates: search snippets surface a firm's most *discoverable* content, not necessarily its most *recent*, and several results below turned out to be evergreen 2026 annual-cycle reports (published months ago) rather than anything from the last 24-48h. I've held the line on the ≥3 relevance bar rather than padding with stale-but-relevant material, and flagged every date I could and couldn't confirm.

## What I found instead (below the bar, logged for context)

**arXiv:2608.30522 — "Tariff Threats, Macroeconomic Expectations, and Policy Communication Strategies: Experiments Based on a Multi-Agent System"** (econ.GN, submitted Aug 31, 2026 — the one item I could confirm inside the 24-48h window). Uses a multi-agent simulation to test how central-bank communication style shapes macro expectations. Relevant to the "multi-agent systems" bucket only at the architecture level (agents-as-economic-actors, not agents-as-operational-tooling); nothing here touches PE ops, real estate, or signage. **Score: 2** — interesting methodologically, no line to a thesis we hold.

**Cross-firm pattern, not a single paper**: search results this week (not confined to 24-48h — spanning roughly the last month, Aug 5–31) converged on the same story from four independent sources: KPMG's Q2 AI Pulse (nearly half of executives have pulled back agent deployments over token-metering costs), McKinsey's agentic-AI cost pieces (agentic tasks run ~1,000x the tokens of a chat task, and ~60% of that cost is verification/repair rather than the first answer), BCG's "Agentic AI Strategy for CIOs and CTOs" (only 5% of companies are actually agent-first despite the hype cycle), and Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise (governance maturity lagging autonomy). No single one of these is a 24-48h item, so none qualifies as today's paper — but the pattern itself is worth naming because it's the same signal from four firms that don't coordinate: **enterprise agentic AI is hitting a cost-and-governance wall well before it hits a capability wall.** That's the thread I pulled into the public brief below and into today's research target.

## What it means for The Construct

The cost/governance-wall pattern is a live input to the Hartley Capital agent-ops thesis: if PE-portfolio agent deployments are getting throttled by token economics before they scale, that's a headwind for any roll-up thesis premised on "bolt on agentic AI, cut headcount fast." It argues for underwriting agent-ops value creation on a longer, cost-disciplined timeline rather than a land-grab one. No implication for the Brand 9 signage/homebuilder engine today — nothing scanned touched FL real estate, monument signage, or homebuilder demand specifically.
