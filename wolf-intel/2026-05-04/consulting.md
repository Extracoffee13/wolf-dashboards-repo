### PAPER 1 · Score: 4
**"How Agentic AI Can Reshape Real Estate's Operating Model"**  
**Firm:** McKinsey & Company  
**Published:** March 4, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/real-estate/our-insights/how-agentic-ai-can-reshape-real-estates-operating-model

**Executive Summary:**  
McKinsey argues that the winning move in real estate is not deploying isolated AI use cases but redesigning entire operational domains — leasing, facilities, capital allocation — around a common layer of workflows, data, and agent permissions. The MGI labor productivity analysis embedded in the piece estimates $430–550B in annual value unlock globally across real estate, construction, and development from AI automation of knowledge work. The key insight is organizational, not technical: the biggest blocker to AI value capture is not model quality but the governance and permission structures that allow agents to actually execute (rather than recommend). In high-expertise workflows, humans don't outsource judgment because software exists — they trust automation when they can supervise it and step back in. The paper explicitly frames forward-thinking RE portfolios as "operating systems" rather than collections of properties.

**What It Means for The Construct:**  
For Brand 9, this is the strongest external validation yet of the thesis that the built environment becomes a platform — signage, wayfinding, and physical brand identity are early infrastructure layers in that operating system, not afterthoughts. For Hartley Capital, the $430–550B value unlock estimate — and the argument that governance/permission design is the bottleneck — maps directly onto the agent ops thesis: the firms that nail agent governance first will capture disproportionate value in RE-adjacent roll-ups.

### PAPER 2 · Score: 4
**"Agent Capsules: Quality-Gated Granularity Control for Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines"**  
**Source:** arxiv cs.AI  
**arXiv ID:** 2605.00410  
**Submitted:** May 1, 2026  
**Author:** Aninda Ray  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00410

**Executive Summary:**  
A single-author paper that presents Agent Capsules, an adaptive execution runtime treating multi-agent LLM pipelines as optimization problems with empirical quality constraints. The framework instruments coordination overhead per group, scores composition opportunity, selects among three compound execution strategies, and gates every mode switch on rolling-mean output quality. Benchmarks are striking: against a 14-agent competitive intelligence pipeline built in LangGraph, Agent Capsules achieves 51% fewer fine-mode input tokens and 42% fewer compound-mode input tokens at *improved* quality. Against a 5-agent due diligence pipeline (DSPy), it uses 19% fewer tokens than uncompiled DSPy at quality parity, and 68% fewer tokens than MIPROv2 at higher quality. The paper demonstrates that pipeline-level token governance is tractable and high-return.

**What It Means for The Construct:**  
If Hartley Capital is running or planning multi-agent due diligence and competitive intelligence workflows, this is the most directly actionable research in the window — a 51–68% cost reduction in agent pipeline operation is the difference between a marginal tool and a core infrastructure advantage. The "due diligence pipeline" benchmark is not accidental; this paper is written for the PE and corporate intelligence use case.

### PAPER 3 · Score: 3
**"Private Equity Pulse: Key Takeaways from Q1 2026"**  
**Firm:** EY (Ernst & Young)  
**Published:** ~April 28–29, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/private-equity/pulse

**Executive Summary:**  
EY's quarterly PE market read for Q1 2026 documents a 12% decline in deal value vs. Q1 2025 ($172B across 110 announced deals), driven by geopolitical uncertainty and — more significantly — growing awareness of AI-related disruption in software-heavy portfolio companies. Tech PE fell from ~30% of global PE deployment by value in 2025 to just over 10% in Q1 2026. The report frames this not as a tech retreat but a differentiation event: generalist investors are diversifying while specialist investors are doubling down on their ability to distinguish AI-enabled business models from AI-disrupted ones. Meanwhile, two-thirds of PE firms now expect to invest more than 25% of budget in AI by end of 2026 — but only as a *value creation lever in portfolio companies*, not as thematic deal flow.

**What It Means for The Construct:**  
The collapse in tech PE deployment concentration is a direct opening for thesis-driven investors like Hartley Capital: generalists are pulling back, specialist pricing power is rising, and the market will reward LPs who can actually parse AI readiness at the portfolio company level — exactly what an agent-assisted diligence operation enables.
