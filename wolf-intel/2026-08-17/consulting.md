## 1. Inside the AI-First Private Equity Firm

**Firm:** BCG (Boston Consulting Group)
**Published:** April 24, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/inside-the-ai-first-private-equity-firm

**Summary:** BCG argues most PE firms have deployed AI tools across their portfolios but stopped there — few have done the harder work of rewiring the operating model and value proposition around AI and agents. The piece lays out a three-phase framework (deploy → reshape → invent) distinguishing firms that bolt AI onto existing workflows from firms that redesign decision rights, org structure, and how value gets created in the first place. It's blunt that "deploy" alone doesn't move P&L — sponsors have to actively drive change management inside portfolio companies, not just license tools and hope adoption follows.

**What it means for The Construct:** Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis is a bet that AI-native operating models beat AI-adjacent ones at the portfolio-company level — BCG's deploy/reshape/invent ladder is a ready-made diagnostic to run on every acquisition target *before* close, not as a post-close nice-to-have.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the Hartley Capital roll-up thesis by giving it a concrete maturity model instead of a vibe.

## 2. Global Asset Management Report 2026: Rebuilding Asset Management for an AI-First World

**Firm:** BCG
**Published:** June 19, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/rebuilding-asset-management-for-an-ai-first-world

**Summary:** BCG's flagship asset-management report frames 2026 as the year the competitive axis shifts from product performance to ecosystem embedding — digital wealth platforms and neobrokers already account for up to 60% of new retail inflows at some leading managers. The winners, BCG argues, won't necessarily run the best-performing funds; they'll be the ones who embed their offerings into multi-product distribution ecosystems and own the resulting data feedback loop that informs product design and client engagement. In private markets specifically, the report flags AI agents already processing capital calls, reading unstructured LP documents, and autonomously validating waterfall distributions — real ops automation, not a pilot.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the strategic frame Hartley's multi-strategy ops platform should be building toward — not "a better fund," but a better data feedback loop wrapped in a fund wrapper, with the waterfall/capital-call automation piece being the nearest-term, highest-ROI agent build.

**Score: 3** — useful background that reinforces rather than reshapes the current hedge-fund-ops thesis.
