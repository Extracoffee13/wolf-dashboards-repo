### 1. "Family offices adjust asset allocation to meet evolving challenges" — Roland Berger
**Source:** rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html (also syndicated via mynewsdesk.com/rolandberger)
**Date:** July 2026 (exact day within the 14–16 window not independently confirmed; multiple sources describe it as a current release)
**Score: 4**

Survey of 88 family offices in the DACH region (fielded Dec 2025–Mar 2026). Headline finding: geopolitical tension has overtaken rate uncertainty as the top external risk cited (88%), and in response, family offices are re-weighting toward private equity — both fund commitments and direct deals — as their strongest-growth asset class, with more than half planning to increase PE allocation over the next cycle. Infrastructure, healthcare, and AI rank as top sector priorities; internally, cybersecurity (87%) and digitalization (81%) dominate operational concerns; 90% of respondents invest globally rather than staying home-region.

**What it means for The Construct:** Family offices are the exact LP profile Hartley Capital is built to court, and this data says they're actively increasing direct-deal PE appetite right now, not just fund commitments — sharpens the thesis that direct-to-operator conversations (skip the fund-of-funds layer) are the right access point, and that leading with an AI/digitalization operating story will land, since that's already a named internal priority for this LP base.

### 2. "The Four Pillars CIOs Can Use to Scale Agentic AI" (also indexed as "Scaling Agents Without Creating Your Next Tech Legacy") — BCG
**Source:** bcg.com/publications/2026/the-four-pillars-cios-can-use-to-scale-agentic-ai (URL reconstructed from search index; direct fetch blocked)
**Date:** ~July 14, 2026
**Score: 4**

Argues enterprises have a 6–12 month window to retrofit governance, architecture, and accountability structures around agentic AI before today's ad-hoc agent deployments calcify into unmanageable technical debt — and frames this explicitly as a C-suite leadership problem, not an IT backlog item. Companion Oliver Wyman Forum research (below) independently corroborates the adoption-gap framing with hard numbers.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is a direct read on the risk profile of running WOLF/Praxis and the rest of the C-Suite agent stack — the warning about governance debt compounding is a reason to keep investing in the praxis-inbox/lessons-loop discipline now, while the fleet is still small, rather than retrofitting later; it also validates positioning Hartley Capital's agent-native operating model as a genuine differentiator versus portcos still running pilot-stage AI.

### 3. "Capitalizing Risk and Regulation: Sequential Shocks in Florida's Condominium Market" — arXiv (econ.GN)
**Source:** arxiv.org/abs/2607.02791
**Date:** early July 2026 (arXiv:2607.02791)
**Score: 3**

Academic study of how sequential regulatory and risk shocks — read: post-Surfside structural-inspection mandates layered on insurance-cost shocks — get capitalized into Florida condominium prices. Not on signage or homebuilders directly, but it's the closest on-thesis real-estate-economics paper found this week, and it's methodologically useful for anyone pricing FL real estate risk.

**What it means for The Construct:** Useful background, not action-forcing — confirms that FL residential/condo valuations are still absorbing regulatory-shock repricing, which is relevant context for any Brand 9 client work tied to FL condo or HOA-adjacent developments (monument signage, wayfinding) where the underlying real estate economics are in flux.
