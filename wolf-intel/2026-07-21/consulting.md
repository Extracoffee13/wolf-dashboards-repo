## 1. KPMG — "Reorganise or fall behind: The real race in the AI decade"

- **Firm:** KPMG (India insights)
- **Published:** July 2026
- **Source:** https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2026/07/reorganise-or-fall-behind-the-real-race-in-the-ai-decade.html

**Summary:** KPMG's whitepaper argues the winners of the "AI decade" won't be the organizations that adopted AI first — they'll be the ones that reorganized their operating models, workforce strategies, and capability systems around AI first. Built from roundtables and structured interviews with senior industry leaders, the core diagnosis is that most enterprises have invested heavily in pilots, tools, and training but have left the actual machinery of the organization — how work is structured, how decisions get made, how value gets created — untouched. AI is framed as evolving from a productivity add-on into the operating logic of the business itself. The paper's punchline: competitive advantage accrues to whoever reorganizes around AI earliest, with clarity and sequence — not to whoever has the best model or the most pilots running.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is a direct external validation of the bet already made — that The Construct's structural advantage isn't "we use AI," it's that the org chart itself (agents as functional units, not tools bolted onto a legacy structure) is AI-native from day one. For Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis specifically, this sharpens the diligence question on any acquisition target: not "do they use AI," but "could their org survive being rebuilt around agent-first operations," which is a much harder and more valuable filter.

**Score: 4/5** — Sharpens a thesis we hold.

## 2. Oliver Wyman Forum — "The Industrial AI Divide: How AI Leaders Are Pulling Ahead Across Transportation, Logistics, and Defense"

- **Firm:** Oliver Wyman Forum (with UC Berkeley)
- **Published:** July 14, 2026
- **Source:** https://www.oliverwyman.com (Oliver Wyman Forum research, industrial/TLD sector)

**Summary:** Drawing on interviews with 50+ senior executives across transportation, logistics, and defense, this report finds only 8% of CEOs in these sectors report using AI at scale — yet those early movers are already compounding advantage through AI-enabled, interconnected operations that improve safety, performance, and cost simultaneously. The report's central diagnosis is organizational, not technical: most companies in these traditionally slow-moving, capital-intensive industries still treat AI as an IT initiative rather than a C-suite/board-level enterprise priority, and that misclassification is what's widening the gap between the 8% and everyone else.

**What it means for The Construct:** Swap "transportation, logistics, defense" for "signage, homebuilding, monument/wayfinding" and the shape of the argument holds almost exactly — these are the same kind of capital-intensive, relationship-driven, technologically conservative industries where an 8%-scale AI adopter would look like a completely different kind of competitor to everyone else in the trade area. This is evidence, not just intuition, for the Brand 9 thesis that AI-native operations in a sleepy vertical is a wedge, not a nice-to-have.

**Score: 4/5** — Sharpens a thesis we hold.

## 3. arXiv — "Agent-to-Agent Finance: Blockchain Payments and Trust Infrastructure for Autonomous AI Agents"

- **Author:** Hui Gong, UCL Institute of Finance & Technology
- **Published:** July 2026 (arXiv:2607.00245)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00245

**Summary:** The paper argues autonomous AI agents are moving from being analytical tools to being transacting economic counterparties, and that this shift creates a genuine infrastructure gap: identity, authorization, payment, verification, reputation, and accountability all need to exist in machine-native form once agents are discovering counterparties and executing payments on their own. It develops "agent-to-agent finance" as a formal layer of machine-mediated financial interaction, and argues blockchain isn't a universal substrate for this but that programmable settlement, smart wallets, decentralized registries (e.g., ERC-8004-style agent registries), and verifiable computation solve specific coordination frictions unique to autonomous agents. The decisive design question the paper poses: how to grant agents bounded autonomy to transact without making markets more opaque, fragile, or unaccountable.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is early, academic, and not investable today — but it's a preview of the plumbing question Hartley Capital will eventually have to answer once agent-run funds or agent-operated portfolio companies start needing to transact with each other's agents directly rather than through humans. Useful background for when "agent ops at scale" stops meaning "agents inside one org" and starts meaning "agents across counterparties."

**Score: 3/5** — Useful background.
