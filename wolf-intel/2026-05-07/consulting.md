## Paper 1 of 3

**Title:** Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets
**Source:** arXiv · cs.AI · arXiv ID 2604.02460
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460
**Date:** Submitted April 2026; surfaced in May 2026 arXiv cs.AI current listings
**Score: 5 / 5 — Changes a Thesis**

### Executive Summary

This paper delivers a direct challenge to the multi-agent orthodoxy that has dominated enterprise AI architecture discussions since 2024. The authors demonstrate that when total compute is controlled — specifically the "thinking token budget" allocated across the entire system — single-agent LLMs consistently match or outperform multi-agent pipelines on complex multi-hop reasoning tasks. Prior benchmarks showing multi-agent superiority were, in large part, methodological artifacts: the multi-agent system was simply getting more total compute than the single-agent baseline, creating an apples-to-oranges comparison. Once budgets are equalized, the decomposition overhead of routing tasks through multiple specialized agents erodes any reasoning advantage. The finding holds across multiple model families and task types. The authors caution that multi-agent architectures remain valuable for parallelism, role specialization, and fault isolation — but not as a reasoning amplifier per se.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital's multi-agent ops build, this is a direct thesis stress-test: if the architecture is being justified partly on reasoning quality grounds, that justification is now contested. The investment case should pivot toward parallelism, observability, and role isolation as the genuine value drivers — and those are harder to measure and sell. For Brand 9, any agentic signage-workflow pitch that leads with "multi-agent intelligence" needs a sharper proof point than benchmark scores.

## Paper 2 of 3

**Title:** Agents for Growth: Turning AI Promise into Impact
**Source:** McKinsey & Company — Growth, Marketing & Sales practice
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/agents-for-growth-turning-ai-promise-into-impact
**Date:** May 2026 (confirmed in May 6 McKinsey publication cluster)
**Score: 4 / 5 — Sharpens a Thesis**

### Executive Summary

McKinsey's growth-and-sales practice has been refining its agentic AI field work since the 2025 "State of AI" and this piece represents their latest synthesis from enterprise deployments. The central argument: value from AI agents is not a function of how many agents are deployed or how sophisticated their architectures are — it comes from precisely mapping the decisions and handoffs in a customer or operational journey, then embedding agents at those exact leverage points. Companies generating compounding returns from agents have built cross-functional human-AI teams with shared data products and governance structures that explicitly treat agents as "managed talent" — with accountability, performance metrics, and career-path-equivalent frameworks. The report surfaces a persistent pattern: organizations that deploy agents broadly without this decisional mapping get diffuse productivity gains that don't show up on the P&L; those that map first and instrument second get step-change outcomes. End-to-end transformation requires simultaneously redesigning workflows, not just automating them. The data point: AI high performers are more than 3x as likely as peers to be scaling agents across a given business function.

### What It Means for The Construct

Brand 9's pitch to homebuilders and commercial developers on intelligent wayfinding and signage has a new vocabulary from this piece: "decisional mapping" of the physical customer journey (arrival, navigation, conversion, egress) is exactly what monument signage and experiential wayfinding systems do in the built environment — the analogy is tight enough to use in a deck. For Hartley Capital's PE roll-up playbook, the "managed talent" agent framework gives a credible diligence lens: are target companies' AI programs instrumented for accountability, or is this diffuse spend with no P&L line?

## Paper 3 of 3

**Title:** The AI Value Gap (Profitless Prosperity in AI)
**Source:** Roland Berger
**URL:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html
**Date:** 2026 (high circulation; surfacing in May 2026 searches)
**Score: 3 / 5 — Useful Background**

### Executive Summary

Roland Berger surveyed enterprise AI programs across industries and found that approximately 90% of firms report AI investment returns are lagging spending — "profitless prosperity." Three structural failure modes account for nearly all of the gap: (1) deploying AI on top of broken or poorly-documented workflows rather than redesigning them first; (2) treating change management as a post-launch activity rather than a co-design input; (3) failing to instrument AI output for measurement, so there is no feedback loop and no clear attribution of value. The paper argues that the window for genuine competitive differentiation through AI is closing rapidly as implementation best practices commoditize. First movers who close the value gap by fixing all three structural failures will lock in durable cost and capability advantages that laggards will find structurally difficult to replicate.

### What It Means for The Construct

Validates Hartley Capital's core PE thesis: the alpha is not in deploying AI, it's in AI-native workflow design at the portfolio-company level, which is operationally intensive and not easily copied. The "broken workflow" diagnosis is also a due-diligence red flag — any acquisition target claiming AI productivity gains without workflow redesign evidence deserves deep skepticism on the AI line items.
