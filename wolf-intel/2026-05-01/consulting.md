## PAPER 1 — SCORE: 5

**Title:** Global Asset Management Report 2026: Rebuilding Asset Management for an AI-First World
**Firm:** BCG (Boston Consulting Group)
**Published:** April 28, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/rebuilding-asset-management-for-an-ai-first-world

### Executive Summary

BCG's 2026 Global Asset Management Report exposes a structural paradox that has defined the industry for 15 years: despite global AuM reaching $147 trillion in 2025 — nearly triple the 2010 base — profit margins have held flat at ~30%, as cost growth has precisely offset revenue gains. Every dollar of scale has been consumed by operational drag. The report's central claim is that AI represents the first mechanism capable of actually breaking this dynamic. Firms that redesign their operating models around AI-native economics can reduce costs 25–35% over three to five years while multiplying research coverage 2–5x and client relationship manager capacity 3–5x. The urgency is structural: capital is concentrating rapidly — the top 10 US passive fund providers captured >90% of net inflows over the past decade, and private markets capital is gravitating to fewer, larger platforms. Firms still in the "pilot" stage face a shrinking window before AI-native competitors lock in structurally lower unit economics. Tokenized real-world assets add a secondary pressure, projected to reach $14 trillion by 2030, reshaping distribution and ownership models. Most firms remain in early adoption phases, running pilots rather than operating model redesigns — which is precisely why the window exists.

### What It Means for The Construct

Hartley Capital's PE roll-up thesis is operating inside the exact consolidation dynamic BCG documents — concentrated capital flow, margin pressure on mid-tier operators, and AI as the differentiating operating layer rather than a feature. The 25–35% cost-reduction window is now a documentable underwriting assumption, not an aspiration: deals targeting laggard operators who haven't committed to an AI operating model are better acquisition candidates, not worse — buy the inefficiency, wire it for AI economics, exit into the consolidation wave.

## PAPER 2 — SCORE: 4

**Title:** The Corporate Strategy Function in an AI-First World
**Firm:** BCG (Boston Consulting Group)
**Published:** Q1 2026
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-corporate-strategy-function-in-an-ai-first-world

### Executive Summary

BCG argues that the strategy function is on the verge of fundamental reinvention as AI agents cross from data analysis into autonomous strategic signal detection. Rather than waiting for humans to query systems, agents now actively investigate what incoming data means for strategic priorities — pulling from internal knowledge stores, flagging emerging threats and opportunities, and triggering follow-up analyses without human initiation. The practical consequence is a bifurcation in corporate and PE strategy: smaller operational refinements (price adjustments, inventory realignment, production scheduling) can be automated entirely, while major strategic moves — acquisitions, vertical exits, partnership commitments — can be executed faster and with greater conviction as accumulating agent-gathered evidence validates the thesis before any human analysis begins. Nearly 75% of CEOs now report being their organization's primary AI decision-maker, double the prior year share. Corporate AI spend is on track to double in 2026, from 0.8% to ~1.7% of revenues. The implication: strategy teams that still operate on quarterly review cadences are not just inefficient — they're structurally blind relative to competitors running continuous agent-driven signal loops.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital's deal origination and portfolio monitoring, the inflection point is here: firms deploying autonomous signal-monitoring agents will identify distressed operators and roll-up targets faster than those running quarterly reviews. The structural edge is not the AI capability — it is the workflow redesign that puts agents on continuous watch and humans on validation. This is a process architecture advantage, not a technology advantage.

## PAPER 3 — SCORE: 4

**Title:** State of AI Trust in 2026: Shifting to the Agentic Era
**Firm:** McKinsey & Company
**Published:** April–May 2026
**Source:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/tech-forward/state-of-ai-trust-in-2026-shifting-to-the-agentic-era

### Executive Summary

McKinsey's companion to the State of Organizations 2026 tracks enterprise AI trust as agentic systems move from experiment to early deployment. 62% of organizations are at least experimenting with agents; 23% are actively scaling them somewhere. The sharpest quantitative finding: high performers are 2.8x more likely (65% vs. 23%) to have formally defined "human in the loop" validation processes. The report frames trust architecture — auditability, defined intervention points, explainable escalation paths — as the primary bottleneck in agentic adoption, outweighing capability gaps and cost concerns. Organizations treating trust as a governance design problem (not a UX feature) are scaling agent systems faster and with fewer rollbacks. The agentic organizations moving fastest are not those with the most capable models; they are those with the clearest intervention protocols. The broader State of Organizations findings show that among the 23% scaling agents, the technology industry leads, followed by insurance for marketing/sales agent use. Only 25% of leaders expect agents to act as autonomous teammates in the near term — but that minority is disproportionately concentrated among the high performers driving outsized returns.

### What It Means for The Construct

For agent AI ops across Hartley Capital's deal stack and Brand 9's workflow automation: the moat is not raw agent capability but governance architecture — auditability and defined human checkpoints. Building trust infrastructure now (before it's mandated by regulators or demanded by LPs) is the durable competitive advantage, not the agent capability itself.

### arXiv — "When Agents Shop for You: Role Coherence in AI-Mediated Markets"
**Authors:** Soogand Alavi, Salar Nozari
**Submitted:** April 30, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/list/cs.MA/recent

Finds that in multi-agent market environments — where AI agents negotiate, recommend, or procure on behalf of human principals — role coherence (how consistently an agent maintains its assigned identity, constraints, and principal alignment across extended task sequences) is the primary determinant of trust and outcome quality. Incoherent agents introduce systematic bias and drift in multi-stakeholder settings, degrading outcomes not by making errors but by gradually misrepresenting whose interests they are serving. Directly relevant to the architecture design of Hartley Capital's emerging multi-agent PE ops stack: role boundaries and principal alignment are not a configuration detail — they are the structural safety layer.
