**Title:** Your AI Budget Is Growing. Your Returns Aren't. Here's Why.
**Firm:** Bain & Company
**Published:** June 1, 2026
**URL:** https://www.bain.com/insights/your-ai-budget-is-growing-your-returns-arent-heres-why/
**Secondary coverage:** Bloomberg, June 1, 2026; CFO.com; GuruFocus; Yahoo Finance

### Executive Summary

Bain surveyed 951 global companies and documented a compounding paradox: AI investment is accelerating while measured returns disappoint. 40% of firms that targeted cost reductions of 11–20% landed in the 0–10% bucket; only 4% of all respondents achieved AI-related savings above 30%. The most structurally important finding is the response pattern: 90% of companies that missed their cost-reduction targets are now raising AI budgets *again* — this time specifically for AI agents that will "operate with greater autonomy, complexity, and consequence." The diagnosis is a gap between business-case assumptions (full automation economics) and deployed reality (supervised automation), meaning CFOs approved one set of numbers while organizations live with another. Data access and integration is the single largest cited barrier (41% of respondents), ranking above compliance, talent gaps, and executive buy-in. On the finance function specifically: 56% of CFOs are increasing enterprise-wide AI investment by more than 15% this year; 83% plan increases above 15% over two years; 42% expect increases above 30%.

### What It Means for The Construct

Hartley Capital's agent AI roll-up thesis is directionally correct but needs a credibility firewall: any PE due diligence process for an AI-native acquisition must now include an independent "automation actuals" audit against what the original business case promised. The 90%-doubling-down figure is the real opportunity signal — firms that cannot generate ROI internally will pay a premium to acquire operational AI capability through roll-up platforms that already have clean data architectures and measurement infrastructure in place.

**Score: 4** — *Sharpens a thesis.* Thesis sharpened: agent AI creates durable competitive moats in operational businesses; incumbents will structurally fail to build those moats internally, generating a repeating M&A window for data-infrastructure-first roll-up platforms.

**Secondary coverage:** IntelligentCIO (multiple regions), PRNewswire, AI Journal, IntelligentCXO

### Executive Summary

Deloitte surveyed 660+ technology leaders globally (Dec 2025–Feb 2026) and documented three tectonic shifts redefining the tech leadership mandate. (1) The Value Mandate: technology is now expected to deliver measurable enterprise value — growth, productivity, customer impact — not merely uptime; 79% cite this as their top priority, yet 42% report low or no ROI on AI investments. (2) The Resource Squeeze: more mandate, same budgets, with AI scaling blocked by data quality, security concerns, talent shortages, and legacy systems — the gap between ambition and execution is structural, not incidental. (3) The Orchestration Shift: with 71% of organizations now housing five or more technology leaders, the effective model has evolved from control to coordination; success depends less on technical oversight and more on human-AI collaboration, trust architecture, and translating ambition into action. The study frames the emerging tech leader archetype as "orchestrator of human-AI systems" rather than manager of infrastructure.

### What It Means for The Construct

The "operators to orchestrators" frame is the organizing logic Hartley Capital needs for its multi-agent hedge fund architecture: the competitive advantage is not the agents themselves but the governance and orchestration layer sitting above them — a different org chart, a different hiring profile, and a different KPI regime than anything incumbents are currently measuring or building.

**Score: 4** — *Sharpens a thesis.* Thesis sharpened: in agentic finance, the moat is orchestration infrastructure, not model capability. Most firms are still structured as operators and will remain so.

### Executive Summary

BCG's 26th annual Global Wealth Report frames 2026 as the inflection point of the largest wealth transfer in recorded history. Surveying 333 family offices (average net worth $1.6B): two-thirds cite geopolitics as their primary risk; succession planning and family wealth education are identified as the largest operational gap. A structural contradiction surfaces: two-thirds of family offices intend to prioritize AI investments, yet more than half have zero exposure to growth equity, venture capital, or infrastructure — alternative asset classes that are precisely where AI is generating early measurable returns. Hong Kong has overtaken Switzerland as the world's leading cross-border wealth booking center; PE and direct co-investment are growing as liquidity pressures push family offices from passive allocation toward hands-on investing. The report describes a "returns decomposition" shift in PE: alpha can now be attributed to growth, margin, leverage, or multiple — enabling more selective manager choice.
