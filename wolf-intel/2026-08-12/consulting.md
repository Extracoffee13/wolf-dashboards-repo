## 1. McKinsey — "How to close the agentic adoption gap"

**Firm:** McKinsey & Company
**Source URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/how-to-close-the-agentic-adoption-gap
**Date:** August 7, 2026 (confirmed)
**Score: 5 — sharpens a thesis we hold, arguably changes it**

**Executive summary:** McKinsey's People & Organizational Performance practice argues that agentic AI transformation is fundamentally a change-leadership problem, not a technology-procurement problem — the gap between agent capability and agent value-capture is an adoption gap, not a model gap. The piece introduces a "1:3:5" spend ratio as the pattern behind the deployments that actually stick: for every $1 spent on the agent technology itself, successful organizations spend roughly $3 on redesigning the surrounding processes the agent touches, and $5 on capability-building and change management to get humans to actually use and trust the new workflow. Organizations that spend disproportionately on the tech layer and treat rollout as an IT event consistently underperform ones that treat it as an organizational-redesign program.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the strongest external validation yet that PRAGMA's actual moat isn't the agent stack — it's the surrounding adoption and process-redesign layer, which is exactly where we've been under-selling ourselves relative to competitors who lead with model capability; we should be pricing and marketing the 3:5 (process + change management), not the 1 (tech).

## 2. Oliver Wyman — "The private equity agenda in the age of AI: Core strategies"

**Firm:** Oliver Wyman (with NYSE)
**Source URL:** https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2026/jul/how-private-equity-ceo-outpace-public-companies-with-ai.html
**Date:** July 2026 (exact day unconfirmed — flagged as stale relative to strict window)
**Score: 4 — sharpens a thesis we hold**

**Executive summary:** A joint Oliver Wyman/NYSE survey of 260 public companies and 51 PE-owned companies finds that PE-backed CEOs are notably more anxious about AI-driven competitive displacement than their public-company peers: 49% cite falling behind on AI deployment as a top-three threat to their business, and PE-backed firms report prioritizing AI/tech deployment for shareholder-value creation at a materially higher rate than public companies (49% vs. 35%). The gap is attributed to PE ownership's shorter hold periods and direct board-level pressure to show operating-model transformation before exit, versus public companies' more diffuse governance and longer AI-payback tolerance.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the wedge for Hartley Capital's portfolio-company play — PE sponsors are actively hunting for AI/agent deployment wins on a hold-period clock, which means the sales motion isn't "convince them AI matters," it's "be the fastest credible agent-ops vendor a sponsor's operating partner can plug into a portco before the next board meeting."

## 3. Roland Berger — "Family offices adjust asset allocation to meet evolving challenges"

**Firm:** Roland Berger
**Source URL:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html
**Date:** Published July 2026; survey fielded Dec 2025–Mar 2026 (exact publish day unconfirmed — flagged as stale relative to strict window)
**Score: 3 — useful background**

**Executive summary:** A survey of 88 DACH-region family offices finds private equity (via both fund commitments and direct deals) remains the primary vehicle for growth allocation, running alongside a defensive tilt toward precious metals. Infrastructure ranks as the top sector priority, followed by healthcare and AI, while venture capital and crypto allocations are being actively scaled back. Geopolitical risk is now cited as a significant or very significant challenge by 88% of respondents, up sharply from 65% the prior year — the single largest year-over-year shift in the survey.

**What it means for The Construct: ** Family offices are simultaneously de-risking (out of VC/crypto, into infrastructure and defensive assets) and doubling down on direct PE deals — that combination is a good match for Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis, but the 88% geopolitical-risk spike is a signal to watch for slower deal cadence and longer diligence cycles in the next two quarters.
