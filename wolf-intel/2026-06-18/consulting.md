## PAPER 1 — Score: 5/5

**Title:** How AI Agents Reshape Knowledge Work: Autonomy, Efficiency, and Scope  
**Source:** arxiv cs.AI — arXiv:2606.07489v2  
**Authors:** Jeremy Yang (Harvard Business School) & Jerry Ma (Perplexity AI)  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07489

### Executive Summary

This is the first large-scale empirical study measuring what autonomous AI agents actually do to knowledge work — not in a lab, but from production usage data across hundreds of thousands of Perplexity users. The study compares Perplexity Search (conversational AI) against Perplexity Computer (autonomous agent) on matched tasks and finds three compounding effects: **Autonomy** — the agent runs 26 minutes of uninterrupted, self-directed work per session (vs. 33 seconds for Search), decomposing tasks and executing sub-steps with no human handoff. **Efficiency** — agents reduce task completion time from 269 minutes to 36 minutes on matched work, a 87% reduction in time and 94% reduction in estimated cost vs. search-augmented humans. **Scope** — and this is the critical finding — agents don't just accelerate the work users were already attempting. They expand the category of work attempted: Computer queries cross occupational boundaries more often, require higher-order cognition, draw on broader expertise, and bundle interdependent sub-tasks that would never be initiated with a search tool. Dissatisfaction rates are 55% lower on Computer. The implication is not incremental efficiency; it's a step-change in what a single operator can attempt and complete.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital, this paper provides the empirical floor under any agent AI thesis: the productivity data is now on the record, from production, at scale. More critically, the "scope expansion" finding means AI-first firms aren't just doing the same work faster — they're competing in categories their non-agent peers literally cannot access, which reframes the PE roll-up question from "does AI cut costs?" to "does the target have the operating model to unlock agent scope, and if not, can we install one?"

### Executive Summary

Bain's midyear PE checkup delivers a sobering diagnosis: the recovery that was building momentum in Q4 2025 was derailed by a "triple shock" arriving in rapid succession. First, the AI-driven SaaSpocalypse — software valuations in PE portfolios fell ~8% overall while public markets corrected far harder, and tech deal value slumped 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 as large software transactions evaporated. Second, redemption stress in private credit, constraining financing availability. Third, the Iran war and attendant oil price spike, injecting macro uncertainty at exactly the wrong moment. Bid-ask spreads widened. Investment committees pulled back. Exit momentum that was recovering stalled again. The macro backdrop is not catastrophic — global expansion continues, debt markets remain open, dry powder is abundant — but deal activity is projected "flat through July 2026, stable but far from broad-based recovery." The structural take: a deal that required 5% annual EBITDA growth to return 2.5x a decade ago now requires 10–12% to hit the same hurdle. Winning firms are being told to lean into value creation plans, AI adoption in portfolio companies, and selective bets where they have a genuine "right to win."

### What It Means for The Construct

Hartley Capital's PE roll-up playbook lands in the exact window Bain describes: elevated EBITDA requirements create a buyer's discipline lens — only roll-ups with demonstrable AI-driven value creation pathways (cost reduction + scope expansion) clear the new return bar. The flip side: the triple shock has created attractive entry pricing in non-correlated sectors (signage, trade services, branded services) that weren't in the SaaSpocalypse blast radius.

### Executive Summary

BCG's report quantifies what "AI-first" means for real estate developers, investors, and property managers — and the numbers are sharp. AI-first development companies (DevCos) can achieve 400–700 basis points of margin uplift from AI applications including construction site assistants and procurement optimization, while compressing project timelines by ~30% and accelerating capital deployment cycles. The industry currently lags the global AI maturity average by ~15%, and AI real estate investment is forecast 0.8% below the 2026 cross-industry average — a gap Bain expects to widen rather than close quickly. Operationally, real estate is structurally exposed: 66% of projects experience schedule overruns, 39% face cost overruns, driven by fragmentation and rework. AI addresses these at the workflow level — procurement, site management, compliance, and investor reporting — rather than requiring a top-down operating model overhaul. BCG frames the strategic window as narrow: the firms that move in the next 12–18 months gain durable structural cost advantage; those that wait face a compounding gap.

If AI-first DevCos compress timelines by 30%, their signage partner must match that cycle; a digitally-native, fast-turnaround signage operator who integrates into AI-first DevCo workflows isn't a vendor — they're a structural component.
