**Score: 4 — Sharpens a thesis**

**Executive Summary:**
Based on Bain's Automation and AI Pathfinder Survey 2026 (951 global companies), this report is the clearest data-driven indictment of how corporate AI investment is being mismanaged at scale. The core finding: while 37% of companies targeted cost reductions of 11–20% from AI, nearly 40% of those measuring outcomes landed in the 0–10% bucket instead — a systematic miss. The differentiating factor for the high-performer cohort: they treated data access and process redesign as CEO-level problems rather than IT problems. Importantly, high performers actually cite data as a *bigger* barrier (44% vs 40%) — not because they solved it, but because they ran into it harder by deploying at scale.

### 3. Inside the AI-First Private Equity Firm

**Published:** 2026

**Score: 4 — Sharpens a thesis**

**Executive Summary:**
BCG's diagnostic of AI adoption across the private equity sector finds the industry largely stuck in the "Deploy" phase — distributing AI tool licenses to portfolio companies and employees without changing how the organization operates, which improves task-level efficiency but does not structurally reinvent process design, governance, or execution. The core diagnosis: most PE-backed companies have deployed AI tools across the portfolio, but few have done the hard work of transforming their operating model, and even fewer have done the harder work of becoming AI-first — putting AI and agents at the center of decision-making. The honest assessment: the actual impact of AI on the PE sector in 2025 was modest, with few firms able to show meaningful returns from AI in many of their portfolio companies. BCG's three-stage model (Deploy → Reshape → Invent) frames where value actually lives: Reshape requires writing new playbooks that all or nearly all portfolio companies can adopt, with leaders needing to reimagine how work gets done job function by job function. Agentic AI is positioned as the enabling architecture for the Reshape phase — not a feature of tool deployment, but the mechanism by which portfolio-wide process transformation becomes executable at scale.

### 4. The AI Value Gap (Profitless Prosperity in AI)
**Firm:** Roland Berger
**Published:** 2026
**Source:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html
**Score: 3 — Useful background**

**Executive Summary:**
Roland Berger surveyed 203 senior executives across Europe, Japan, and the US and found that nearly 90% of firms report financial returns from AI lagging their investment levels. Only approximately 10% — categorized as "Industrializers" — consistently capture meaningful impact. The rest are split between "Stalled" (matching ambition and spend, but not returns) and "Observers" (piloting without scaling). The root cause of the gap is organizational architecture, not technological access: organizational silos block the shared capability platforms that agentic systems demand, change management is chronically underestimated, and management culture is biased toward continuous improvement rather than reinvention. The Industrializer profile treats AI as an industrial capability to be engineered systematically, not a series of pilots to launch. A companion Roland Berger publication ("From Digital Twin to Agentic Twin," April 2026) extends this framework to argue that organizations that fail to stress-test their operating models against a "zero-legacy benchmark" will be caught off guard by competitors who were never burdened by legacy infrastructure.

**What It Means for The Construct:**
The "10% Industrializers" framing is a useful diagnostic for Hartley portfolio company assessments — and for Brand 9 enterprise clients who claim to be "doing AI."

### 5. Human Capital, AI, and Labor Commoditization
**Authors:** Auyon Siddiq, Niuniu Zhang (UCLA Anderson)
**Published:** June 20, 2026
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21880
**Score: 3 — Useful background**

**Executive Summary:**
Using high-dimensional text embeddings applied to a panel of 49,610 workers completing 2.26 million contracts on Upwork (January 2021 – March 2026), the authors measure how AI exposure affects the relative importance of human capital versus price in predicting labor demand. The key finding: in more AI-exposed job categories, the importance of human capital *declines* and the importance of price *rises*, consistent with a commoditization effect of AI on labor. Demand premiums for workers with strong human capital shrink in AI-exposed categories, and demand reallocates toward lower-priced workers. The study spans the November 2022 ChatGPT release and tracks the subsequent structural shift in how buyers evaluate freelancers. Implications: workers in AI-exposed roles have weakened incentives to invest in human capital, since the premium has compressed; and platform design faces new challenges as price becomes the dominant selection variable in affected categories.

**What It Means for The Construct:**
The labor commoditization dynamic is relevant to Hartley Capital's staffing thesis in agent-ops: if the goal is to build lean, AI-augmented operating teams, the underlying labor market data now supports a "hire fewer but better" strategy over time, as AI commoditizes the mid-tier knowledge worker layer.
