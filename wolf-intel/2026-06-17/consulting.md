## Paper 1 — Score: 5

**Title:** Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest: Private Equity Midyear Report 2026  
**Firm:** Bain & Company  
**Published:** June 8, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/

### Executive Summary

Bain's PE Midyear 2026 is a hard reset on the recovery narrative that had been building since late 2025. Three rapid-fire shocks stalled the market: an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" that repriced software multiples, redemption stress in private credit (Blue Owl's February gate was the canary), and a war in Iran that spiked oil and froze risk appetite. The result: deal value dropped roughly 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026, and 33,000 unsold portfolio companies remain in limbo with stretched holding periods. Bain's central frame is "12 is the new 5" — the EBITDA growth rate required to generate a 2.5x return has risen from ~5% per year a decade ago to ~10–12% today, compressing the tolerance for multiple-expansion deals and raising the bar for operational execution. Winning firms are told to lean into value creation plans, harness AI proactively in portfolio cos, and focus on disciplined bets where they have genuine right to win.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital's PE roll-up thesis: the SaaSpocalypse makes software-heavy roll-ups cheaper to acquire but operationally harder to exit — the window for buying distressed SaaS platform businesses is open, but only if you can demonstrate genuine AI-driven EBITDA growth, not multiple-expansion storytelling. The "12 is the new 5" framework is the new underwriting standard; any Hartley roll-up that can't credibly project 10–12% EBITDA CAGR with agent AI as the lever should not be done at current prices.

## Paper 2 — Score: 4

**Title:** Agentic AI Enterprise Token Cost  
**Firm:** EY  
**Published:** June 1, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/ai/agentic-ai-token-costs

### Executive Summary

EY published a CFO/CTO-oriented framework for understanding the true cost of deploying agentic AI at enterprise scale — a topic where most organizations are still using pre-agentic mental models. The headline number: a simple customer service AI interaction that cost $0.04 in 2023 now costs $1.20 in an orchestrated agentic workflow in 2026 — a 30x increase driven by reasoning loops, tool calls, memory retrieval, and iterative orchestration. But EY's larger argument is that token cost is only a fraction of total agentic cost. The framework identifies six hidden cost buckets consistently omitted from enterprise AI budgets: governance burden (oversight, audit trails, compliance), organizational change (workflow redesign, retraining, change management), expected failure and recovery costs (hallucination remediation, rollbacks), regulatory risk provisioning ("AI taxes" for agentic decisions), orchestration infrastructure, and Agent FinOps tooling needed to monitor and control variable compute spend. EY's recommendation: treat agentic AI as a new operating cost category — variable, not fixed — and build Agent FinOps discipline before scaling.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Hartley Capital's agent AI buildout and hedge fund operations: this is the cost model you need to underwrite before deploying multi-agent systems at the fund or portfolio level. The 30x per-interaction cost jump is not a reason to stop — it is a reason to be precise about which workflows justify agentic orchestration vs. simpler automation, and to build cost governance from day one rather than discovering the bill after scale.

## Paper 3 — Score: 4

**Title:** The AI-First Real Estate Company: An Opportunity for Structural Advantage  
**Firm:** BCG  
**Published:** April/June 2026  
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-ai-first-real-estate-company-advantage

### Executive Summary

BCG's real estate AI report argues that the sector is at a structural inflection point: real estate lags the global AI adoption average by ~15%, with only 25% of firms qualifying as "AI leaders" versus 40% across other industries, and spending roughly half the cross-industry average on AI. The opportunity is large — development companies (DevCos) can achieve 400–700 basis points of margin uplift from AI applications including construction site assistants, procurement optimization, and project management automation. AI-first DevCos are compressing project timelines by approximately 30% and accelerating capital deployment cycles. The key framing is a three-stage model: "deploy" (hand out licenses — what most are doing), "reshape" (redesign workflows around AI capabilities), and "invent" (build new AI-native business models). BCG's warning: the window for first-mover advantage is narrow, and firms that stay in the "deploy" phase will be structurally disadvantaged within 3–5 years.

### What It Means for The Construct

For Brand 9 and the homebuilder/FL real estate clients it serves: the 400–700 bps margin uplift for DevCos is the financial case for selling AI-integrated signage and wayfinding solutions, not just physical products. Brand 9's pitch to homebuilders should explicitly frame signage programs as part of the AI-first site operations stack — project milestone markers, wayfinding tied to CRM/MLS data, digital monument signage as a living brand layer — not a commodity vendor relationship.
