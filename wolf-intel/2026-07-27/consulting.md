## 1. Agentic AI Turns Every Team into Its Own Transformation Engine

**Firm:** BCG (BCG Henderson Institute)
**Published:** July 27, 2026 (today)
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/agentic-ai-turns-every-team-into-its-own-transformation-engine

**Summary:** BCG argues the enterprise AI transformation model is inverting. The old pattern — a centralized transformation office running a top-down AI rollout — is being displaced by team-led workflow redesign, because agentic tooling is now cheap and accessible enough that individual teams can rebuild their own processes without waiting on a program office. BCG's deployment data shows agents can cut costs 60%+ but only when workflows are redesigned end-to-end, not bolted onto the existing process; companies are splitting into a small "agent-first" cohort and a much larger group stuck below 30% adoption. The piece's sharpest claim: success is roughly 70% people and change management, 30% algorithm — grassroots tool access is already happening whether or not leadership sanctions it, producing real productivity gains alongside real governance risk if nobody sets guardrails.

**What it means for The Construct:** This directly sharpens the Hartley Capital agent-ops thesis for portfolio-company integration — the roll-up playbook should favor embedding agent capability inside each portfolio company's existing team workflows rather than standing up a centralized "AI PMO" across the platform, and guardrails/governance need to be a day-one deliverable in every integration plan, not a phase-two cleanup item.

**Score: 5** — changes a thesis we hold (centralized vs. decentralized agent-ops rollout model for portfolio companies).

## 2. The Private Equity Agenda in the Age of AI: Core Strategies

**Firm:** Oliver Wyman (Oliver Wyman Forum, with NYSE)
**Published:** July 2026
**Source:** https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2026/jul/how-private-equity-ceo-outpace-public-companies-with-ai.html

**Summary:** Drawn from the 2026 Oliver Wyman Forum/NYSE CEO Survey (415 CEOs: 149 PE-backed, 266 public), the report quantifies a real gap between PE-backed and public-company AI urgency. 49% of PE-backed CEOs cite falling behind peers on AI deployment as a top-3 business threat, versus 22% of public CEOs — more than double. 49% of PE-backed CEOs rank AI/tech deployment as a top-3 lever for shareholder value versus 35% of public peers, and they're nearly twice as likely to name it their #1 priority (18% vs. 10%). Growth (75%) and cost/operational excellence (66%) dominate PE-backed CEO priorities, with efficiency framed explicitly as the mechanism that funds growth rather than an end in itself.

**What it means for The Construct:** Confirms and quantifies the Hartley Capital roll-up thesis that PE ownership structurally forces faster AI-agent adoption than public markets — the operating advantage is real and measurable, so portfolio companies should be benchmarked against a PE-specific adoption bar, not an industry-wide one, when evaluating integration progress.

**Score: 4** — sharpens (and quantifies) an existing thesis rather than overturning it.

## 3. Family Office Study: New Asset Allocation in Challenging Times

**Firm:** Roland Berger
**Published:** July 2026 (survey of 88 DACH-region family offices; widely covered July 21, 2026)
**Source:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html

**Summary:** Family offices are pivoting hard toward control and stability. Infrastructure is now the top allocation priority (69%), followed by healthcare, with AI allocation rising fast; 55% plan to expand private-equity fund commitments and direct investing is gaining share at the same time. Venture capital and crypto/digital assets are being scaled back by a growing share of respondents. The throughline is risk-wariness: family offices are chasing inflation-linked, cash-flow-visible, control-oriented exposure over speculative upside.

**What it means for The Construct:** Reinforces the Hartley Capital family-office thesis — rising direct-investment appetite and a stated preference for control-oriented, real-asset-adjacent exposure supports pitching Brand 9 / monument-signage and roll-up vehicles as direct co-investment opportunities rather than fund LP positions; the venture/crypto pullback is a cue to lean into "boring, cash-flowing, real-asset-adjacent" positioning in any family-office-facing materials.

**Score: 4** — sharpens an existing thesis with fresh survey data.
