## 1. Bain & Company — "The Executive's Guide to Agentic AI" (adoption data, 2026)

**Firm:** Bain & Company
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/topics/agentic-ai/

**Summary:** Bain's 2026 agentic-AI research quantifies a gap the hype cycle has been papering over: 79% of enterprises have adopted AI agents in some form, but only 11% run them in production. Bain calls this the "production-readiness gap" and names it the defining agentic-AI challenge of 2026 — not model capability, not cost, but the operational distance between a pilot and a live system with real accountability. Where agents do reach production, average ROI is 171%, but 19% of deployments never reach payback at all. The differentiator Bain identifies: companies that stand up governance frameworks, baseline metrics, and a named business owner *before* deployment reach positive ROI 2.4x faster than those that bolt governance on after the fact.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is a moat confirmation, not new information — WOLF, Hermes, and the Buzz flock are already past the 11% line while most of the market is still piloting. The strategic read is for Hartley Capital diligence: when a PE target claims "AI-enabled operations," the Bain data says ask for the production number, not the pilot count — 89% of "AI adopters" are lying to themselves about where they actually are.

**Score: 4** — sharpens an existing thesis (agent-ops maturity as a diligence filter) with a hard, citable stat.

## 2. BCG — "How CIOs Can Govern AI Agents at Scale in 2026"

**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-cios-govern-ai-agents-at-scale

**Summary:** BCG's companion piece to the production-readiness problem: centralized governance — permissioning, audit trails, kill-switches, a standing review layer — is what collapses agent deployment time from weeks (approving each new agent one at a time) to about a day, a claimed 10x gain. The article frames this as the actual bottleneck CIOs are underinvesting in relative to model/tooling spend, and ties directly into BCG's parallel "Agentic Leadership Playbook" piece on the COO becoming an "enterprise orchestrator" — i.e., the org chart, not the model card, is where 2026's agentic-AI competition is actually being fought.

**What it means for The Construct: ** Read against Bain's stat above, this is close to a second-order confirmation of what WOLF's own architecture already does (PRAXIS inbox as an audit trail, subscription-gated PR/CI oversight, structured decision blocks) — worth stress-testing whether our own governance layer would survive being audited by this framework, and worth using as a due-diligence checklist item for Hartley Capital's PE roll-up targets that pitch "AI-native ops."

**Score: 4** — same thesis as #1, different mechanism (governance-as-unlock), strong enough on its own merits to stand as a second paper rather than a duplicate.

## 3. PwC / Urban Land Institute — "Emerging Trends in Real Estate 2026" (Miami market chapter)

**Firm:** PwC (with Urban Land Institute)
**Source:** https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/asset-wealth-management/real-estate/emerging-trends-in-real-estate-pwc-uli/markets-to-watch/miami.html

**Summary:** The 2026 Emerging Trends Barometer scored 3.74 — a 20-year high for the survey — signaling real-estate sentiment has fully priced in a new-normal expansion rather than a return to pre-2022 conditions. The Miami/South Florida chapter specifically: Miami-Dade has drawn 127 relocating companies occupying 2.2M sq ft of office space since 2020, and South Florida broadly has pulled in 205 companies across 3.5M sq ft over the same window. Homebuilders quoted in the report flag labor availability and rising material costs as the real headwinds for the next two years — not demand.

**What it means for The Construct:** Background confirmation, not new signal — the corporate in-migration pipeline into South Florida that underwrites Brand 9's monument-signage and wayfinding demand is still intact and, per the barometer, still accelerating. Nothing here changes strategy; it's a green light to keep the current FL build-out thesis unchanged rather than second-guess it.

**Score: 3** — useful background, doesn't sharpen or threaten an existing thesis on its own.
