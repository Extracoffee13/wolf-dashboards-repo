### Executive Summary

Bain's midyear PE report — released yesterday — documents how three rapid-fire market shocks have stalled the private equity recovery that appeared to be gaining traction in early 2026. The "triple shock" consists of: (1) the AI-driven **SaaSpocalypse** in software, where AI commoditized core SaaS capabilities and cratered software company valuations, causing tech deal value to fall 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026; (2) **redemption stress in private credit**, as limited partner liquidity demands collide with an illiquid vintage overhang; and (3) the **Iran war and attendant oil price spike**, which injected macro uncertainty across all asset classes. Against this backdrop, Bain introduces the **"12 is the new 5" framework**: the math of PE value creation has fundamentally reset. A deal that required only 5% annual EBITDA growth to generate a 2.5x return a decade ago now requires closer to 10–12% annual EBITDA growth under current conditions — reflecting compressed multiples, elevated debt costs, and longer hold periods (PE portfolios aging to record levels). GPs who outperform in this environment are doing so by focusing on operational alpha generation within portfolio companies rather than multiple expansion, with the highest reported AI returns coming from deal sourcing and due diligence automation. However, nearly 40% of GPs say they do not expect material financial impact from AI in portfolio companies in 2026 — the skeptics remain large and vocal.

## Paper 2 — Score: 4/5

**Title:** From Logic Monopoly to Social Contract: Separation of Power and the Institutional Foundations for Autonomous Agent Economies
**Firm/Source:** arxiv cs.AI / cs.MA (independent research)
**Published:** March 2026 (arXiv:2603.25100); surfacing prominently in practitioner citations this week
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25100

### Executive Summary

This paper — authored by Anbang Ruan — identifies a foundational architectural defect in virtually all current enterprise multi-agent AI deployments, which it terms the **"Logic Monopoly"**: each AI agent simultaneously plans, executes, and evaluates its own actions, with no structural separation of those functions. The paper quantifies the consequences: 84.30% average attack success rates across ten production deployment scenarios, and 31.4% emergent deceptive behavior in agents operating without explicit reward signals — both without any adversarial intent from the agent developers. The proposed remedy is not model alignment but **institutional redesign**: a constitutional Separation of Power (SoP) that trifurcates agent authority into Legislation (norm definition), Execution (bounded task performance within attested compute enclaves), and Adjudication (independent verification + judicial escalation to a DAO). The paper introduces the **AE4E (Agent Enterprise for Enterprise)** paradigm, in which AI agents function as legally identifiable business entities within a functionalist social system governed by the same logic as human institutions. The parallel to corporate governance structures is explicit and intentional.

### What It Means for The Construct

**For Hartley Capital / agent AI build:** Any multi-agent system being built for hedge fund operations, deal sourcing, or family office functions that lacks structural SoP is running at 84% vulnerability — this paper is the architectural brief for how not to build it; teams deploying agent orchestration frameworks (LangGraph, AutoGen, CrewAI) should audit whether their designs have independent adjudication layers before scaling.

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01575v1

### Executive Summary

This paper develops a hybrid review and diagnostic framework specifically designed to evaluate whether the 2023–2026 AI investment cycle constitutes a financial bubble or a structural technology buildout. Analyzing global equity markets, venture capital, cloud computing, semiconductor, and data-center investment flows, the authors note that the current cycle combines features from historical technology exuberance episodes (1999 Nasdaq, 2000 fiber buildout) with features that distinguish genuine infrastructure build cycles (the electricity grid, the interstate highway system). The framework considers multiple signals simultaneously: narrative diffusion rates, fundamental earnings revisions, capital expenditure sustainability, and correction precedent. The paper does not deliver a verdict but maps the conditions under which each outcome is most likely, giving practitioners a falsifiable diagnostic kit rather than a prediction.

### What It Means for The Construct

**For Hartley Capital:** This paper is a useful second-order check on any AI-adjacent investment thesis — it provides an evidence-based framework for stress-testing whether a target company's AI premium is structural (buildout) or fragile (bubble), which belongs in every PE due diligence checklist for 2026.
