## 1. Oliver Wyman — "The Private Equity Agenda in the Age of AI: Core Strategies"

**Firm:** Oliver Wyman (Oliver Wyman Forum × NYSE CEO Survey 2026)
**Published:** July 2026
**Source:** https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2026/jul/how-private-equity-ceo-outpace-public-companies-with-ai.html

**Summary:** Cuts the third annual Oliver Wyman Forum/NYSE CEO Agenda survey (415 CEOs, ~10% of global market cap) specifically for the PE-backed subset. PE-backed CEOs prioritize growth harder than public peers (75% vs 70% name a growth lever as top priority; revenue uplift cited by 59%), and treat operational excellence as the funding mechanism for that growth rather than an end in itself (two-thirds of PE respondents rank cost/ops excellence top-3 vs 55% of public peers). The AI finding is the sharp one: 49% of PE-backed CEOs name "falling behind peers on AI deployment" as a top-3 business threat — more than double the 22% of public-company CEOs — and 49% rank deploying AI as a top-3 lever for shareholder value vs 35% of public peers. PE boards are also pulling AI oversight onto their own agenda faster (29% vs 22% of public boards) alongside heavier board involvement in capital allocation and operating performance.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the clearest outside confirmation yet that PE-backed operators see AI-deployment speed as existential in a way public-company boards don't — which is exactly the wedge Hartley Capital is building into: roll-ups where agent AI is the operating-model lever, not a bolt-on pilot. The board-level data point (AI oversight climbing the PE board agenda faster than public boards) is the strongest justification we have for pitching agent-ops maturity as a diligence criterion, not a nice-to-have, in any roll-up thesis.

**Score: 5** — directly sharpens the Hartley Capital thesis.

## 2. Bain & Company — Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest"

**Firm:** Bain & Company
**Published:** June 8, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/ (press release: https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/winning-firms-will-focus-on-what-they-can-control-weather-the-rest-as-triple-shock-brakes-private-equitys-latest-revival-bain--company-2026-midyear-pe-report/)

**Summary:** Bain's midyear check-in on its own "12 is the new 5" 2026 PE thesis (deals now need ~10-12% annual EBITDA growth vs ~5% a decade ago to clear return hurdles) documents a "triple shock" that stalled H1 2026's recovery attempt: an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" that compressed software multiples, redemption stress spreading through private credit, and an oil-price spike tied to the Iran conflict. Bid-ask spreads widened, investment committees pulled back, and exit momentum that had been building reversed. Bain frames this as a repeating "Groundhog Day" pattern — the industry keeps positioning for a broad recovery only to have an external shock reset the clock before real momentum takes hold. The report still holds the line that winning firms invest in talent, AI, and data-backed value creation to hit the higher EBITDA bar, but the "AI-driven SaaSpocalypse" phrase is doing real work here: the same technology PE firms are told to bet on is also the proximate cause of one of the three shocks that just braked the market.

**What it means for The Construct:** The tension is the finding — AI is simultaneously the thing PE-backed CEOs fear falling behind on (see #1) and the thing that just compressed software-sector multiples hard enough to help stall a market-wide recovery. Any roll-up thesis levered to AI-augmented SaaS-adjacent assets (agent-ops tooling, vertical software bolt-ons) should be underwriting for a second leg of multiple compression, not assuming the SaaSpocalypse is a one-time reset already priced in.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the roll-up risk thesis with a concrete, named shock mechanism.

## 3. McKinsey — "How Agentic AI Can Reshape Real Estate's Operating Model"

**Firm:** McKinsey & Company
**Published:** March 2026
**Source:** https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/real-estate/our-insights/how-agentic-ai-can-reshape-real-estates-operating-model

**Summary:** McKinsey sizes a $430B–$550B annual labor-productivity opportunity across real estate, construction, and development from applying agentic AI to knowledge work — resident-request ticketing, prioritization, dispatch, and status updates handled autonomously, with documented pilots cutting workflow times more than 30%. The core argument: value goes to firms that redesign entire operational domains around autonomous agents embedded in core systems, not firms running isolated AI pilots alongside unchanged workflows. It's a companion piece to McKinsey's broader "agentic organization" work, which frames the shift as "help me understand" giving way to "help me get it done" as the operating default.

**What it means for The Construct: ** Directly validates the Brand 9 thesis that agent-ops maturity — not point-solution AI — is the differentiator in property-adjacent trades (signage, wayfinding, monument work sit inside exactly the workflow layer McKinsey is describing: ticketing, dispatch, status). The 30%+ workflow-time cut from real pilots is a number worth benchmarking Brand 9's own dispatch/fulfillment agents against.

**Score: 4** — useful validating benchmark data for the signage/real-estate engine's agent-ops build-out.
