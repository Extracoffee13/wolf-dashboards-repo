**Executive Summary**

Bain's flagship midyear PE report lands with a blunt verdict: the recovery that was gathering real momentum entering 2026 has been killed again by three rapid-fire shocks. First, the "SaaSpocalypse" — AI's disruption of legacy SaaS business models triggered a software valuation reset that slashed tech deal value by 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 (private software portfolios fell ~8%, but public SaaS fell far more, widening the bid-ask gap and freezing sponsor exits). Second, redemption stress cracked through private credit markets. Third, the Iran conflict spiked oil prices and froze risk appetites globally. Investment committees pulled back, exit momentum stalled again, and bid-ask spreads widened across the board.

The structural read, though, is not bearish: public equities hold, debt markets are open, and there is abundant dry powder. The real shift is cost: the deal cost index (purchase multiples + financing costs combined) now sits at the highest point in PE history. That forces a new performance standard — Bain labels it **"12 is the new 5"**: today's deals require EBITDA growth at a 12% CAGR to generate the same returns that a 5% grower once delivered. That is not a refinement; it is a fundamental re-rating of what constitutes a winnable deal.

The report's prescription for winning firms: lean hard into proactive value creation, build "right to win" theses rather than market-riding returns, and treat AI adoption inside portfolio companies not as a nice-to-have pilot program but as the primary lever for compressing the time to EBITDA growth. Firms that move on AI transformation now — inside their portfolios — will separate from those waiting for market conditions to normalize.

The evidence is hard: 66% of projects experience schedule overruns, 39% face cost overruns — driven by fragmentation, poor data flow, and rework loops that AI can directly interrupt. The firms that move now — while the industry is still at low AI maturity — capture a durable structural advantage; those that wait are looking at commoditization.

### 3. The AI Value Gap: Profitless Prosperity in AI
**Firm:** Roland Berger  
**Published:** March 2026 (prominent circulation June 2026)  
**URL:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html  
**Score: 4 / 5** — sharpens a thesis we hold

**Executive Summary**

Roland Berger's AI Lab surveyed 203 senior executives across industries and geographies to measure the gap between AI investment and AI return. The headline is stark: **~90% of firms report AI returns lagging spending**. Only approximately **10% of firms — labeled "Industrializers" — consistently capture meaningful impact**. The gap is not a technology problem; it is structural. Four root causes dominate: (1) legacy IT architectures that balkanize data and prevent AI from accessing the full information set it needs; (2) organizational silos that block the shared capability platforms required to run AI at scale; (3) chronic underestimation of change management — deploying AI without changing the human workflows around it produces tool adoption metrics, not business results; and (4) fundamentally wrong strategic framing — most firms are building AI to automate existing processes, when the real value comes from questioning whether those processes should exist in their current form at all. "Industrializers" treat AI as an industrial capability to be engineered with rigor — pipeline discipline, integration standards, outcome metrics — not as a pilot program to be run and admired.

**What it means for The Construct**  
This is the analytical lens Hartley should use on every portfolio company AI assessment: are they in the 90% (pilots everywhere, ROI nowhere) or building toward the 10% (industrial capability, embedded in operations)? For Brand 9 specifically, the lesson is sharper — don't automate the current signage quoting and production workflow; interrogate whether that workflow is the right architecture at all.
