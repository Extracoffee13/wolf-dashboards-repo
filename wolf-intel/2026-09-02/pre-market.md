(US Central Command hit Iranian targets; Tehran claimed retaliation against American assets
in the region) sent oil, bond yields, and the Fed's tone all moving the same direction at
once — the exact combination equity longs don't want to see three days before NFP.

## Rates / Oil — the actual driver

- **10-year Treasury yield**: hit a high of **4.814%** intraday Tuesday — highest since November 2023. A separate afternoon read had it at 4.788–4.79%, up from 4.74% the prior session. Either way: multi-decade/multi-year highs, moving fast.
- **Oil**: Brent crude trading near **$94.50–95/bbl** (highest in ~6 weeks); WTI advanced toward **$91/bbl** after jumping 5.2% Tuesday — the biggest one-day gain in five weeks. Driver: renewed US-Iran strikes raising fresh concern about Gulf energy infrastructure and Strait of Hormuz shipping.
- **Mortgage rates**: climbed for a third straight day, tracking the Treasury move. 10yr above 4.70% is the line technicians flag as the point where builder margin compression accelerates; a sustained move below 4.30% would be the tailwind. We are moving the wrong way for housing right now.

## What would change the setup

- A ceasefire signal or de-escalation headline out of the Iran conflict would likely reverse the oil/yield move fastest and hardest — that's the single biggest risk to being short the tape here.
- Friday's NFP, after a soft ADP, is the next real catalyst; a big miss would force the market to choose between "Fed cuts on weak labor" and "Fed hikes on war-driven inflation" — those two narratives cannot both be right, and Friday likely resolves which one the market prices.
