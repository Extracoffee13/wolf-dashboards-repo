## 1. Decision Agents Bring AI into the Boardroom
**Firm:** BCG (BCG Henderson Institute) · **Published:** July 7, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-ai-decision-agents-transform-strategy

**Summary:** BCG argues most enterprise AI spend today chases productivity and process automation, missing a higher-leverage application: a custom-built "decision agent" that sits inside the executive committee itself, functioning as an omniscient chief of staff with supercomputing power. Briefed ahead of a meeting, the agent arrives with a preliminary memo-level assessment, updates it live as discussion unfolds, gathers and synthesizes inputs, runs scenario tests, and converts the resulting recommendation into concrete follow-up actions. BCG frames the canonical failure mode this fixes: an executive asks "what happens if demand softens in Q3?" and the answer either isn't in the room or takes a week of spreadsheet reconciliation to produce. The piece names supply chain management as the first high-impact deployment area and stresses that guardrails — not agent capability — are the binding constraint on adoption.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is BCG telling Fortune 500 boards to build, at enormous internal cost, what a small AI-native operator can already run cheaply — a persistent agent embedded in IC decision flow rather than bolted onto individual tasks. That's a validation of the PRAXIS/agent-orchestration approach, but it's also a countdown clock: once this pattern is consulting-firm-blessed, larger PE shops will buy or build it within 12-18 months, and Hartley's edge shifts from "we have an IC decision agent" to "ours is better-integrated and cheaper to run."

## 2. Family Offices Adjust Asset Allocation to Meet Evolving Challenges
**Firm:** Roland Berger · **Published:** July 2026
**Source:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html

**Summary:** Roland Berger's family office study finds that geopolitical upheaval has overtaken interest-rate uncertainty as the top external risk facing family offices, particularly in the DACH region, as investors adapt to a rate environment they've now priced in. Private equity — through both funds and direct deals — shows the strongest growth momentum of any asset class, alongside defensive assets like precious metals, with roughly half of surveyed family offices planning to expand their PE exposure further. Sector priorities run infrastructure first, then medicine/healthcare, with artificial intelligence gaining ground fastest among the three. Most family offices still concentrate capital in stable developed markets (North America, Northern Europe) despite claiming international mandates, and their top operational worry is cybersecurity, ahead of digitalization and process professionalization.

**What it means for The Construct: ** Family offices are simultaneously increasing PE allocations and chasing AI exposure — exactly the two things a fundraising pitch for an AI-native roll-up platform needs to say in the same sentence. Hartley Capital's LP narrative should lead with "AI-operated roll-up" framing now, while family offices are actively reallocating toward both themes, rather than waiting for a dedicated fund cycle.

## 3. Global IPO Trends Q2 2026 / Global Private Equity Exit Readiness Study
**Firm:** EY · **Published:** July 7, 2026
**Source:** https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/ipo/trends

**Summary:** EY's Q2 2026 data shows IPO activity trending toward a four-year high, but PE sponsors continue to favor M&A and secondary sales over public listings for speed and execution certainty — a "bird in hand" pattern that has held through 2025 into 2026. Two-thirds of GPs surveyed say they expect to step up exit activity generally over the next 12-24 months. The companion Exit Readiness Study frames exit prep as a continuous discipline that needs to start 12-24 months pre-sale, and flags a new wrinkle: buyers are now explicitly diligencing target companies' AI strategy, AI adoption maturity, and disruption exposure as part of valuation — AI readiness is becoming an exit differentiator, not just an operating nice-to-have.

**What it means for The Construct:** Hartley's roll-up integration playbook needs to bake documented AI adoption into each platform company from acquisition day one, not retrofit it before a sale process — buyers are now pricing that in as diligence, and arriving at exit with a thin AI story will show up as a discount, not a neutral.
