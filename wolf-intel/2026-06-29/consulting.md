### PAPER 1 — BCG: "The AI-First Real Estate Company: An Opportunity for Structural Advantage"

**Published:** April 2026 (Executive Perspectives); June 2026 (main publication)  
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-ai-first-real-estate-company-advantage  
**Score: 4 / 5**

#### Executive Summary
BCG's sector analysis of AI adoption in real estate finds that real estate firms plan to spend less than **1% of revenues on AI** — the lowest investment level across all major industries surveyed — creating a structural first-mover window. For development companies, AI applications (construction site assistants, procurement optimization, timeline compression) deliver **400–700 basis points of margin uplift**, and AI-first DevCos compress project timelines by approximately 30%. Investment management firms capture **300–400 basis points of IRR uplift** through AI-driven valuation forecasters and automated LP commitment cycles. Facility and property management sees **200–300 bps EBIT uplift** from invoice automation and AI technician dispatch for predictive maintenance. The window is narrow — early movers who invest materially in AI now will build structural advantages that laggards cannot close inside a typical market cycle.

#### What It Means for The Construct
Brand 9's homebuilder clients and Florida real estate operations exist inside an industry systemically under-investing in AI — which means an AI-first signage-to-property-marketing workflow built today is a competitive moat most competitors will not close for 3–5 years. For Hartley Capital's real estate holdings, the 300–400bps IRR uplift from AI-driven LP automation and valuation tooling is not theoretical — it is deployable inside the current hold period.

### PAPER 3 — KPMG: "Global AI Pulse Survey Q2 2026"
**Firm:** KPMG  
**Published:** June 24, 2026  
**Source:** https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/06/growing-adoption-signals-progress-as-cost-visibility-and-accountability-drive-ai-value.html  
**Score: 3 / 5**

#### Executive Summary
KPMG's Q2 2026 Global AI Pulse, surveying 2,000+ business leaders across 20 countries at organizations with $50M+ annual revenues, finds organizations entering a **"pragmatic phase"** of AI — success is no longer defined by experimentation but by converting investment into measurable business value. Critical finding: organizations where CEOs hold explicit accountability for AI output decisions report **60% confidence** in their AI strategy vs. **22%** for those without CEO-level accountability. Leaders with strong AI cost visibility are **5x more likely** to achieve ROI (15% vs. 3%). Only 14% of firms with CEO accountability report established ROI (vs. 4% without). AI is shifting from an IT function to a board-level strategic responsibility, and the gap between pragmatic deployers and experimenters is now measurable.

#### What It Means for The Construct
This is a governance benchmark, not just an AI adoption survey. If Hartley Capital's portfolio companies don't have CEO-level accountability structures and AI cost visibility dashboards in place, they're statistically in the 78% reporting low confidence and the 85% not yet achieving ROI. The accountability infrastructure must precede — not follow — scaled agent deployment.
