### PAPER 3 — Score: 4 / 5

**Title:** Reinforcement Learning for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems through Orchestration Traces  
**Source:** arXiv:2605.02801  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02801  
**Published:** May 2026

**Executive Summary:**  
This paper introduces Parallel-Agent Reinforcement Learning (PARL), a training methodology demonstrated through Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5, which deployed an Agent Swarm trained at up to 4,000 coordinated steps. The central insight is methodologically significant: rather than training individual agents in isolation, PARL trains agents on *orchestration traces* — the actual coordination patterns, handoffs, and decision sequences that emerge when multi-agent systems execute complex workflows. This produces agents that have internalized not just task completion but team dynamics, dramatically improving performance on long-horizon tasks that require multi-agent collaboration. The implication for enterprise agent deployments is that the training substrate matters as much as the model — organizations that run agentic workflows and capture orchestration telemetry are building a proprietary training asset. This is the mechanism by which early adopters compound their advantage: their agents get better faster because they generate richer orchestration data.

**What It Means for The Construct:**  
Every agentic workflow Hartley Capital runs — deal sourcing, portfolio monitoring, market intelligence — generates orchestration traces that, if captured, become training data for the next generation of those agents. Starting now means compounding for 12–24 months before the methodology becomes standard practice. This is not theoretical: Moonshot already demonstrated it at 4,000-step coordination scale.

**Score: 4** — Sharpens the thesis that Hartley Capital's agent deployment strategy should prioritize telemetry capture from day one; the logs are the moat.
