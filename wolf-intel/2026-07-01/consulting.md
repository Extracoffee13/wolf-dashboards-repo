## 1. The Shift to Agentic AI: Evidence from Codex — Score: 4

**Source:** OpenAI (Johnston, Holtz) via arXiv — [arxiv.org/abs/2606.26959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26959) — submitted 29 Jun 2026

**Summary:** An OpenAI-authored empirical study of internal and enterprise Codex adoption over H1 2026. Active users are up 5x since the start of the year; usage among *non-developer* employees inside adopting organizations is up 189x since August 2025. Codex now generates over 85% of the average OpenAI employee's output tokens. The paper frames this as evidence that "agentic AI" has moved past pilot programs into being the default interface for a meaningful share of enterprise knowledge work — and that the fastest-growing user segment isn't engineers, it's everyone else.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the clearest evidence yet that agent-ops-at-scale is not a 2027 bet — non-technical adoption is already compounding inside the frontier labs' own customer base, which means Hartley Capital's agent-AI operating thesis for portfolio companies should assume competitors are further along than they look from the outside, and the differentiator shifts from "using agents" to "governing them well."

## 2. M&A Midyear Report 2026: A Winner's Paradox — Score: 4

**Source:** Bain & Company — [bain.com/insights/m-and-a-midyear-outlook-2026-a-winners-paradox](https://www.bain.com/insights/m-and-a-midyear-outlook-2026-a-winners-paradox/) — published 29 Jun 2026 (confirmed via press-release syndication timestamp)

**Summary:** Global M&A deal value is up 41% YoY to $2.4T through the first five months of 2026, with megadeals (>$10B) up ~52% in count. Bain's headline framing is the "AI winner's paradox": acquirers are being forced to run aggressive dealmaking programs and fund AI transformation simultaneously, with the firms that try to sequence the two (deal first, digitize later) losing ground to those funding both at once.

**What it means for The Construct:** This directly sharpens the Hartley Capital roll-up thesis — it's no longer enough to underwrite a roll-up on multiple arbitrage and integration savings alone; the capital plan needs an explicit AI-transformation line item from day one, or the acquired platform arrives already behind the market it's consolidating.

## 3. AI Premium — Score: 3

**Source:** Borri (LUISS), Liu (U. Rochester), Tsyvinski (Yale/NBER) via arXiv — [arxiv.org/abs/2606.30583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30583) — submitted 29 Jun 2026, corroborated independently across two research passes including external pickup in a quant newsletter

**Summary:** Using 380 trillion tokens of licensed real-world AI-consumption data across 400+ LLMs (~2% of global monthly AI token use), the authors construct a firm-level "AI Beta" from how closely a company's stock return co-moves with aggregate AI usage. High-AI-beta firms show statistically significant excess returns going forward; a long-short portfolio sorted on AI Beta earns ~64.1 bps/week, concentrated among firms with frontier, closed-source, paid AI exposure.

**What it means for The Construct:** Gives Hartley Capital's hedge fund operations a candidate factor to backtest — if AI-usage exposure is genuinely priced with a lag, it's a screenable signal worth testing against the existing multi-strategy book before the factor gets arbitraged away.
