#### Executive Summary
McKinsey's real estate practice drops the clearest quantified case yet for agentic AI as an operational layer — not a productivity add-on — inside property businesses. Analyzing labor-productivity data across 48 countries, the report sizes the addressable value at $430–550 billion in annual economic impact across real estate, construction, and development. The mechanism is the paradigm shift from generative AI as a research assistant ("help me understand") to agentic AI as an action layer ("help me get it done"), automating multi-step workflows directly inside core business systems — CRMs, lease management, maintenance dispatch, and tenant portals — without human handoffs at each step. Early deployments already show >30% time savings on maintenance workflows, 3–7 percentage-point improvement in renewal rates, and >90% reduction in lead response time. The report frames this as a structural operating-model redesign, not an efficiency initiative, and argues that operators who move first will accumulate data and process advantages that compound.

- 62% of operators are at minimum experimenting with agents (sourced from broader McKinsey AI state data)

### 3. Business and IT Leaders Report AI Agents Are Scaling Faster Than Their Guardrails
**Firm:** Deloitte Insights
**Published:** 2026 (recent; exact date unconfirmed within 48h window)
**URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html
**Score: 3 / 5 — Useful background**

#### Executive Summary
Deloitte surveys 3,235 IT and business leaders and finds a structural governance gap at the precise moment enterprises are scaling agentic AI. The headline: 74% of respondents expect their companies to be running AI agents by 2027, but only 21% report having a mature governance model in place for agentic AI today. The gap is not ignorance — it's velocity mismatch. Procurement and deployment of agent infrastructure is outrunning policy, risk frameworks, and human-oversight protocols. Deloitte's data shows organizations are moving from "exploration" to "implementation" faster than their compliance infrastructure can track. The report identifies three failure modes: unauthorized action (agents doing things outside their intended scope), accountability gaps (unclear who owns an agent's decision), and data-leakage risk (agents traversing systems without adequate access controls).

#### Key Data Points
- 74% of respondents expect AI agent deployment by 2027
- Only 21% have a mature agentic AI governance model
- 3,235 IT and business leaders surveyed
- Three failure modes: unauthorized action, accountability gaps, data leakage
- Survey includes both private-company and enterprise respondents

#### What It Means for The Construct
For Hartley Capital building agent AI infrastructure for portfolio operations: the 79% governance-gap figure is a liability, not a badge of speed. Any PE firm building agent stacks without concurrent governance frameworks is creating acquirer-diligence risk.
