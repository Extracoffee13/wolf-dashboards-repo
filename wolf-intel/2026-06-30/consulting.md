### 1. Bain & Company — Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest"

#### Executive Summary

The private equity recovery that had gathered momentum at the start of 2026 has stalled. Bain identifies a "triple shock" that hit in H1 2026 simultaneously: (1) an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" that cratered software multiples as LLM-native alternatives disrupted SaaS incumbents, (2) redemption stress in private credit as liquidity concerns mounted, and (3) the Iran war and attendant oil price spike. Tech deal value collapsed 70% quarter-over-quarter (Q4 2025 → Q1 2026); $1B+ tech deals fell from 15 to 4 in a single quarter. The headline framework is "12 is the new 5" — a deal that would have needed 5% annual EBITDA growth a decade ago to hit a 2.5x return over five years now requires 12%, because purchase multiples and financing costs have pushed the deal cost index to record levels. Buyout distributions have run at record lows for four consecutive years, implying a capital cycle approaching 7 years versus historical norms. Bain's verdict is blunt: PE is not in a crisis, it is *stuck*. The issue is not capital — dry powder remains substantial — it is confidence. Best-positioned firms are leaning into AI-enabled value creation in portfolio companies and maintaining discipline in deal selection. The report emphasizes that roll-up and buy-and-build strategies remain viable but require true operational expertise, not financial engineering, to generate returns in this environment.

For Hartley Capital, "12 is the new 5" is a real-time recalibration of return math that should filter every roll-up opportunity through a tighter operational lens — if your value creation thesis doesn't compound EBITDA at double-digit rates organically (not just through multiple expansion), you are running a losing trade. #### Executive Summary

BCG's report makes the structural argument that real estate is the sector most exposed to inefficiency AND the sector most under-investing in AI to fix it. The data is striking: 66% of real estate projects experience schedule overruns and 39% face cost overruns, driven by fragmentation and rework. Yet real estate firms plan to spend less than 1% of revenues on AI — dramatically behind tech and financial services. AI-first development companies (DevCos) that move now can compress project timelines by approximately 30%, accelerate capital deployment cycles, and capture 400–700 basis points of margin uplift from applications including construction site assistants, procurement optimization, and generative design. The transformation framework is a six-step sequence: set multiyear AI targets tied to asset value; CEO owns AI as chief AI officer; focus on two or three high-impact bets; embed AI across the workforce via upskilling and redesigned workflows; establish governance with an AI delivery office; and build a single BIM-centric data model across core real estate systems. BCG is explicit that this is a narrow window — the structural advantages accrue to the first movers, and the gap between AI-native and laggard firms is widening faster than most real estate executives appreciate.

### 3. McKinsey — "The Agentic Organization: Contours of the Next Paradigm for the AI Era"

**Firm:** McKinsey & Company
**Published:** June 18, 2026
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/the-agentic-organization-contours-of-the-next-paradigm-for-the-ai-era
**Score: 4 / 5** — *Sharpens a thesis*

#### Executive Summary

McKinsey defines the "agentic organization" as a new operating paradigm in which humans and AI agents — both virtual and physical — work side by side at near-zero marginal cost per unit of work. The report maps five pillars: business model, operating model, governance, workforce/people/culture, and technology/data. Notably, McKinsey finds that the largest new job category in the agentic era is not engineers but AI-augmented frontline workers in sales, service, HR, and operations — these roles outnumber technical roles 10-to-1 and require no code. Organizations win by combining clear strategic intent (defined outcomes, not tool selection) with tight feedback loops and disciplined governance. The report warns that without top-down change management to communicate how agents function as coworkers, reskilling efforts remain ineffective. Agentic AI also enables organizational flattening — leaders can manage larger scopes with AI augmentation, compressing middle management layers and accelerating decision velocity. ROI, McKinsey emphasizes, comes from embedding agents deep in *core workflows*, not pilot programs.

#### What It Means for The Construct

The "agents-in-core-workflows not pilots" framework is the exact lens Hartley Capital should apply when evaluating AI-adjacent portfolio companies or when building internal ops infrastructure — the question is not whether to experiment, but whether the bet is embedded in the operational core or sitting in a sandbox; only the former accrues to enterprise value. For The Construct's own operations, the finding that frontline AI augmentation (not engineering talent) is the highest-leverage hire aligns with building agent-enhanced client service capacity rather than building proprietary models.
