### 3. How AI Agents Are Transforming Supply Chains
**Firm:** BCG
**Published:** June 1, 2026 (2 days ago)
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-ai-agents-are-transforming-supply-chains
**Score: 4 / 5 — sharpens a thesis**

**Executive Summary:**
BCG argues that AI agents break "longstanding tradeoffs" in supply chain management by enabling parallel, multi-constraint optimization that sequential human workflows structurally cannot perform. The mechanism: where a human supply chain team works through problems linearly (identify issue → escalate → coordinate → decide), a multi-agent system simultaneously runs simulations across supplier alternatives, inventory buffers, logistics routes, and demand forecasts, surfacing solutions no individual human workflow would reach because the solution space is too large to traverse sequentially. The paper frames this not as automation of existing tasks but as a category expansion — agents identify *new* solutions by exploring solution spaces humans skip. BCG cites early enterprise deployments showing 15-25% reduction in expedite costs and material availability improvements in disruption scenarios.

**What it means for The Construct:**
This is the theoretical scaffolding for Hartley Capital's multi-agent operations thesis in PE portfolio companies — the argument is not "agents do what humans do, faster," it is "agents do what humans *cannot* do, because the search space is too large." That reframe has material implications for how The Construct pitches agentic infrastructure to portfolio company CFOs.
