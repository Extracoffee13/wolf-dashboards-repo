## 1. Agent-to-Agent Finance: Blockchain Payments and Trust Infrastructure for Autonomous AI Agents
**Firm/Source:** arXiv (q-fin.GN), Hui Gong, UCL Institute of Finance & Technology
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00245
**Published:** 2026-06-30 (submitted; in scan window when broadened by a few days)
**Score: 4/5**

Gong argues autonomous AI agents are moving from analytical tools to transacting counterparties — able to interpret goals, call external tools, negotiate with other agents, and initiate payments or on-chain transactions on their own. The paper develops "agent-to-agent finance" as a distinct infrastructure layer: identity and authorization for agents, payment rails, verification of claims, reputation systems, and auditable accountability trails for machine-mediated commerce. It's a systematization paper (22 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables) rather than an empirical study — the contribution is naming and structuring the problem, not solving it.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the infrastructure layer Hartley Capital's agent-AI thesis will eventually run on top of — if autonomous agents are going to transact on our behalf (executing trades, negotiating vendor contracts, running PE deal-sourcing pipelines), we need to be tracking identity/reputation/audit standards for agent commerce now, before they calcify around someone else's stack.

## 2. Global Family Business Report 2026
**Firm/Source:** KPMG (with Meridian West)
**URL:** https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ai-and-technology/global-family-business-report.html
**Published:** report PDF filed under KPMG's 2026/07 asset path; in-window
**Score: 4/5**

Survey of 1,927 senior leaders across 41 jurisdictions — one of the broadest of its kind. Headline finding: family enterprises describe an accelerating shift from family-run operating businesses to family-owned enterprises governed by boards and led by professional (often non-family) management, with many expecting the transition substantially complete by 2035. Leaders report strategic confidence but a widening gap between current governance/AI-readiness/talent capability and what professionalized operation will require over the next decade — creating what KPMG frames as an "urgent governance challenge."

**What it means for The Construct:** Professionalization creates supply — family enterprises that can't close the governance/AI-readiness gap on their own become acquisition or partnership targets, and Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis should treat "governance gap" as a sourcing signal, not just a risk flag on diligence.

## 3. Human skills will matter more than ever in the age of AI
**Firm/Source:** McKinsey Global Institute
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/media-center/human-skills-will-matter-more-than-ever-in-the-age-of-ai
**Published:** 2026-07-09 (in-window, confirmed)
**Score: 3/5**

MGI's latest cut on AI-and-work: more than 70% of skills employers seek today apply to both automatable and non-automatable work, and while existing tech could automate tasks making up over half of current U.S. work hours, that doesn't translate into wholesale job elimination — the shift is people doing different things within their workday, with social-emotional, coordination, and process-management skills at a premium as agents absorb the rest.

**What it means for The Construct: **Background context, not a lever — but it's a useful reality check against over-indexing on headcount-reduction narratives when pitching agent-AI-driven ops efficiency to PE targets; the sellable story is workflow redesign, not pure substitution.
