## Paper 1 — Score: 4 (Sharpens a thesis)

**Title:** Reimagining Tech Infrastructure for Agentic AI  
**Firm:** McKinsey & Company (McKinsey Technology)  
**Published:** ~late April 2026  
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/our-insights/reimagining-tech-infrastructure-for-and-with-agentic-ai

### Executive Summary

McKinsey's technology practice identifies a structural infrastructure crisis hiding inside the agentic AI hype cycle. IT infrastructure costs are projected to grow 2–3× by 2030 while budgets stay flat — a collision course. The data point that should stop every serious operator: 62% of organizations are currently experimenting with or piloting AI agents, but in any given business function, fewer than 10% have reached true scale. The gap between pilot and production is not a model problem; it's a foundation problem. For CTOs, McKinsey frames a dual challenge — rebuild infrastructure fast enough to support agents (speed, scalability, reliability), while simultaneously deploying agents to contain the rising cost of infrastructure itself. High-value domains where this flywheel is already turning: service desk automation, observability and ITSM, network operations, and active cost/contract management. The paper's thesis is that organizations that build agent-ready foundations will see infrastructure transform from a cost center into the platform that *orchestrates how work is executed* across the entire enterprise — a fundamental shift in the role of IT.

### What It Means for The Construct

For **Hartley Capital**, this is the best empirical framing for the agent ops investment thesis available in print: the 62%-experimenting / <10%-scaling gap is exactly the moat. The firms that close that gap first — embedding agent-ready infrastructure at the platform level, not the project level — capture compounding speed and cost advantages that latecomers cannot replicate by buying more compute. The paper validates building the stack now, before the gap closes.

## Paper 2 — Score: 4 (Sharpens a thesis)

**Title:** Future of Real Estate: Ecosystems and Active Ownership  
**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group (BCG)  
**Published:** March 13, 2026  
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/future-of-real-estate-ecosystems-and-active-ownership  
**PDF:** https://web-assets.bcg.com/pdf-src/prod-live/future-of-real-estate-ecosystems-and-active-ownership.pdf

### Executive Summary

BCG's real estate practice makes an argument that reframes the industry's competitive logic entirely: real estate is no longer won in capital markets — it is won in operations. The report's central thesis is that buildings must evolve from standalone assets into adaptive, continuously optimized *platforms*. The levers reshaping the operating model are AI, industrialized construction methods, next-generation facility management, and sustainability execution — not cheaper debt or better acquisition timing. "Active ownership" displaces passive asset-holding as the value-creation mechanism. The report also surfaces a damning data point: most real estate firms plan to spend less than 1% of revenues on AI — a figure BCG frames as existentially underpowered given the scale of the operational transformation underway. Winning players will integrate physical assets into digital ecosystems where performance is continuously monitored, optimized, and re-monetized.

### What It Means for The Construct

For **Brand 9**, this is the macro validation of the wayfinding-as-infrastructure thesis: when buildings are platforms, intelligent signage and wayfinding stop being a finish specification and become a *data layer* of the operating system. Homebuilder and commercial developer clients who read BCG are already being told their competitors will win on ops, not capital — which means Brand 9's pitch into that audience is now backed by the most credible strategy voice in the room.

## Paper 3 — Score: 3 (Useful background)

**Title:** HFS Horizons: Agentic Services, 2026  
**Firm:** HFS Research (distributed / excerpted by KPMG and EY)  
**Published:** April 2026  
**URL:** https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2026/04/hfs-horizons-agentic-services-2026.pdf  
**EY excerpt:** https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/about-us/analyst-relations/documents/ey-gl-hfs-horizons-agentic-services-2026-ey-excerpt-04-2026.pdf

### Executive Summary

The inaugural HFS Horizons ranking of enterprise agentic services providers — 36 firms evaluated across a three-horizon maturity model: Horizon 1 (functional digital transformation via agentic AI), Horizon 2 (OneOffice enterprise innovation across front/middle/back), Horizon 3 (enterprise-wide agentic modernization). KPMG and EY are named Horizon 3 Market Leaders. Data collection ran September 2025–January 2026. The report's most durable finding: the bottleneck to agentic enterprise scale is no longer the technology — it is operating models, data readiness, and governance maturity. Nearly 90% of employees say their employer's agentic AI training is insufficient. Technical complexity, limited application time, weak incentives, and gaps in internal infrastructure are the identified brakes. The report frames "agentic services" as a distinct category — AI-led services that augment human capability through smart agents to optimize processes and decision-making — and treats it as a procurement category that enterprise buyers should be evaluating now.

### What It Means for The Construct

For **Hartley Capital** evaluating AI service partners for portfolio company ops, this is the definitive 2026 landscape map — and the 90% training gap signals the real alpha: the PE firm or family office that solves agent *enablement of people*, not just agent *deployment of technology*, builds a durable performance advantage that vendor commoditization cannot erase.
