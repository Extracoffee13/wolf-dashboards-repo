### 3. Roland Berger — "The AI Value Gap" (a.k.a. "Profitless Prosperity in AI")
**Published:** March 2026  
**Firm:** Roland Berger  
**URL:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html  
**Score: 3 — Useful background**

**Executive Summary:**  
Based on a survey of 203 senior executives across industries, Roland Berger finds that investment in AI is surging while nearly 90% of firms report returns lagging spending — what the firm calls "profitless prosperity." Only ~10% consistently capture meaningful AI value. The differentiator is not budget or speed: it is operating model. The 10% are "Industrializers" — firms that treat AI as an engineered industrial capability (with defined ownership, feedback loops, retraining pipelines, and production-grade infrastructure) rather than a portfolio of purchased pilots. The Industrializer model reaches financial breakeven faster precisely because it separates AI capability-building from AI project-buying. The report draws an explicit analogy to how manufacturers treated quality management in the 1980s: firms that institutionalized it won; those that bolted it on lost.

**What it means for The Construct:**  
The Industrializer/pilot-buyer distinction is exactly the framing Hartley Capital should use when evaluating AI maturity in roll-up targets — a portfolio company that has launched 12 AI pilots but has no production models and no retraining infrastructure is not an AI company, it's a cost center. This paper gives the language and framework to evaluate AI readiness as a due-diligence dimension.

## ARXIV TOP-3 (cs.AI / econ.GN / q-fin this week)

**1. "Multi-agent AI systems outperform human teams in creativity"**  
arXiv:2605.17885 | Submitted May 18, 2026  
University of Cambridge, Zhejiang University, Microsoft Research Asia  
Across 4,541 multi-agent LLM runs vs. 341 human-team trials on six problem-solving tasks: multi-agent LLM teams outperform human teams with Cohen's d = 1.50 (large effect). Advantage driven by novelty, not usefulness gap. Teams that range widely in conversation (low global coherence) produce more creative output — both LLM and human. Implication: tight agent orchestration constrains value; wide-ranging multi-agent dialogue is the architecture that wins on creative tasks.  
**Construct relevance:** Agent architecture design for Hartley Capital's multi-agent analyst system — loose coupling over strict orchestration for generative/ideation tasks.

**2. "Scaling Teams or Scaling Time? Memory-Enabled Lifelong Learning in LLM Multi-Agent Systems"**  
arXiv:2604.03295 | March 27, 2026  
Emory, Illinois Tech, Notre Dame, Cisco Research  
Key finding: the scaling landscape for multi-agent LLMs is non-monotonic. Larger teams do not always outperform smaller ones; smaller teams with well-designed persistent memory can outperform large teams. Introduces LLMA-Mem, a lifelong memory topology framework that improves long-horizon performance while reducing cost. Scaling via diversity (different models, prompts, tools) yields more sustained gains than scaling via agent headcount.  
**Construct relevance:** Directly relevant to Hartley Capital agent stack design — don't scale headcount, scale memory and heterogeneity.

**3. "Multi-Agent Teams Hold Experts Back"**  
arXiv:2602.01011 | February 2026  
Key finding: expertise dilution worsens with team size in LLM multi-agent systems. Positive correlation between team size and synergy gap across all tasks — LLM teams increasingly underperform their best individual member as team size grows, due to compromise behavior. Under imperfect monitoring, agents fail to self-organize complementary roles.  
**Construct relevance:** Confirms the principle that agent quality > agent quantity; relevant when designing hedge fund research agent workflows where precision matters more than coverage breadth.
