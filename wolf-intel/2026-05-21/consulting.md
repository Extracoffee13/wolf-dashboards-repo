### Executive Summary

BCG's real-estate-specific AI-first report lands a precise indictment: only 25% of real estate firms qualify as AI leaders, compared to 40% across all industries — and the sector is investing roughly half the cross-industry average in AI. This is not a tech-readiness gap; it's a structural lag that creates a closing window for early movers. The report is explicit that the window for establishing structural advantage is open but not indefinitely so: first movers lock in operating model advantages that compound as the rest of the sector catches up.

**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/inside-the-ai-first-private-equity-firm  
**Published:** 2026 (confirmed surfacing May cycle alongside companion RE and payments pieces)

### Executive Summary

BCG's PE-specific AI-first report is a direct challenge to the current state of portfolio AI deployment. The core finding: most PE-backed companies have deployed AI tools across the portfolio but few have done the hard work of transforming their operating model or value proposition to capture real value. BCG introduces a three-tier transformation ladder — **Deploy** (handing out AI tool licenses with no structural change, what most portfolio companies are doing today), **Reshape** (redesigning core business functions to be AI-first), and **Invent** (fundamental rethinking of customer value proposition to build new business models). The argument is that PE firms treating AI as a productivity vendor, rather than as a core operating model redesign, are capturing maybe 10–20% of the available value. The report also addresses the GP level: PE firms themselves are early in AI adoption for deal sourcing, portfolio monitoring, reporting, and LP communications — firms that build systematic AI infrastructure at the GP level differentiate on both returns and fundraising.

### What It Means for The Construct

This paper is essentially Hartley Capital's differentiation thesis written by a third party: the firms that win in the next cycle are the ones that operate in Reshape/Invent mode, not Deploy mode. WOLF can arm Hartley Capital with this framework for both internal positioning (how we operate differently from vanilla PE) and LP communications (why our portfolio companies capture more of the AI value curve than the median PE-backed company).

**Press release:** May 7, 2026

### Executive Summary

EY's infrastructure-focused agentic AI report frames the core problem as coordination failure, not technology failure: infrastructure productivity is constrained not by a lack of technology but by fragmented systems, data, and stakeholders that limit effective coordination. The report claims rework alone accounts for up to 15% of total project costs, and the global infrastructure sector faces a $64 trillion funding gap through 2050. The proposed solution is an "intelligence layer" — an agentic AI system sitting above and between existing software systems, delivering real-time actionable insights by connecting siloed data sources. This is not a replace-your-stack play; it's an overlay-and-connect architecture. EY provides a five-pillar governance framework: accountability/legal liability, transparency/explainability, data sovereignty, human oversight and competence, and systemic resilience. The model is presented as immediately deployable for governments and infrastructure operators managing large program portfolios.

### What It Means for The Construct

The "intelligence layer" model maps precisely to how WOLF's own multi-agent architecture operates — agentic systems sitting above fragmented data sources, connecting them in real time — giving The Construct third-party language and credibility for enterprise pitch decks. For Hartley Capital, any infrastructure-adjacent PE thesis (construction tech, prop-tech, facility management roll-ups) now has a $64T funding gap as the demand justification.
