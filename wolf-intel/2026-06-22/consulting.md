### Paper 1 — Score 4

**Title:** Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest: Private Equity Midyear Report 2026  
**Firm:** Bain & Company  
**Source URL:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/  
**Published:** June 8, 2026

**Executive Summary:**  
Bain's biannual PE report lands with a blunt verdict: the 2026 buyout recovery is stalling for the second consecutive year, this time killed by a "triple shock" — the AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" compressing software revenue at scale, redemption stress in private credit markets, and the Iran conflict spiking oil prices. The result: 33,000 unsold portfolio companies remain in limbo, M&A exit activity is down sharply, and fundraising is under pressure. The headline framework Bain introduces is "12 is the new 5" — a deal that needed only 5% annual EBITDA growth to return 2.5x a decade ago now requires 10–12%, reflecting higher entry multiples, slower exits, and a compressed multiple environment. The report finds that winning PE firms in this environment are those that control operational levers rather than relying on multiple expansion or debt engineering. AI-enabled operational value creation is specifically called out as a differentiator — firms deploying AI for portfolio company efficiency are pulling ahead. LP patience is wearing thin, with distributions well below expectations entering 2026.

**What it means for The Construct:**  
For Hartley Capital, this reshapes the roll-up entry thesis: the SaaSpocalypse is *depressing valuations* on software-adjacent businesses while creating 33,000 distressed portfolio companies actively seeking exits or add-on partners — the entry price window for disciplined roll-ups is opening. Brand 9's insulation from SaaS revenue dynamics (physical signage/services are not software) makes it a structurally differentiated asset in this environment.

### Paper 2 — Score 4

**Title:** AI-First Enterprise Operations: Reinventing the Operating System of Work  
**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group  
**Source URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/reinventing-the-operating-system-of-work-with-ai  
**Companion PDF:** https://www.bcg.com/assets/2026/executive-perspectives-applied-ai-with-agentic-enterprise-operations.pdf (June 2026 Executive Perspectives)  
**Published:** June 15, 2026 (Executive Perspectives companion: June 2026)

**Executive Summary:**  
BCG's most rigorous 2026 agentic AI operations paper draws a decisive line between organizations using AI at the task level (copilots, point automation) and those redesigning processes end-to-end for agentic execution. The former achieve sub-20% efficiency gains. The latter achieve 60%+ cost reductions. BCG's framing: AI without process redesign is "paving the cowpath." The report presents a framework for "outcome-managed agents" — where AI orchestrators own multi-step workflows with defined outcome metrics rather than simply augmenting human steps. Key data point: only 30% of surveyed organizations have integrated AI agents into workflows (up from 13% last year), but among those, the variance in outcomes is vast — the top quartile is operating with 60%+ cost structures while the bottom quartile is seeing minimal ROI. The paper makes the case that the "operating system of work" — the underlying process architecture — is the constraint, not the AI tooling itself.

**What it means for The Construct:**  
For Hartley Capital's agent AI investment thesis: this validates the bet on *infrastructure and process redesign* over UI-layer copilots — the 60% cost gap IS the moat. For Brand 9: a signage firm that rebuilds its project management, customer intake, and production scheduling around agent-native workflows will have a structural cost advantage competitors won't be able to copy with off-the-shelf tools.

### Paper 3 — Score 3

**Title:** Agentic AI is Scaling Faster Than Guardrails  
**Firm:** Deloitte Insights  
**Source URL:** https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html  
**Published:** 2026 (specific June date not confirmed; part of the 2026 State of AI enterprise series)

**Executive Summary:**  
Deloitte's enterprise AI scan finds the governance architecture for agentic AI is badly lagging deployment. While 85% of enterprises are customizing agents for their workflows and ~75% plan to deploy agentic AI within two years, only 21% report having a mature governance model for agent management. Deloitte surveys 3,235+ senior leaders and finds the top risks cited are: unintended actions, tool misuse, scope creep beyond guardrails, and inter-agent coordination failures in multi-agent environments. The report calls out a specific gap: organizations are scaling agents without building the "control layer" — logging, audit trails, rollback capability, and human-in-the-loop escalation paths. Deloitte frames this as both a risk and a structural market opportunity for governance tooling vendors. The report also notes that agentic AI is being deployed earliest in customer support and knowledge management, while high-stakes domains (finance, legal, compliance) remain largely pre-agentic.

**What it means for The Construct:**  
The 79% governance gap (of the 75% planning to deploy, only 21% are governance-ready) is Hartley Capital's signal: the earliest defensible position in agent AI is *agent governance infrastructure*, not the agents themselves — the compliance and control layer is the high-margin B2B play that enterprise buyers will pay for before the market commoditizes the AI stack below it.
