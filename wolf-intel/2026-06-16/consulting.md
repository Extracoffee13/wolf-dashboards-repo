### [SCORE 4] Bain & Company — "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest: Private Equity Midyear Report 2026"
**Published:** June 8, 2026
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/

**Executive Summary:**
Bain's H1 2026 PE midyear report opens with a blunt verdict: the recovery stalled. Three rapid-fire shocks killed momentum — an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" hammering software valuations, redemption stress in private credit, and an Iran-war-driven oil spike. Dealmaking, fundraising, and exit activity all compressed. More structurally alarming: Bain's deal cost index shows PE transactions are now arguably more expensive than at any point in the industry's history, requiring 12% EBITDA growth over five years to hit a 2.5x return — versus 5% a decade ago. The report identifies a bifurcation emerging between firms that are actively harnessing AI across portfolio operations (moving from "pilot" to "Day 1 execution plan") and those treating AI as a future consideration. Buy-and-build / bolt-on strategies are specifically called out as thriving in small- and mid-cap, where headroom for value creation remains widest. Bain frames inaction on AI as a strategic choice — not a neutral one.

**What it means for The Construct:**
Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis is specifically where PE is still working — mid-market buy-and-build is the one sub-strategy where deal economics still pencil, and the firms winning there are the ones with AI-embedded value creation plans from day one, not retrofitted post-close. The "12% EBITDA growth required" data point is a forcing function: if Hartley's roll-up targets can't show AI-driven margin improvement in the underwriting model, the return math doesn't work at current entry prices.

### [SCORE 4] Roland Berger — "The AI Value Gap" (Profitless Prosperity in AI)
**Published:** Study conducted March 2026, published June 2026
**Source:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html
**Press release:** https://rolandberger-com.mynewsdesk.com/pressreleases/the-ai-cost-trap-why-the-added-value-of-billion-dollar-investments-remains-elusive-3435729

**Executive Summary:**
Roland Berger surveyed 200+ senior executives across Europe, Japan, and the US in March 2026 and found that nearly 90% report financial returns from AI investments lagging behind spending levels. Only ~10% consistently capture meaningful impact. The core failure mode is structural: firms purchase modern AI tools but integrate them superficially — new layers on top of old systems rather than interconnected ecosystems. The problem compounds with AI agents specifically: agents that "can think logically but cannot reliably operate within company systems" because those systems were never rebuilt to be machine-navigable. Roland Berger names this "profitless prosperity" — boards are green-lighting AI spend, headline deployments are running in production, but the financial breakeven never arrives because the value capture layer was never built. The 10% that consistently capture meaningful ROI share one trait: they define specific, narrow use cases tied to measurable financial outcomes (pricing, procurement, logistics, engineering) rather than deploying AI broadly.

**What it means for The Construct:**
This is the precise moat thesis for Hartley Capital's AI-native portfolio strategy — the 90% who are stuck in profitless prosperity are the acquisition targets; the 10% discipline (narrow use case, clean data, measurable EBITDA link) is the operational playbook for value creation post-close. For agent AI builds internally, this is the warning: don't paper over legacy workflows with an agent layer. Rebuild the substrate, or the ROI never appears.

### [SCORE 3] PwC — "2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer"
**Published:** June 15, 2026 (today)
**Source:** https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html
**Press release:** https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html

**Executive Summary:**
PwC's third annual AI Jobs Barometer analyzed over 1 billion job advertisements across 27 countries and territories, tracking how AI is bifurcating the labor market in real time. The central finding: two distinct labor tracks are solidifying. "Professionalised" roles — where AI automates routine tasks so human judgment, expertise, and creativity are emphasized (radiologists, senior recruiters, strategy analysts) — are growing at twice the rate and commanding 42% faster salary growth than "democratised" roles, where AI makes the role itself easier for non-experts to perform (basic coders, junior analysts). The macro signal: companies most exposed to AI are growing headcount 52% faster than the least exposed (vs. 36%), while "superstar companies" in the top AI adoption tier are logging 163% labor productivity gains over peers. The report warns that most of the coming labor disruption will be quiet — not mass layoffs but gradual role restructuring that disadvantages workers who don't upskill into human-judgment functions.

**What it means for The Construct:**
For Hartley Capital's agent AI buildout, this is the operating framework: you're building an infrastructure that creates "professionalised" leverage — fewer humans handling vastly more analytical work — not democratizing away the expertise. Brand 9 / signage is a watch zone: AI is actively democratizing design and spec work, which compresses margins on commodity signage; the B9 move must be toward consultative, judgment-heavy work (site-specific wayfinding strategy, permit navigation, builder relationships) where AI augments rather than replaces.
