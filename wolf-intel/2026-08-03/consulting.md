## 1. Score: 4 — "The private equity agenda in the age of AI: Core strategies"

**Firm:** Oliver Wyman (Oliver Wyman Forum / NYSE 2026 CEO Survey, 415 CEOs)
**Source:** https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2026/jul/how-private-equity-ceo-outpace-public-companies-with-ai.html
**Published:** July 2026 (exact day not exposed by search index)

**Summary:** Drawing on a 415-CEO survey run with the NYSE, Oliver Wyman finds PE-backed CEOs are deploying AI meaningfully faster than public-company peers — 49% cite falling behind on AI as a top-three threat to their business, versus 22% of public-company CEOs. The gap is attributed to structural incentives: finite hold periods and concentrated ownership push PE-backed operators toward faster, higher-conviction AI bets, with growth (75%) and cost/operational excellence (roughly two-thirds) as the leading priorities for where AI spend goes.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is closer to consensus-validation than contrarian signal now — PE operators themselves report the urgency Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis assumes. Read it as a closing window, not an open one: if 49% of PE-backed CEOs already treat AI-lag as existential, "AI-native operator" stops being a differentiator in diligence rooms within 12-18 months and becomes a checkbox. The edge shifts from *having* agent AI to being able to prove it's actually driving the multiple.

## 2. Score: 4 — "The Cost of Caution with AI Investments"

**Firm:** BCG
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/building-business-value-with-ai-investment
**Published:** July 31, 2026

**Summary:** BCG argues the dominant 2026 AI risk is under-investment, not over-investment. Leading companies commit roughly 1.7% of revenue to AI versus 0.8% for laggards — a 2x+ gap — and the differentiator isn't the number so much as treating AI as core infrastructure: funding transformation strategically and redesigning the operating model around it, rather than layering AI tools onto legacy processes.

**What it means for The Construct: ** The 1.7%-vs-0.8%-of-revenue split is a clean, citable external benchmark for framing agent-ops spend to portfolio companies and family offices — "underinvesting is the real risk" is now a top-three-consultancy argument we can borrow rather than one we have to make unsupported. It also backs the operating-model framing PRAGMA already uses: agent infrastructure as core, not bolt-on.

**Firm:** EY
**Source:** https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2026/07/ey-expands-nvidia-powered-enterprise-ai-capabilities-with-industry-first-validation-of-nvidia-nemoclaw-for-langchain
**Published:** July 29, 2026

**Summary:** EY validated five EY.ai agentic solutions against NVIDIA's "NemoClaw for LangChain Deep Agents" reference blueprint (Nemotron 3 Ultra model + LangChain Deep Agents + NVIDIA OpenShell secure runtime), across supply chain, sustainability, cybersecurity, data engineering, and insurance underwriting use cases. **What it means for The Construct:** Useful proof-point language for agent-ops pitch material — concrete percentage lifts read better than "agents improve accuracy." Caveat: this is EY+NVIDIA co-marketing of a joint reference architecture, not independent research, so treat the specific numbers as directional vendor validation rather than a benchmark to cite as neutral.
