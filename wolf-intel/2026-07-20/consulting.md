## Papers scoring ≥3

### 1. "Agentic AI Will Make the CMO's Role More Consequential" — BCG
**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-agentic-ai-transforms-marketing
**Published:** ~July 16, 2026 (4 days outside strict window — included as the cycle's most relevant hit; see honesty note above)
**Score: 3/5 — useful background**

BCG argues that as AI agents increasingly mediate how customers discover, evaluate, and shortlist vendors — doing the browsing, comparing, and pre-filtering that humans used to do themselves — CMOs have a narrowing window to make agentic AI a growth lever rather than a channel they've lost visibility into. The piece frames concrete shifts: content and brand signals need to be legible to agents (not just to search engines or humans), attribution needs to move to real time, and org design needs a function that owns "how we're represented when an agent is doing the deciding." It sits alongside a cluster of adjacent BCG pieces this month (shared AI vision for boards/CEOs, AI-first enterprise operations) that together read as BCG's marketing arm moving from "agentic AI is coming" framing (2025) to "here's the operating model" framing (2026) — i.e., the industry conversation has shifted from awareness to operationalization.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the enterprise-marketing mirror of Hartley Capital's agent-ops thesis, and it lands closer to home than it first looks — Brand 9's signage and wayfinding RFPs increasingly get pre-filtered by procurement copilots and agent-mediated vendor search before a human ever sees a shortlist, and firms that aren't legible to agents (structured product data, agent-crawlable spec sheets, machine-readable capability claims) risk losing deals they never knew were being decided. Worth a scoping pass on whether Brand 9's web presence and spec documentation are agent-legible, not just SEO-legible.
