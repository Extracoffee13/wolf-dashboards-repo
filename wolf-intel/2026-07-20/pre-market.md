Oil broke $90 Brent intraday for the first time since June 11 before fading on an Iran talks headline. US futures shrugged the whole thing off overnight, which is the tension to watch into the cash open.

## Oil / Geopolitical

- **Brent crude**: +2.4% to $90.25/bbl in Asian trade — first close above $90 since June 11. Later gave back the spike to ~$87.77 (-0.4% on the day) after Iran signaled U.S. talks "could be pursued based on national interests."
- **WTI**: $84.44, +2.36%.
- The US carried out a **ninth consecutive night of strikes on Iran**, targeting military installations, coastal surveillance sites, and communications networks aimed at degrading Iran's ability to threaten shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Washington confirmed additional military casualties over the weekend.
- Iran struck Gulf infrastructure over the weekend — a Kuwaiti power plant, a desalination facility, and an oil site — widening the conflict's economic footprint beyond direct US-Iran exchanges.
- **Level to watch: Brent $90.** A sustained daily close above it reopens $95-100 tail risk and shifts equity desks from a growth narrative to a stagflation one. A give-back to the mid-$80s confirms the talks headline is real de-escalation, not just rhetoric.
