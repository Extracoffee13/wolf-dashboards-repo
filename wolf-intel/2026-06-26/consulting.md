### 1. AI-First Enterprise Operations: Reinventing the Operating System of Work
**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/reinventing-the-operating-system-of-work-with-ai
**Published:** June 15, 2026
**Score: 5 / 5**

**Executive Summary:**
BCG's agentic deployment data reveals a stark bifurcation: organizations that redesign processes end-to-end with agentic AI capture 60%+ cost reductions, while those that bolt AI onto existing task structures achieve less than 20%. The paper argues that "the operating system of work" — how processes are designed, governed, and executed — is the true unit of transformation, not individual tasks or tools. The critical differentiator is outcome-managed agents with agent-driven control flow and orchestration across entire workflows, not point-solution AI augmentation. Organizations running parallel operations (legacy human workflows + AI assist) are structurally capped at marginal gains; the 60% achievers rewired process ownership to agents and restructured human roles around exception handling and judgment.

**What it means for The Construct:**
For Hartley Capital's PE roll-up thesis, this changes the value-creation playbook: the question at acquisition is no longer "how do we add AI to our portfolio company?" but "which processes are we prepared to redesign end-to-end on Day 1?" Roll-ups that don't build for agentic process redesign from the first 100 days are engineering themselves into the <20% improvement bucket — the window for first-mover advantage is 12–18 months before every PE value creation team wakes up to the same finding.

### 2. Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest
**Firm:** Bain & Company
**URL:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/
**Published:** June 8, 2026
**Score: 4 / 5**

**Executive Summary:**
A triple shock arriving in rapid succession — the AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" in software (tech deal value slumped 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026), private credit redemption stress, and an oil spike from the Iran conflict — has stalled PE's second consecutive recovery attempt. The result: 33,000 portfolio companies remain unsold with no near-term exit window. More structurally alarming is Bain's updated returns math: generating a 2.5x return over a 5-year hold now requires ~12% EBITDA growth annually, up from ~5% a decade ago — a function of compressed multiples, higher carry costs, and tighter exit conditions. Bain's prescription is to treat AI as a revenue-generation and workflow transformation tool (not cost-cutting) and to control what's controllable: operational value creation, portfolio resilience, and selective bolt-on M&A at compressed prices.

**What it means for The Construct:**
For Hartley Capital, the exit math has fundamentally shifted — the 12% EBITDA growth hurdle means roll-up targets must be underwritten for genuine operational transformation, not financial engineering alone. The current dislocation (33,000 stuck portfolio companies) also creates a buyer's market for bolt-on acquisitions at distressed multiples if Hartley Capital has dry powder and agentic operating playbooks already built.

### 3. The AI-First Real Estate Company: An Opportunity for Structural Advantage
**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group
**URL:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-ai-first-real-estate-company-advantage
**Published:** April–June 2026 (Executive Perspectives series)
**Score: 4 / 5**

**Executive Summary:**
BCG documents that real estate lags the global cross-industry average on AI maturity by approximately 15%, with AI investment running 0.8% below the 2026 cross-industry average — and the gap is widening. The upside is correspondingly large: development companies (homebuilders, devcos) that deploy AI for construction site management, procurement optimization, and project scheduling can achieve 400–700 basis points of margin uplift while compressing project timelines by ~30%. BCG frames this as a narrow structural window: early adopters reach AI-first before competitors realize the race has started. The report also notes that 66% of real estate projects experience schedule overruns and 39% face cost overruns, nearly all driven by fragmentation and rework that AI orchestration is now mature enough to address at scale.

**What it means for The Construct:**
Brand 9's homebuilder and commercial real estate clients in Florida are almost certainly in the laggard 15% — which is both a risk and an opportunity. Any signage or wayfinding firm that helps homebuilder clients instrument their construction timelines with AI earns a deeper seat at the planning table; those that don't will watch AI-native project management tools absorb the relationship.
