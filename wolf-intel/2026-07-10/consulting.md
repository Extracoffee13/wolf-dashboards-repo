## 1. BCG — Agentic AI Strategy for CIOs and CTOs in 2026
**Firm:** Boston Consulting Group | **Published:** ~2026-07-07 (~3 days old) | **Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/agentic-ai-strategy-cio-cto

**Summary:** BCG's latest CIO/CTO-facing piece draws a hard line: only 5% of companies are genuinely "agent-first" in 2026, while the rest are stuck below 30% agentic adoption, still asking what agentic AI *can* do rather than operationalizing it into workflows. The bifurcation is widening, not narrowing — agent-first leaders are compounding advantage while laggards debate pilots. BCG's diagnosis: success is ~70% people and change management (governance, workflow redesign, trust, org structure) and only ~30% model/algorithm capability. This pairs with BCG's companion piece "Return on AI: How CFOs and CIOs Can Manage the Token Meter" (2026-07-01, https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/managing-ai-token-costs), which frames token spend as a new P&L line item requiring its own unit-economics discipline — relevant to anyone running agent fleets at scale, not just prototyping them.

**What it means for The Construct:** The Construct is already operating inside BCG's 5% cohort — production multi-agent orchestration (WOLF committee, PRAXIS inbox, this pipeline) running daily, not piloting. That gap between leaders and the 95%-still-stuck majority is Hartley Capital's addressable market: portfolio companies and roll-up targets stuck at "piloting" are acquisition and operational-turnaround targets, not just AI-vendor prospects.

**Score: 5** — directly validates and sharpens the core operating thesis (agent-first as a moat, not a feature).

## 2. Bain & Company — Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest
**Firm:** Bain & Company | **Published:** 2026-06-08 | **Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/

**Summary:** Bain's midyear PE report documents three shocks landing in quick succession — the AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" repricing software, redemption stress in private credit, and an oil spike tied to the Iran war. The stock of unrealized PE assets has swollen to roughly 32,000 companies worth $3.8 trillion, with average holding periods at exit stretching to ~7 years (up from 5-6 years in 2010-2021) as sponsors avoid forced sales into a weak exit market. The deal math has hardened: a deal that needed 5% EBITDA growth a decade ago to hit a 2.5x return over 5 years now needs 12% — multiple expansion is dead, operational value creation is the only lever left. GP-led continuation vehicles grew 62% YoY as funds choose to retain control and return capital selectively rather than exit outright.

**What it means for The Construct:** $3.8T trapped in aging vintages with 7-year holds is a widening pool of tired sponsors who will trade control for real operational lift — the 12% EBITDA bar is exactly the kind of gap AI-driven operational improvement can close, which is the wedge for Hartley Capital to buy in as the value-creation partner rather than just another capital source competing on price.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the roll-up-timing thesis (buy from fatigued sponsors, sell operational AI lift as the differentiator).

## 3. PwC — Real Estate and Real Assets: US Deals 2026 Midyear Outlook
**Firm:** PwC (US Deals) | **Published:** early June 2026 | **Source:** https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/library/asset-wealth-management-real-estate-deals-outlook.html

**Summary:** PwC reframes 2026 real estate dealmaking as structural rather than cyclical: capital is rotating toward operationally intensive, infrastructure-adjacent sectors (digital infrastructure, logistics, senior housing, residential-oriented housing) and away from legacy property types facing refinancing walls. US equity REITs are trading at a 16.2% median discount to NAV entering 2026, adding pressure toward consolidation, strategic asset sales, and take-private activity. The valuation lens has shifted: cost of capital used to dominate; now durability of cash flow, technology, and operating capability drive pricing, meaning platforms with AI-enabled operating models command a premium over passive holdings.

**What it means for The Construct:** Signage and wayfinding sit squarely in the "operationally intensive" layer PwC says is being re-rated upward — combined with the REIT NAV discount and consolidation pressure, FL homebuilders and monument-signage-adjacent platforms look primed for roll-up and take-private activity on both the buy and sell side of a Hartley Capital deal.

**Score: 4** — sharpens the Brand 9 thesis (signage-as-infrastructure gets the same re-rating logic as digital infra).
