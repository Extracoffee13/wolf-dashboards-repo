## 1. Family Office Study: New Asset Allocation — Roland Berger
**Source:** https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html
**Published:** July 2026 (survey fielded Dec 2025–Mar 2026, 88 family offices, DACH region)
**Score: 4/5**

**Executive summary:** Roland Berger surveyed 88 family office principals across the DACH region and found a decisive rotation toward control and stability. Private equity — via both fund commitments and direct deals — is now the primary growth vehicle, with 55% of respondents planning to increase fund exposure. Infrastructure ranks as the single top priority (69%), prized for inflation-linked, resilient cash flows, followed by healthcare and — rising fast — artificial intelligence. Geopolitical upheaval has overtaken interest-rate uncertainty as the dominant external risk. Internally, family offices are prioritizing cybersecurity, digitalization, and professionalizing their own back-office structures — i.e., they're building operating muscle, not just writing checks.

**What it means for The Construct:** Family offices are underwriting exactly the profile Hartley Capital should be building toward — direct PE exposure to durable, cash-flow-generating, infrastructure-adjacent assets, run with real operating discipline rather than multiple arbitrage. It also flags a real allocator appetite for AI-native operating models, which is a positioning argument, not just a product one.

## 2. Private Equity Midyear Report 2026: Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest — Bain & Company
**Source:** https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/
**Published:** June 8, 2026
**Score: 4/5**

**Executive summary:** Bain's midyear read is a cold shower on the "recovery" narrative that dominated PE outlooks entering 2026. Three shocks landed in rapid succession in H1: an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" that gutted software valuations, redemption stress in private credit, and an oil-price spike tied to the Iran war — collectively stalling dealmaking, fundraising, and exits just as momentum was building. Bain's prescription: winning firms won't out-time the market, they'll out-execute it — leaning hard into value-creation plans, deploying AI for operational leverage rather than as a slide-deck story, and making fewer, more disciplined bets to build a genuine "right to win" rather than spreading capital thin.

**What it means for The Construct:** This is the timing signal for Hartley Capital's roll-up mandate. Software-adjacent sellers hit by the SaaSpocalypse are exactly the kind of distressed, motivated counterparties a disciplined operator-buyer wants to be shopping in the back half of 2026 — while overleveraged competitors are busy managing their own credit stress instead of sourcing deals.

## 3. CAVA: Canonical Action Verification and Attestation for Runtime Governance of Agentic AI Systems — arXiv (cs.AI)
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13716
**Published:** ~July 15, 2026 (arXiv:2607.13716)
**Score: 4/5**

**Executive summary:** This paper tackles a problem every organization running autonomous multi-agent fleets will hit: once agents act rather than just advise, how do you verify *after the fact* that an agent's action was authorized, correctly scoped, and attributable — without forcing a human into every loop? CAVA proposes a canonical action-attestation layer that records a verifiable, auditable trail of what an agent decided and why it was permitted to act, aimed at runtime governance rather than pre-deployment testing alone. It lands in the same gap Deloitte's own 2026 State of AI survey flagged separately: only 21% of enterprises report a mature governance model for agentic AI even as adoption races ahead of it.

**What it means for The Construct: This is a live operational risk for The Construct itself.** The C-suite agent fleet (WOLF, Keystone, Vector, Prism, Pulse, etc.) is exactly the kind of "agents that act, not just advise" system this paper is warning about — and right now there's no attestation layer logging why any given agent was authorized to take a given action. Worth a scoping look at whether a lightweight version of this pattern belongs in the PRAXIS pipeline before the fleet scales further.
