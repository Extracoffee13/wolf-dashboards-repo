# Spike 2026-10-06: Why does a sign's unit price fall with quantity, and where does the curve flatten?

## First-principles answer (derived before any lookup)
Primitives: a job consumes (a) one-time costs that do not depend on count and (b) per-unit costs that do.
- Fixed F: art/file prep, proofing, machine setup and tooling, one mobilization per site.
- Variable v: material, machine time, hand labor, per-sign install labor.
- Unit cost = F/n + v. This is a hyperbola: steep at low n, flat as n grows.
- Flattening: F/n drops below ~10% of v at n ≈ 10·F/v. A shop with a large F (CNC and paint setup, custom art) keeps falling longer; a shop with a small F (vinyl cut from a ready file) flattens almost at once.
- Second-order effects: (1) Material yield: more pieces nest better on a sheet, cutting waste; this is a step function at sheet boundaries. (2) Learning: repeat labor gets faster per doubling, but with a floor, since the hands-on time is physical. (3) Capacity steps: a batch that fills a machine bed or a truck is cheaper per piece than one that leaves it partly empty.
- Install differs: mobilization is per site, not per sign, so install price falls with signs per site and with site clustering, not with total count.
- Price is not cost: the buyer's price is capped by the next-best alternative (print vs CNC vs outsourced), so discounts will be tiered by those thresholds rather than smooth.
- Implication for B9: quote in tiers keyed to F-recovery points, and sell the sequence (many signs across stages of a community) as one setup.

## Corpus answer
Standard sign-shop practice: setup fee plus per-unit charge, tiered volume discounts, press/setup as a fixed cost spread across more units (see Signs101 quantity-discounting threads, 4over4 quantity-discount help, state sign-shop price lists). Learning-curve theory is not in sign-pricing sources; it comes from general operations management.

## Delta: rediscovered
The F/n + v model, tiered discounts, and the per-site install point all match orthodox practice. The flattening rule (n ≈ 10·F/v) and the point that discount tiers follow competing-process thresholds were stated more explicitly here than in the sources found, but are unverified, so not scored as novel.
