# Spike: What is the correct unit of economic value in commercial signage?

**Date:** 2026-05-01
**Question:** What is the correct unit of economic value in commercial signage — the fabricated artifact, the installation service, or the ongoing attention stream it generates?
**Delta category:** rediscovered
**Confidence:** 0.78

---

## First-Principles Answer

*(Derived before any corpus search.)*

### Primitives

1. **A sign is a communication device.** Its purpose is to transmit a message from a sender (business) to receivers (passersby, tenants, customers). The physical artifact is a delivery mechanism, not an end in itself.

2. **Value exists only at the point of reception.** A sign sitting in a warehouse has zero communication value. A sign in front of a high-traffic intersection has high communication value — purely because of audience exposure.

3. **Capital equipment valuation principle.** Economic theory prices durable capital at the present value of the service stream it produces, not at its fabrication cost. A machine is worth the PV of output it generates; a building is worth the PV of rents; a sign is worth the PV of impressions it delivers.

4. **The attention stream is the product.** Impressions × attention quality × behavioral conversion rate × value per conversion, discounted over useful life. This is structurally identical to how media planners value out-of-home advertising: CPM × audience × duration.

5. **Current industry pricing is mis-anchored.** Most sign fabricators price by: materials + labor markup + installation. This captures only production cost. The media value — the ongoing attention stream — is given to the customer for free, beyond the one-time transaction.

6. **The real estate analogy.** Commercial landlords face the same information asymmetry (they can't perfectly measure how much a tenant will earn from a location), yet they price by *location* ($/sq ft varies enormously by traffic, visibility, and demographics). They solved the problem by using observable proxies for audience value. Sign fabricators haven't applied the same logic to their own product.

### Prediction

- Sign companies operating in high-traffic locations are systematically underpricing.
- The correct pricing model is audience-adjusted: a sign for a 50,000 daily-impression storefront should cost more than the same sign for a 500 daily-impression strip mall — not because the materials differ, but because the media value differs by 100x.
- The gap between fabrication price and media value is the customer surplus sign shops give away on every job.

### Implication for Brand 9 Signs / Construct

A sign shop with real estate fund relationships (Hartley Capital) has an unusual data advantage: it knows both the fabrication cost and the property-level traffic/visibility data. That combination enables audience-adjusted pricing that a standalone sign shop cannot offer.

## Corpus Answer

*(From web search after first-principles derivation.)*

- **OOH advertising and digital signage networks** fully apply CPM-based pricing ($10–$25/CPM indoors, $30–$50 outdoors for digital; billboard pricing is audience-adjusted by traffic count and DMA).
- Physical signage is explicitly framed as a "shrinking cost per impression" asset in industry marketing: one-time production cost spread over years of impressions.
- A 2022 *International Journal of Advertising* study found 27% higher ROI for digital vs. static signage, largely due to audience targeting capability.
- Digital signage ad networks (in-store retail media, waiting room networks) price by audience, dwell time, and demographic quality — not by screen cost.
- **Sign fabrication shops**, however, still use cost-plus pricing tools (shopVOX, SignCraft pricing calculators, Signs.com estimators) — all anchored to materials × markup + labor.

### The Gap

The corpus knows the attention-stream framing — but it has been applied almost exclusively to *media networks* (digital signage ad platforms, OOH billboard operators). It has **not** been systematically applied to **custom sign fabrication shops**, which remain stuck in cost-plus pricing despite producing the same underlying product (durable impression-delivery infrastructure).

## Delta Analysis

**Category: rediscovered**

The first-principles chain arrived independently at the attention-stream framing, which the OOH advertising corpus has held for decades. This validates the reasoning chain.

However, the specific sub-claim — that *custom fabrication shops* should price by audience rather than materials — does not appear in corpus sources. The fabrication industry's pricing guides show no awareness of the media-value gap. This sub-claim is closer to **novel**, but requires more evidence to upgrade.

**The durable insight:** Sign shops are in the impression-delivery business disguised as a manufacturing business. Pricing should reflect where on the value chain the customer's gain actually sits.

## Commentary

The most practically actionable takeaway for Construct / Brand 9 Signs: any sign job where the client's location is publicly known (street address) can be augmented with estimated daily traffic count (from AADT data, Google Maps popularity, or foot traffic providers). That single variable, added to the cost-plus estimate, reveals whether the shop is leaving significant value on the table. Over a portfolio of installs, the pattern would show which client segments are most undercharged.

For Hartley Capital: properties with high signage visibility but low current sign quality represent the highest-ROI improvement per dollar of signage spend — the math works in both directions.
