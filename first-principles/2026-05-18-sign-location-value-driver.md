# Sign Location Value Driver: Traffic, Dwell, or Decision Proximity?

**Date:** 2026-05-18  
**Domain:** Signage industry primitives / Brand 9 Signs operations  
**Delta category:** rediscovered

---

## Question

What is the primary value driver of a commercial sign location: traffic volume, dwell time, or decision proximity?

---

## First-Principles Answer

### Primitive 1: What is a sign for?
A sign is an information artifact designed to change a specific behavior. For commercial signage, the target is one of: enter this business, buy this thing, navigate here, remember this brand. ROI is behavior change per dollar.

### Primitive 2: What must information do to change behavior?
Four gates must open in sequence:
1. **Perception** — the viewer receives the signal
2. **Comprehension** — the viewer decodes the message
3. **Relevance** — the message connects to a currently open need-state
4. **Action-ability** — the viewer can act on it (right place, right time)

All four must open. If any is closed, the impression either produces zero result or stores only as weak brand memory.

### Primitive 3: Which gate is the binding constraint?
Perception and comprehension are design problems — solvable with good sign execution. But relevance and action-ability are *location problems* that design cannot fix.

- **Traffic volume** addresses only gate 1. It tells you how many eyes are present, nothing about whether any viewer is in a relevant need-state or capable of acting.
- **Dwell time** addresses gate 2 — more processing time, better message comprehension. Useful, but comprehension without action-ability still produces only brand storage, not conversion.
- **Decision proximity** addresses gates 3 and 4 simultaneously. A viewer at the entrance of a restaurant is: (a) plausibly in a food need-state (they're in a retail corridor), and (b) capable of acting right now.

Decision proximity is the gate that connects perception to behavior. It is the binding constraint in direct-response commercial signage.

### Thought experiment
Three signs for the same business:
- **Sign A:** Highway billboard — 500,000 daily impressions, 3 miles from location
- **Sign B:** Street monument — 12,000 daily impressions, 50 feet from entrance
- **Sign C:** Illuminated entrance panel — 400 daily impressions, at the door threshold

Which drives the most same-day foot traffic? Almost certainly C → B → A. Decision proximity inverts the traffic-volume ordering entirely.

### The brand vs. direct-response asymmetry
If the goal is long-term brand recall, Sign A may win. But for small commercial operators (retail, restaurants, services) — the core Brand 9 Signs customer — the overwhelming majority of value comes from direct response. The sign must close the loop, not just open awareness.

### Conclusion
**Decision proximity > dwell time > traffic volume** for direct-response commercial signage.

Pricing, placement, and sales strategy should weight proximity to the decision moment above raw impression count.

---

## Corpus Answer

The traditional OOH (out-of-home) industry metric is **Daily Effective Circulation (DEC)** — essentially traffic volume. Until ~2011, DEC was the primary measurement standard: how many vehicles/pedestrians pass the ad per day.

Modern OOH measurement has evolved away from DEC toward:
- **Attribution models** — connecting exposure to store visits, branded search lift, app installs via mobile device ID matching
- **Brand lift studies** — survey-based recall, awareness, consideration, purchase intent
- **Dwell time** — increasingly recognized as a primary cost-allocation variable for DOOH
- **Decision proximity** — the industry now explicitly notes "campaign recall increases when OOH is placed near decision locations"

The corpus is in active transition: the traditional answer is traffic-volume-centric (DEC), but the leading edge of OOH measurement science validates decision proximity as the more meaningful lever.

## Commentary

The gap between DEC (traffic-volume) and decision-proximity thinking reveals a classic measurement trap: industries measure what is easy to count (vehicles per day) rather than what drives the outcome (decision-moment exposure). The industry's movement toward attribution models is essentially rediscovering that proximity to the purchase funnel matters more than raw reach.

For Brand 9 Signs sales: this logic supports premium pricing for entrance panels, POS displays, and tenant-facing interior signage even at low traffic counts — because they sit at the decision threshold.
