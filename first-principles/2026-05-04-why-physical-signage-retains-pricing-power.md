# Spike: Why Does Physical Signage Retain Pricing Power Relative to Digital Media?

**Date:** 2026-05-04
**Category:** rediscovered
**Confidence:** 0.75

---

## Question

Why does physical signage retain pricing power relative to digital media, despite digital having near-zero marginal cost per impression?

---

## First-Principles Answer

### Starting primitives

1. **Advertising value function:** `value = audience_size × attention_quality × conversion_propensity`
2. **Price is set by scarcity, not by production cost.**

### Digital media supply dynamics

- One additional impression costs ~$0 to produce.
- Global ad inventory is effectively infinite.
- Result: CPM races toward marginal cost → commoditization.
- Attention quality degrades: banner blindness, ad blockers, scroll fatigue.
- Conversion propensity weakens as trust in digital formats erodes.

### Physical signage supply dynamics

- A high-traffic location is a **fixed geographic resource**. There is exactly one "corner of Main and 5th."
- Zoning codes and permits cap new inventory — supply is **structurally inelastic**.
- Installation cost + landlord lease creates a capital barrier to entry.
- The underlying resource (prime location) is non-replicable.

### Attention quality of physical signage

- **Forced exposure:** a commuter cannot scroll past a billboard.
- **Repetition at zero incremental cost:** the same audience sees the same sign daily.
- **Spatial relevance:** near-point-of-purchase placement reaches audiences at moment of decision.

### Core inference

Physical signage pricing power is not about the sign itself. It is a derivative of:

**(a) Geographic monopoly on a specific location** — supply cannot expand to meet demand.  
**(b) Forced-attention property of the physical world** — the audience has no opt-out mechanism.

These two properties jointly produce an impression that is *categorically unavailable* in digital markets.

### Corollaries for Construct work

- **Brand 9 Signs:** competing on CPM against digital is a losing frame. The correct frame is that physical signage delivers *location-locked, forced-attention impressions* that have no digital substitute. Premium pricing follows from that distinction.
- **Hartley Capital:** real estate with signage rights or high-visibility facades carries latent advertising value that appreciates as digital CPMs commoditize and advertiser demand for guaranteed attention intensifies.

---

## Corpus Answer

From industry sources (OOH measurement firms, The Drum, Solomon Partners CPM comparison 2025):

- OOH CPMs average **$6.41** vs. $12.20 for other media — OOH CPMs are *lower*, not higher, than digital.
- But ROI is superior: OOH returns **$5.97 per dollar** vs. **$3.00 for PPC** (Keen Decision Systems, Oct 2025).
- Nielsen 2025: OOH delivers better-than-expected brand recall, ad retention, and cross-channel conversion lift.
- Geographic scarcity is confirmed: cities with strict outdoor ad regulations (SF, Paris) show elevated costs and limited supply — confirming the structural inelasticity mechanism.
- DOOH (programmatic digital OOH): CPMs rising to **$4–$50**, expanding OOH market to $20B in 2026, growing at 10–14% CAGR — CPM inflation emerging as programmatic demand meets fixed physical supply.

## Delta Analysis

**Category: rediscovered** (with one novel framing element)

The first-principles reasoning correctly identified the structural mechanisms:
- Geographic monopoly ✓
- Regulatory barriers to supply ✓
- Forced attention / no ad-blocking ✓
- Repetition at zero incremental cost ✓
- Spatial relevance ✓

The corpus confirms all mechanisms. The one correction: my framing assumed "pricing power" would manifest as *higher CPMs*. The corpus reveals OOH CPMs are actually *lower* than digital on average — yet ROI is nearly 2× higher.

This means the pricing power is **latent in the ROI gap, not explicit in the CPM rate**. The market is currently underpricing physical signage relative to the value it delivers. This is visible in:
- OOH CPM: $6.41 → ROI: $5.97/dollar
- Digital CPM: $12.20+ → ROI: $3.00/dollar

**The novel element:** physical signage's geographic monopoly creates durable pricing power that has *not yet been fully converted to CPM premium by the market*. As programmatic DOOH grows and demand meets fixed supply, CPM inflation is beginning ($4–$50 range). This validates the scarcity model and suggests the gap will close — static OOH is systematically underpriced relative to its attention value.

**Practical implication for Brand 9 Signs:** there is an arbitrage opportunity in the current market. Clients buying physical signage are receiving more value than the CPM rate implies. This is a selling point, not a weakness.

## Commentary

The first-principles chain held. The main miss was framing "pricing power" as CPM premium when the market currently expresses it as ROI efficiency. The DOOH CPM inflation trend ($4–$50 range emerging) is direct empirical validation of the scarcity mechanism — as programmatic demand aggregates against fixed physical supply, prices rise. Static OOH remains underpriced in CPM terms but overperforms in ROI terms. The gap is a market inefficiency.
