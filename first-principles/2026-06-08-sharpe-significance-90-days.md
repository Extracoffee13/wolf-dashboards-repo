# Spike: Sharpe Significance Over 90 Days

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Question:** What minimum Sharpe ratio over 90 trading days is statistically distinguishable from pure luck at 95% confidence — and what does this imply about the WOLF Brief's kill criterion of Sharpe < 1.0?

---

## First-Principles Answer

### Primitives

- Sharpe ratio (annualized from daily): `SR_ann = (mean_daily / std_daily) × √252`
- Null hypothesis H₀: true expected excess return = 0 (no edge)
- Goal: reject H₀ at 95% confidence (one-tailed) with T = 90 trading days

### Chain of Reasoning

**Test statistic.** For IID daily returns, testing whether the mean excess return is nonzero:

```
t = mean / (std / √T) = (mean / std) × √T = SR_daily × √T
```

Under H₀ this follows approximately N(0,1) for large T (≥30 is conventional).

**Minimum detectable SR at T = 90.**

```
Reject H₀ when: SR_daily × √90 > z₀.₉₅ = 1.645
→ SR_daily_min = 1.645 / √90 = 1.645 / 9.487 = 0.1734
→ SR_ann_min   = 0.1734 × √252 = 0.1734 × 15.874 ≈ 2.75
```

**What a SR of 1.0 actually means over 90 days.**

Convert back: `SR_daily = 1.0 / √252 = 0.0630`

```
t = 0.0630 × √90 = 0.0630 × 9.487 = 0.598
p-value = P(Z > 0.598) ≈ 0.275
```

A 90-day paper Sharpe of 1.0 has a **27.5% chance of occurring under H₀** — i.e., by pure luck. You cannot reject the null at any conventional significance level.

### Core Insight

90 days is structurally insufficient to distinguish a SR=1.0 strategy from noise at 95% confidence. The minimum *statistically detectable* annualized Sharpe over 90 trading days is approximately **2.75** — a level sustained by essentially no real strategy.

## Corpus Answer

**Lo (2002) — "The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios"** (Financial Analysts Journal):

- Derives the asymptotic distribution of the SR estimator under IID and stationary returns.
- Key result: SE(SR_ann) ≈ √((1 + SR²/2) / T) where T is the number of *annual* periods. For T daily observations, this becomes √(252 × (1 + SR_daily²/2) / T).
- At SR ≈ 0: SE(SR_ann) ≈ √(252/90) = √2.8 ≈ 1.673. Minimum detectable SR (one-tailed, 95%): 1.645 × 1.673 ≈ **2.75**. Identical to first-principles result.

**Bailey & López de Prado — "The Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio" (2012):**

- Extends Lo with bias corrections for non-normal returns (excess kurtosis, skewness).
- Introduces *Minimum Track Record Length* (MTL): the minimum number of observations needed to be 95% confident SR_observed > SR_benchmark.
- For a target SR of 1.0 and sharpe-benchmark of 0, MTL ≈ 5–7 years of daily data (1250–1750 observations), not 90 days.

**Standard practitioner result:** Any strategy with fewer than ~2–4 years of daily data cannot be statistically validated at conventional significance levels unless the realized Sharpe is unrealistically high.

### Commentary

The derivation is clean: the Sharpe ratio test is just a t-test in disguise (`t = SR_daily × √T`), which means the minimum detectable effect size scales as `1/√T`. This is the same statistical power curve as any mean-difference test.

**Operational implication for WOLF Brief:** The kill criterion "Sharpe < 1.0 over 90 days" is an *operational* threshold, not a *statistical* one. That distinction matters:
- As an operational gate: reasonable (magnitude check, not significance check).
- As evidence of edge: insufficient. A skilled strategy with true SR = 2.0 could easily print SR = 0.8 over 90 days through sampling variance alone.
- Honest framing: the 90-day proof establishes rough magnitude (positive or negative), reader engagement, and operational discipline — not statistical proof of alpha. Statistical proof would require ~3–7 years of data.

The corpus doesn't frame this in terms of what a kill criterion implies probabilistically. That framing is the value-add here.
