# Spike: Why Does a Sign Generate Business Value?

**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Question:** What is the primitive mechanism by which a commercial sign generates business value?
**Delta category:** novel

---

## First-Principles Answer

Starting from absolute primitives:

**Primitive 1 — The find problem.**
A business exists to exchange value with customers. Before any exchange, the customer must locate the business. This is the most upstream bottleneck in commerce: the cost of being found.

**Primitive 2 — Attention economics.**
Customer attention is finite and costly to acquire. Every marketing channel competes for the same attention budget. The question is: which channel acquires that attention at the lowest cost per *proximate* impression?

**Primitive 3 — Signs as location-indexed infrastructure.**
A sign is a passive, persistent broadcast device anchored at a physical location. Unlike ads (recurring spend), unlike digital (reaches anyone, intent unknown), a sign reaches people who are already geographically proximate — the highest-intent cohort possible. The install cost is one-time; impressions accumulate for years.

**The mechanism chain:**
`visual capture → pattern recognition → semantic decode → memory encode → action (enter / call / note for later)`

At each step there is drop-off. A sign's design job is to minimize drop-off at each stage: contrast drives visual capture, clarity drives semantic decode, distinctiveness drives memory encode.

**The value formula:**
```
sign value = (daily_eyeballs × relevance_fraction × decode_success × retention_rate
              × conversion_rate × customer_LTV) / annualized_sign_cost
```

**Key insight — leverage multiplier, not standalone channel:**
A sign does not need to close the sale. It only needs to reduce the cost of the *first step* (find). Once a customer can find and recognize the location, every other marketing investment — Google Ads, referrals, social, loyalty — compounds on that foundation. The sign is **location-indexed attention infrastructure**, a force multiplier on all other channels, not a standalone advertising medium.

**Corollary — sign type maps to funnel stage:**
| Sign type | Funnel stage | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Monument / pylon | Discovery | "A business is here" |
| Channel letters / fascia | Recognition | "This is the right brand" |
| Window graphics | Confirmation + lure | "This is the right place, come in" |
| Interior signs | Wayfinding + upsell | Retain + increase basket |
| Vehicle wrap | Broadcast | Reach pre-search audience |

Pricing strategy, design priority, and fabrication investment should follow this mapping. Monument signs justify higher spend because they sit at the highest-leverage funnel position.

**Structural underpricing hypothesis:**
Signs are chronically underpriced relative to their value because buyers anchor to install cost rather than to lifetime impression cost. A $5,000 sign generating 1,000 impressions/day for 10 years delivers 3.65M impressions at $0.0014/impression — orders of magnitude cheaper than any paid channel. Sign shops that surface this math can command higher margins and justify premium fabrication.

## Corpus Answer

Standard industry framing (from trade sources and sign shop marketing):

- Signs are the "silent salesperson" — always working, no wages, no sick days.
- Primary mechanism: one-time cost amortized over years = extremely low cost per impression.
- Passive influence builds familiarity; familiarity builds trust; trust converts to purchase.
- Empirical benchmarks: ~14% average sales lift from effective on-premise signage; 83% recall rates cited for targeted digital displays; $5–6 ROI per $1 invested when attribution is measured.
- Categories of value: foot traffic, brand recall, local presence, wayfinding friction reduction.

The corpus is accurate and actionable at the operational level. The dominant frame is efficiency (cost per impression) rather than structural leverage.

---

## Delta Analysis

**Category: novel**

The corpus converges on two correct claims: (1) signs have low cost per impression, (2) repeated exposure builds trust. Both are in the first-principles derivation.

What the corpus lacks:

1. **Leverage multiplier framing.** The corpus treats signage as one channel among many, compared on CPM basis. The first-principles derivation reveals that signs operate at a categorically different level — they enable all other channels rather than competing with them. A business with no visible sign wastes a fraction of every dollar spent on Google Ads (customer finds the ad, can't find the door).

2. **Funnel-stage decomposition by sign type.** The corpus addresses sign types descriptively (monument vs. channel letter) but doesn't explicitly map them to funnel stages or derive implications for investment priority and design focus from that mapping.

3. **Structural underpricing as a pricing strategy opportunity.** The corpus notes low CPM but doesn't frame it as a sales argument sign shops can use to justify premium fabrication and command higher margins.

**Confidence:** 0.72
