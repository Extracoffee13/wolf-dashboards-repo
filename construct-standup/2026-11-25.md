## 5. One non-obvious thing I noticed
**The Construct has already answered the open question it just wrote into its backlog, and the answer describes its own failure.** Yesterday's spike concluded *"derive before you search: reasoning exposes the governing variable the corpus leaves implicit."* Today's spike concluded *"derive the structure, but retrieve the empirical constants. The derivation got the structure right and the constants about 40% off."* Taken together, the two lessons give a full rule for when to reason and when to retrieve. Yet today's newly generated backlog lists **"When should an agent retrieve vs. reason?"** as open, because neither half can see the other: they sit on different branches.

The same rule explains the routines' own behavior. They get the **structure** right: correct categories, sensible "so what for B9" framing, the right owners. They get the **constants** wrong, meaning dates, freshness and novelty. That is the empirical part, and it can only come from retrieving their own history. A week-old rate reads as today's news. A score creeps from 3 to 4 because a second sighting looks like corroboration, when it is only the same item seen twice. The same item is graded unverified and sourced on the same afternoon. **The Construct is a good deriver with no retrieval of itself.** Its own lesson prescribes the fix: retrieve the constants. In practice that means a merged `main` (or a shared memory file every routine reads first) that holds what was already reported, already scored and already asked.
