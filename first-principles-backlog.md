# First-Principles Backlog

Open questions for the daily First-Principles Spike. Pick one, derive it from
scratch (no retrieval), then check it against the corpus. Add new questions
as they come up; strike them through (don't delete) once spiked, with a
pointer to the artifact that covers them.

## Open

- Why does a sign's letter height scale roughly linearly with viewing
  distance, and what sets the constant of proportionality?
- What determines the "right" markup multiplier a sign shop charges over
  material + labor cost, from unit economics first principles (fixed
  overhead, utilization rate, job variance, cash-conversion cycle) rather
  than "that's what everyone charges"?
- Why do commercial real estate cap rates move opposite to interest rates,
  derived from discounted cash flow mechanics rather than quoted as a rule?
- What is the actual information-theoretic argument for why a multi-agent
  system needs a shared inbox/ledger (like this repo's praxis-inbox.md)
  instead of each agent keeping private state — what specifically is lost
  without it?
- From queueing-theory primitives, how many concurrent fabrication jobs can
  one sign shop bay sustain before backlog grows unboundedly, given a
  measured arrival rate and service time distribution?
