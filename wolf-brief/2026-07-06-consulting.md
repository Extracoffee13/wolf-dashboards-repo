# WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-06

*The dark horse: opinionated synthesis of expensive strategy research, filtered to one corner of the universe nobody else covers.*

The surprise this week isn't in the AI hype — it's in the numbers underneath it. KPMG's latest Global AI Pulse found that 49% of enterprises running AI agents have already scaled deployments *back* because the costs outran the benefits. Deloitte, in a separate survey of 3,235 leaders, found 80% still lack a mature governance model for autonomous agents — even as a meaningful slice of the Fortune-scale crowd expects agents to become a *core operating component* of the business within a year.

Nobody outside a handful of CFO briefing decks has connected these two data points yet. The agent-AI story your feed is telling — "autonomy is here, ship it" — and the agent-AI story CFOs are actually living — "we don't know what this costs and we're already regretting it" — are two different stories. The gap between them is where the money is. It's why most readers haven't seen this yet: it lives in a KPMG pulse survey and a Deloitte enterprise report, not in a headline, and it takes reading both against each other to see the same pattern twice in one week.

**The pattern:** the leaders who *do* have it figured out share one trait — real-time cost-monitoring dashboards, cost reviews baked into the agent-approval process, before they scaled. Those leaders are 5x more likely to report established ROI (15% vs. 3%) than everyone else. Cost governance, not model capability, is now the binding constraint on running agents at scale.

**What it means:** if you're building or backing anything that runs AI agents at scale — a fund, a portfolio-ops layer, a roll-up — cost-governance and audit-trail infrastructure isn't overhead you bolt on after product-market fit. It's the moat. The firms writing checks in 18 months will be underwriting the governance stack, not the model. WOLF's own guardrails — hard daily/weekly loss limits, published Sharpe, a public 90-day kill criterion — are already the minority-case setup both firms say most of the market lacks. That's not a coincidence.

**If I'm wrong:** by 2026-10-04 (90 days out), if the share of enterprises citing "agent cost overruns" as a reason for scaling back has *fallen* rather than risen in KPMG's next Global AI Pulse survey (Q3/Q4 2026 wave), and no major consulting firm has published a dedicated "Agent FinOps" or agent-governance-maturity framework by then, this thesis is wrong — cost and governance infrastructure is a second-order concern, not the moat.
