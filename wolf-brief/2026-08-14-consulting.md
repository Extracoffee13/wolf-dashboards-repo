## The surprise

Deloitte just surveyed 501 senior-manager-to-C-suite executives — every one of them already piloting agentic AI — and found a gap that should reframe how you think about the "AI agents" story: 74% expect nearly half their business processes to be redesigned around agents within four years. 61% expect those agents to be running largely autonomous, humans just supervising.

And yet only **15%** have actually scaled an orchestrated, multi-agent system past the pilot stage. Only **5%** call their processes "highly prepared." Two-thirds of these executives — again, people already committed enough to be piloting agentic AI — are quietly re-evaluating their entire business model, because they've realized this isn't a tooling purchase.

That's not a story about AI hype. It's a story about a 15% club. Almost everyone believes agents will run the business. Almost nobody has actually gotten multi-agent orchestration working at scale. The gap between belief and execution is the whole opportunity right now, and it's wider than the headlines about "agentic AI adoption" suggest — those headlines count pilots, not scaled systems.

*Source: Deloitte Insights, "AI Agents are Only the Beginning," published Aug 12, 2026. [deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html)*

## Why you haven't seen this yet

This ran as a press-release-flavored Deloitte survey drop — the kind of thing that gets syndicated once on a finance wire and then disappears into the churn of "AI adoption survey" stories, of which there are ten a week. Nobody is reading the 15% figure next to what's actually happening on the frontier of multi-agent engineering, which is where the second half of this story lives: arXiv had a paper go up in the same 48-hour window — "Discovering Efficient and Explainable Communication Topologies for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems via Causal Inference" — that's solving the exact operational problem the 85% haven't gotten to yet: once you have more than one or two agents, how do you keep their communication auditable instead of an opaque mesh. That paper will never cross a generalist's desk. It's the kind of signal you only catch if you're already running a multi-agent operation and reading both the survey data and the systems research on the same day.

## If I'm wrong

**Falsifiable prediction, graded 2026-11-12 (90 days out):** the "15% have scaled multi-agent systems" figure becomes a repeated benchmark stat — cited by at least two more major consulting-firm reports (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, PwC, or KPMG) between now and then, the way "shadow AI" usage stats spread through 2024-25 coverage. If nobody picks it up and it dies as a one-off press release, I'm wrong about how central this gap is to the market conversation — and that would mean the "agent ops at scale" wedge is narrower or later than this brief is betting.
