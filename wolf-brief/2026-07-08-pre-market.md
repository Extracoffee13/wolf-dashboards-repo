# WOLF Pre-Market — July 8, 2026

**Trump kills the Iran ceasefire, oil rips 6%, and the Fed picks today to turn hawkish. Futures are down across the board and it's not one story, it's two colliding.**

- **The setup**: US struck Iran overnight after attacks on three ships in the Strait of Hormuz; Trump says the ceasefire is "over." WTI +6.4% to ~$75, Brent +6.3% to ~$78.80. S&P futures -0.8% to -1.4%, Nasdaq 100 futures -1.3% to -2.7% (chips leading down, again), Dow futures off ~440-600 points. Kospi got hit hardest overnight, -5.4%, as the AI/chip unwind (Samsung earnings, DeepSeek building its own chip) piles onto the geopolitical shock.
- **The level**: S&P 500 7,450. Tuesday closed 7,503.85 — a clean 1% gap opens right on this line. Hold it and this reads as a headline-driven fade candidate; lose it on a close and you're back in the low-7,400s support shelf from late June. Watch it at the open, not the print.
- **The wildcard nobody's pricing right**: gold is flat (~$4,161, -0.14%) despite the risk-off tape. That's not normal — it says rising real yields (10Y near 4.50%, a two-week high) are fighting the safe-haven bid to a draw. If gold starts moving with the VIX instead of against yields, that's the tell that this stops being a one-day headline trade.
- **Today's real catalyst isn't Iran, it's 2pm ET**: FOMC minutes from the June meeting drop this afternoon, and the dot plot already leaked the story — 9 of 18 officials now want a hike before year-end, up from zero in March. A hawkish Fed landing the same day as an oil shock kills the "Fed put" narrative that usually cushions geopolitical selloffs. That combination, not the ceasefire headline itself, is what should worry anyone long beta into the close.
- **SpaceX (SPCX) is the tape to watch regardless of macro**: down 17% Monday, another 6% Tuesday on its bumpy Nasdaq-100 debut, over $915B erased from its peak a week ago — bouncing ~1% pre-market. This is trading on its own gravity right now, separate from Iran or the Fed.

**What would change my view**: a de-escalation headline out of the Ankara NATO summit, or FOMC minutes that frame the hawkish dots as a minority view rather than consensus — either one unwinds a big chunk of this move fast, since it's headline-driven, not yet a confirmed supply disruption.
