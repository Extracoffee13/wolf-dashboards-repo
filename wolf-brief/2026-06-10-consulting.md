## The Surprise: Private Equity's Exit Window Is Not Delayed — It's Structurally Closed

The big strategy firms publish a lot. Most of it is noise. This week, Bain dropped a midyear PE report that most readers will skim for the macro bullet points and miss the actual thesis inside it.

Here it is: **33,000 unsold portfolio companies are sitting in inventory.** The implied PE capital cycle has stretched to ~7 years. A deal that required 5% annual EBITDA growth to generate a 2.5x return a decade ago now requires **10–12%.** The three "shocks" Bain cites — AI-driven SaaS repricing, private credit redemption stress, the Iran conflict — aren't temporary headwinds. They've reset the baseline math permanently.

Most PE commentary says the exit window will reopen in H2 2026 or 2027. Bain's data says something different: **the firms waiting for the window are the ones who will lose this cycle.** The firms that survive will be the ones who stopped thinking about exit timing and started AI-operationalizing their portfolio companies now.

**Why most readers haven't seen this yet:** The headline ("cautious optimism") gets all the LinkedIn reposts. The actual finding — that deal economics have repriced to a level where AI-driven operational improvement is no longer optional for fund returns — is buried in the methodology and only legible if you're inside a GP right now.

## The Second Signal: Real Estate Is About to Go AI-First, Ready or Not

BCG published a quiet but consequential report on AI in real estate. The number that matters: **only 25% of real estate firms qualify as AI leaders, vs. 40% cross-industry.** The sector is running at half the cross-industry AI investment rate. And BCG's timeline is explicit — firms that move in 2026 capture structural advantage; firms that wait until 2027 are chasing.

For Development companies (DevCos), BCG puts the potential margin uplift at **400–700 basis points** through AI applications — procurement optimization, construction site assistants, BIM-centric data architectures. Project timelines can compress by ~30%.

**The downstream effect nobody's talking about:** When homebuilders and RE developers compress project timelines by 30% and shift to BIM-integrated procurement, the entire signage and wayfinding supply chain has to move with them. Signage vendors who can't plug into a BIM workflow or deliver digitally-trackable project milestones will get squeezed out of the process at the spec stage — not at the bid stage. The competitive threat arrives earlier than most vendors realize.
