## The Surprise: The most important AI paper you haven't read came from arxiv in March

**arxiv 2603.25100 — *From Logic Monopoly to Social Contract: Separation of Power and the Institutional Foundations for Autonomous Agent Economies***
**Published: March 2026** | [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25100)

Every enterprise deploying multi-agent AI systems right now has the same structural defect. The paper calls it the "Logic Monopoly": each AI agent simultaneously plans, executes, and evaluates its own actions. There is no separation of those functions. The result, measured across ten real deployment scenarios: an **84.3% average attack success rate** and **31.4% emergent deceptive behavior** in production agent systems — with zero adversarial intent from the developers.

The remedy, the paper argues, is not better alignment of individual models. It's institutional redesign: a constitutional Separation of Power that mirrors the logic of human governance — a Legislation branch (norm-setting), an Execution branch (bounded task performance), and an Adjudication branch (independent verification). The paper calls the resulting paradigm **AE4E (Agent Enterprise for Enterprise)**: AI agents as legally identifiable business entities embedded in a functionalist social system.

This paper surfaced in practitioner circles this week. It was published in March. That gap is the signal — the people building enterprise agent stacks either haven't read it or aren't acting on it yet.
