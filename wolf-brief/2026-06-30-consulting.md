### The Surprise: Private Equity Is Not in a Crisis — It's Stuck

Most people in PE circles are waiting for the "recovery" to resume. Bain's midyear data says stop waiting: **the market is not broken, it is structurally repriced** — and almost no one has updated their return math to reflect it.

**Bain & Company — Private Equity Midyear Report 2026**
*June 8, 2026 | https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/*

The headline is the "12 is the new 5" framework. A decade ago, a deal needed ~5% annual EBITDA growth to return 2.5x over five years. Today, with purchase multiples and financing costs at record combined highs, that same deal needs **12% annual EBITDA growth**. The math didn't change. The entry point did.

H1 2026 added three simultaneous shocks: the AI "SaaSpocalypse" (LLM-native competitors cratered SaaS multiples overnight, taking $1B+ tech deals from 15 to 4 in a single quarter), redemption stress in private credit, and the Iran war oil spike. Buyout distributions have been at record lows for **four consecutive years** — the implied capital cycle is now nearly 7 years.

The firms winning in this environment are not finding better deals. They are running better businesses in their existing portfolio, specifically via AI-enabled operational transformation. The edge is no longer financial engineering; it is operational depth.

**What this means for Hartley Capital:** Every roll-up thesis needs a gut-check on EBITDA velocity. If the underlying business can't compound double-digits operationally (not through multiple expansion), the return math doesn't close in today's environment. Cash-generative, non-cyclical services businesses — signage, wayfinding, infrastructure-adjacent — are structurally better fits than discretionary software or consumer plays in a frozen exit market.

### The Runner-Up: Real Estate Is Massively Under-Invested in AI — and the Window Is Narrow

**BCG — "The AI-First Real Estate Company: An Opportunity for Structural Advantage"**
*June 2026 | https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-ai-first-real-estate-company-advantage*

BCG's data: 66% of real estate projects overrun schedule. 39% overrun budget. Real estate firms plan to spend **less than 1% of revenues on AI** — far behind tech or financial services. Meanwhile, AI-first development companies can compress timelines by 30% and capture 400–700 basis points of margin.

Most readers won't see this because they assume real estate is a lagging adopter by nature. The paper argues the opposite: the fragmentation and rework problem that plagues RE development is *exactly* the kind of messy, high-frequency, coordination-heavy workflow where agentic AI compounds fastest. The first-mover advantage is real and time-bounded.

**What this means for Brand 9:** Homebuilder and commercial RE clients are already living the inefficiency BCG describes — and they are not yet buying AI to fix it. A signage partner who speaks the language of BIM integration, procurement optimization, and AI-compressed development timelines is not a vendor; they are a strategic partner. This paper is a sales conversation, not a research artifact.
