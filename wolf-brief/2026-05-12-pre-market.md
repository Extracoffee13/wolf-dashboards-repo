# WOLF Pre-Market Brief — May 12, 2026

**HEADLINE: STAGFLATION TRAP SPRINGS — CPI 3.8% HOT, IRAN DEAL DEAD, OIL $104**

---

1. **April CPI just printed 3.8% YoY (est 3.7%, prior 3.3%) — this is the hottest read since May 2023, driven entirely by the Iran war oil shock: energy +17.9%, gasoline +28.4%.** Core came in 2.8%, above the 2.7% estimate. The Fed cannot cut. Goldman has recession odds at 30%.

2. **Trump called the Iran ceasefire "on life support" Monday night after rejecting Tehran's counter-proposal as "totally unacceptable" — Brent settled at $104.21 (+2.9%), WTI $98.07. Hormuz remains restricted. Europe opened -1.2% with UK bank stocks (Natwest -4.6%, Lloyds -4.1%) leading the selloff — credit markets are pricing stagflation, not just an equity multiple squeeze.**

3. **The level that matters: SPX 7,350.** SPX closed at an ATH of 7,412 yesterday. Hot CPI + no-cut repricing should test 7,350 on the open. A clean break of 7,350 confirms distribution from the ATH and opens a move toward 7,230 (prior breakout). Fade any rip in rate-sensitive sectors (XLRE, XLU, IWM).

4. **DHI is the bright spot: +8.2% pre-market to ~$165.92 after Q2 beat ($7.56B rev, EPS $2.24 vs $2.13 est) with orders +11% and raised guidance. The homebuilder demand signal is real — rate buydowns are working. But the hot CPI print today puts mortgage rate relief further away, so the gap may be a sell-the-news event for the sector by mid-session.**

5. **KOSPI -2.29% is the tell from Asia. Korea is a large oil importer — the Hormuz closure is a direct economic gut-punch for them. When Korea bleeds this hard while Japan only dips, global cyclicals follow the KOSPI lower, not the Nikkei higher. Position accordingly.**

---

**Watch:** SPX 7,350 — if it holds post-CPI, bulls may defend ATH. If it breaks, the six-week run is over and energy/defense is the only place to hide.

**What would change my view:** A credible Iran ceasefire announcement. Oil would drop 10-15% in minutes, CPI path reverses, and rate-cut bets come screaming back. Until that headline crosses, the stagflation trade stays on.
