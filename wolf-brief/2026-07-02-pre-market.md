# WOLF Pre-Market Brief — July 2, 2026

**Headline: Jobs report walks into a market already bleeding — KOSPI just had its worst day in years, and semis are the fault line.**

- The June jobs report (8:30am ET, released early for the holiday) lands on top of a semiconductor rout that took Korea's KOSPI down **7.89%** overnight (Samsung -8%, SK Hynix -12%+). This isn't a US story anymore — it's a global memory-chip unwind, and MU/AMD/INTC (already down 10.6%/6.9%/9% Wednesday) are the direct read-through at the open.
- **Watch Nasdaq 100 vs. Wednesday's close** — two down days running on chips; a third confirms this is a real de-rating, not noise. Dow 52,350 is the pivot on the other side of the tape.
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh went hawkish at Sintra Wednesday — "prices are too high," no hint on July 29, rejected forward guidance outright. 10yr at 4.48%, dollar at a 40-year high vs. yen. **This is a genuine regime tell**: a new Fed chair choosing inflation-fighting credibility over market comfort in his first global speech.
- My read: a soft jobs print (consensus is scattered 70k-172k, converging near 110k) does NOT rescue this tape — it just adds "growth scare" on top of "chip bubble unwind." A hot print is worse for semis (no cut) and better for the dollar. There's no clean bullish outcome here today.
- Homebuilders (LEN, KBH) already reported in June and both showed real margin compression (KBH revenue -27%) — no builder earnings today, but don't buy strength in the group as anything but a relief bounce against a weak fundamental base.

**What would change my view:** a jobs print inside consensus (100-130k, unemployment steady at 4.3%) with semis stabilizing intraday — if MU/AMD/INTC hold Wednesday's lows rather than gapping further down, the Korea move was a localized bubble-pop, not the start of a broader AI-capex de-rating.
