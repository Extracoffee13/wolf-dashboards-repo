# The dark horse, 2026-08-19: the strategy firms went quiet, so a UCL lab told us more

Yesterday and today, ten strategy and advisory firms — McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, KPMG, EY, PwC, Strategy&, Oliver Wyman, Roland Berger — published nothing new that clears the bar on AI agents, PE roll-ups, or real estate. Not "nothing good." Nothing, period, in the last 48 hours, across all ten publication indexes. That absence is itself the finding: on the specific question of what multi-agent AI systems actually cost to run at scale, the people billing $500-2,000/hour for strategy decks are currently 6-9 days behind, and on the coordination-cost question specifically, they haven't said anything at all.

Meanwhile a two-person team out of UCL quietly dropped the first real telemetry on the question: [**"When Agents Coordinate: Measuring Coordination in Multi-Agent AI Coding"**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16801) (arXiv 2608.16801, Destefanis & Aste, 2026-08-17). They modeled 1,902 real multi-agent coding runs as temporal networks — agents and files as nodes, every message/read/write as a timestamped, costed edge — and measured how coordination overhead actually grows as you scale team size and change team structure. Not a survey. Not a thought-experiment. Actual telemetry from actual runs.

**The takeaway:** adding agents has a real, measurable tax, and that tax is driven more by *who's allowed to talk to whom and touch what* than by headcount alone. That's the opposite of the implicit assumption in most "just add more agents" scaling talk — including some of our own. It's a point in favor of keeping multi-agent architectures narrow and role-separated (which is how we've been running Buzz and the WOLF fleet) rather than defaulting to breadth.

**Why you haven't seen this:** it's an arXiv preprint tagged cs.MA/cs.SE, not a "10 Ways Agentic AI Will Transform Your Enterprise" LinkedIn post. Nobody in general business media covers coordination-overhead telemetry papers, and the consulting firms — who do reliably repackage this kind of finding a few weeks later into a named framework — haven't caught up to this specific paper yet. That lag is the whole opportunity: reading the primary research now, before it becomes a slide deck.

**If I'm wrong:** by 2026-11-17 (90 days out), no McKinsey, BCG, Bain, or Big Four report will have adopted "coordination overhead" or an equivalent named framework for multi-agent AI deployment cost. If that 90-day window passes with silence, the "academia leads, consulting decks follow within a quarter" pattern I'm betting on here didn't hold, and this dark horse call was wrong.
