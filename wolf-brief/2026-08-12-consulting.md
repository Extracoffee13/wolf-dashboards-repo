# The WOLF Brief — Consulting Pulse, 2026-08-12

**The dark horse:** every agent-AI vendor pitch you're hearing right now is selling the wrong 20%.

McKinsey published a piece on August 7th called ["How to close the agentic adoption gap."](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/how-to-close-the-agentic-adoption-gap) It's filed under *People and Organizational Performance* — not their AI hub, not Strategy, not Digital. That's almost certainly why you haven't seen it: nobody's "McKinsey AI" alert is keyed to their HR vertical. But it contains the single sharpest number we've seen all quarter.

McKinsey calls it the **1:3:5 ratio**. For every $1 an organization spends on the agent technology itself, the deployments that actually stick spend roughly $3 redesigning the processes the agent touches, and $5 on capability-building and change management to get humans to trust and use the thing. Spend disproportionately on the "1" — the tech layer — and you get a demo, not a deployment.

Pair that with an Oliver Wyman/NYSE survey from July: 49% of PE-backed CEOs now name falling behind on AI deployment as a top-three threat to their business — materially higher anxiety than public-company peers, because a hold-period clock and a board that wants a transformation story before exit is a much sharper form of pressure than public-market patience.

**Put those two together and you get the actual trade:** the buying urgency for agent AI is real and it's coming from PE sponsors on a clock, but the vendors racing to win that budget by leading with model capability and platform features are optimizing for the 20% of spend that doesn't determine success. The 80% — process redesign and adoption — is unsexy, unautomatable-sounding, and currently unpriced by almost everyone selling into this wave.

**What it means for us:** that's the whole PRAGMA wedge, restated by McKinsey with a number attached. We should stop pricing and pitching the agent stack as the product and start pricing the 3:5 — the process redesign and adoption work — as the actual deliverable, especially into PE operating partners who need a fast, credible story before their next board meeting.

**If I'm wrong:** by November 10, 2026 (90 days out), we should be able to point to at least one major agent-ops vendor, or a follow-up strategy-firm report, that has visibly repackaged its offer around change-management and process-redesign bundling rather than raw platform/model capability — evidence the market is starting to price the 1:3:5 ratio the way McKinsey describes it. If instead the market over that window keeps rewarding pure-technology agent vendors on model capability alone, with adoption and process work still treated as an afterthought nobody pays for separately, this thesis is wrong and we should reprice accordingly.

*Sources: [McKinsey, "How to close the agentic adoption gap," Aug 7 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/how-to-close-the-agentic-adoption-gap); [Oliver Wyman/NYSE, "The private equity agenda in the age of AI," Jul 2026](https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2026/jul/how-private-equity-ceo-outpace-public-companies-with-ai.html).*
