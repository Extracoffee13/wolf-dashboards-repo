# WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-21

*The dark horse: a daily synthesis of the strategy-firm white papers your CIO doesn't have time to read, filtered to one corner of the universe nobody else covers.*

## The surprise

Nobody at KPMG or Oliver Wyman is writing about signage companies or homebuilders. That's not an oversight you should shrug off — it's the finding.

This week both firms published research converging on the same structural point from opposite ends of the economy. KPMG, writing about the enterprise AI decade broadly: the winners won't be whoever adopted AI first, they'll be whoever **reorganized around it** first — rebuilt the operating model, not just bolted on a tool. Oliver Wyman Forum, writing specifically about transportation, logistics, and defense: only **8% of CEOs** in those old-line, capital-intensive sectors report using AI at scale — and that thin sliver is already compounding an advantage the other 92% can't see coming until it's a different kind of competitor sitting across the table.

Put those two findings together and point them at a monument-sign shop in a Florida trade area, or a mid-market homebuilder running on spreadsheets and tribal knowledge: the same 8%-scale gap almost certainly exists, unmeasured, because nobody's paying a strategy firm to go look. The research exists for logistics and defense because those sectors are big enough to commission it. Signage and homebuilding aren't — which means the wedge is real and currently invisible to anyone without their own research function pointed at it.

## Why you haven't seen this

Because strategy-firm research follows client budgets, and monument signage doesn't have a Fortune 500 procurement office. The insight is a straightforward transplant of a well-funded finding (industrial AI adoption gaps) onto an underfunded vertical (regional signage/homebuilding) — obvious once stated, invisible until someone actually reads both the KPMG org-design paper and the Oliver Wyman sector study on the same day and holds them next to each other. That's the whole job of this brief.

## If I'm wrong

**Falsifiable prediction, gradable 2026-10-19 (90 days out):** if this thesis is right, at least one of the next three strategy-firm "AI adoption gap" studies covering a traditionally slow-moving, regionally fragmented physical-goods or physical-services vertical (construction, signage, specialty manufacturing, home services) will report an adoption-at-scale number in the single digits — echoing the Oliver Wyman 8% TLD figure rather than the broader-economy 20-40% numbers McKinsey and BCG report for large enterprises generally. If instead the next three sector-specific studies in that category show double-digit-plus adoption already converging toward the broader-economy average, the "invisible wedge" framing is wrong — the gap closes faster in fragmented physical industries than in capital-intensive ones, and the strategic window is smaller than this brief claims.
