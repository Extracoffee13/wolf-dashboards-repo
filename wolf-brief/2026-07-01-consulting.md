# The Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-01

**The dark horse: the agentic-AI adoption curve just published isn't from a consulting firm at all — it's OpenAI grading its own homework, and the number should worry anyone still pricing "agent ops" as a 2027 problem.**

A paper posted to arXiv on June 29 by two OpenAI researchers (Johnston & Holtz, [2606.26959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26959)) tracks Codex usage across the company's enterprise base through H1 2026. Active users are up 5x since January. That's expected — every AI product line claims hockey-stick growth. The number that isn't priced in: usage among *non-developer* employees at adopting organizations is up **189x** since August 2025. Codex now produces over 85% of the average OpenAI employee's output tokens. Translation — the group driving the growth curve isn't engineers automating engineering, it's everyone else automating everything else, and it's happening an order of magnitude faster than the headline adoption number suggests.

Pair that with Bain's M&A Midyear Report (also June 29): global deal value is up 41% YoY, and Bain's own framing is a "winner's paradox" — acquirers now have to fund AI transformation *and* run aggressive dealmaking at the same time, because sequencing them (buy first, digitize later) is starting to lose to firms doing both at once.

**Why you haven't seen this:** it's not that the data is secret — it's that it's split across a primary-source arXiv paper nobody outside ML research reads and a Bain client report nobody outside PE reads, and neither firm has an incentive to connect them for you. The consulting industry's own publication infrastructure made this harder to find than it should be: McKinsey, BCG, PwC, Strategy&, and Oliver Wyman all blocked automated access to their own "insights" pages this week. The firms whose entire business model is publishing thought leadership are increasingly unreadable by anything except a human clicking around in a browser — which means the actual frontier data is moving to arXiv and primary research faster than the consulting synthesis layer can track it.

**If I'm wrong:** grade this by 2026-09-29. My prediction is that non-developer agentic-AI adoption growth *decelerates* sharply from the 189x pace by Q3-end — not because the technology plateaus, but because governance catches up to usage (Deloitte's own H1 survey data put mature agent governance at only ~21% of orgs claiming agent strategies). If instead adoption keeps compounding at anything close to this rate with no visible governance drag, the "agent ops is the moat" thesis is stronger than I'm currently pricing it, and Hartley Capital's timeline for building that capability into portfolio companies should move up, not stay flat.
