## The Surprise

A Harvard Business School professor co-authored a paper with a Perplexity AI engineer — and the data is brutal in the best way.

**"How AI Agents Reshape Knowledge Work: Autonomy, Efficiency, and Scope"** (arXiv:2606.07489, June 2026) is the first study to measure, from live production data at scale, what autonomous AI agents actually do to professional output. The headline numbers get all the attention: 87% reduction in task time, 94% reduction in cost vs. search-augmented humans. Fine. But that's not the finding that matters.

The finding that matters is **scope expansion.**

AI agents don't just accelerate the work people were already attempting. They fundamentally change *what people attempt at all.* Computer (the agent tool) users were found to tackle cross-occupational tasks, higher-order cognitive work, and composite multi-step projects that Search users never initiated. The agent doesn't do your existing job faster. It unlocks work categories your search-equipped colleagues aren't even competing in.

This is not a productivity story. It's a competitive moat story.

And yet it's the most strategically consequential piece of AI research published in the last 30 days for anyone running a firm-building or investing-in-firms operation.

## What It Connects To

Bain's PE Midyear Report 2026 (June 8) tells you the PE market is in a triple-shock freeze — software valuations down, private credit stressed, oil spike. The required EBITDA growth to hit a 2.5x return has doubled from 5% to 10–12%. Winning PE firms are being told to lean into AI-enabled value creation or lose their right to win.

Those two papers read together produce one thesis: **the new PE value creation playbook is agent-first, not just AI-assisted.** The firms that install autonomous agent operating models into their portfolio companies — not just ChatGPT licenses — are the ones who can actually clear the 10–12% EBITDA bar. Everyone else is running on the old playbook with the new hurdle rate.

BCG's **"The AI-First Real Estate Company"** (June 2026) adds the sector-specific layer: AI-first DevCos are capturing 400–700 basis points of margin uplift now, and the industry lags AI maturity by 15%. The arbitrage window for early movers in real estate AI is open but closing.

## The Sharp Takeaway

The question is no longer "should we use AI?" The question is "do we have the operating model to unlock agent scope?" Firms that answer yes gain access to work categories their competitors aren't even attempting. The arxiv paper proves it empirically. Bain's midyear quantifies why the stakes are high. BCG marks the clock on the window.
