# WOLF Brief — June 24, 2026
## The PE Firm That Bet on AI Just Got Hit By It

---

**The surprise nobody's talking about:**

The AI disruption thesis that PE firms sold to their LPs for three years just became their biggest portfolio headwind. Bain's 2026 PE Midyear Report calls it the "SaaSpocalypse" — AI is compressing the pricing power and retention economics of software companies fast enough that PE portfolios built on software roll-ups are now underwater from two directions at once: the AI that was supposed to fuel exits is the same force degrading the underlying business models.

Tech deal value dropped **70%** from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026. Software valuations in PE portfolios declined ~8% through March. And 33,000 unsold portfolio companies are stuck — with an implied capital cycle now at **7 years**, well past the 4–5 year historical norm.

Bain's prescription is to focus on value creation, not market timing. But that's easier said when the value creation thesis — "we'll deploy AI into our portfolio" — runs headfirst into the EY finding published three weeks earlier: orchestrated agentic AI systems cost **$1.20/interaction** in 2026, up from $0.04 in 2023. A 30x increase. The ROI model most PE ops teams built for AI transformation was written with pre-agentic cost assumptions.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:**

The Bain report got covered as a macro PE story — Iran war, oil prices, credit stress. The SaaSpocalypse angle was buried in the tech section. Nobody connected it to the EY cost paper published three weeks prior. The thesis that matters isn't "PE is in a tough cycle." It's that the two largest AI-related documents published in the last 30 days from major strategy firms, read together, tell PE operators that their agent AI strategy and their portfolio recovery strategy have colliding assumptions.
