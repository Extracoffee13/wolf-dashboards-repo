# The Consulting Pulse — 2026-08-10

**Suggested title:** Everyone's Running the Same Agent, and Nobody's Watching the Correlation
**Suggested subtitle:** A finance paper nobody read this week says 88% of shops deploying AI agents have no governance layer — and models the correlated blowup that creates.

---

Here's the surprise: while the big-name consultancies spent this week publishing change-management decks about "closing the agentic adoption gap," an academic paper quietly dropped on arXiv with an actual number attached to the risk everyone's ignoring. **88% of finance professionals have no operational governance framework for agentic AI.** Only 24 of 75 large U.S. money managers disclose a formal AI-use policy in their own regulatory filings. And the paper's model shows that as more shops converge on similar LLM-driven trading signals — which is exactly what's happening as everyone buys the same three or four agent platforms — the probability of a *joint* drawdown across managers climbs from 39.2% to 79.3%.

That's not "AI trading is risky." That's "AI trading is quietly becoming correlated risk," which is a different and much scarier sentence, because correlated risk is the thing that turns a bad Tuesday into a systemic event.

**Why you haven't seen this:** it's an arXiv econ.GN preprint, not a McKinsey deck with a PR team behind it. The strategy firms that publish loudly this week were all writing about agent AI in the abstract — adoption gaps, cost overruns (KPMG's own survey found 49% of enterprises already pulling back on agent spend because token costs blew past ROI, itself getting fresh press pickup this week for a report that's seven weeks old). Nobody in the loud tier connected "everyone's deploying the same agents" to "everyone's exposure is now correlated." The people who did the math work in econ departments, not communications departments.

**What it means for The Construct:** if we or any client is running an agent-AI-driven strategy inside Hartley Capital's orbit, we're inside that 88% until we can prove otherwise with an actual drift-detection layer, not a policy document. The fix here isn't more agents — it's an audit trail that can tell you when your signal has started rhyming with everyone else's.

---

**If I'm wrong:** by 2026-11-08 (90 days out), if either (a) Form ADV-style disclosure of formal agentic-AI governance frameworks rises above 50% of large managers (up from the paper's 24/75 ≈ 32%), or (b) realized cross-manager return correlation among AI-driven strategies is measurably *lower* than in 2025 rather than higher — then this thesis is dead. The market self-corrected on governance without needing a forcing event, and the correlated-blowup risk this paper models never showed up in the data. We'll grade it then.
