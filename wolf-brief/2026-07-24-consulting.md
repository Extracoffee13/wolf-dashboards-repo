# WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-24

*The dark horse: opinionated synthesis of expensive research filtered to one corner of the universe nobody else covers.*

## The surprise

Nobody at Bain, BCG, KPMG, Roland Berger, or Deloitte would tell you they're saying the same thing this cycle — they're rival firms selling rival frameworks. But read all five together and the overlap is too clean to be coincidence: **every one of them independently published, within weeks of each other, some version of "AI agent deployment is now outrunning the governance and cost-visibility needed to prove it's actually worth anything."**

Bain's PE Midyear Report draws the sharpest version of it: winning PE firms in 2026 aren't the ones with the biggest AI budget, they're the ones who've closed the gap between *talking about* AI and *booking it to EBITDA*. BCG puts a number on the gap — firms that redesigned their operating model around agents saw ~60% cost reductions; firms that just automated existing steps saw under 20%. KPMG's Q2 survey of asset managers and PE shops found only 26–36% of firms that already have agents in production can even see their own agent spend in real time. Roland Berger calls it "profitless prosperity in AI" — the same finding from a fourth firm, in different language.

## What it means for The Construct

This is a live warning shot for Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis: agent adoption inside a portfolio company is worthless as a diligence talking point unless it's already showing up in the P&L, and most competing PE shops right now cannot even measure their own agent spend well enough to know if it is. That's the wedge — build the cost-governance layer *before* scaling the agent fleet across portfolio companies, not after, and use "can you see your own agent spend in real time" as a diligence question on every signage/homebuilder acquisition target going forward.

## Why you haven't seen this take

Each firm's report reads, on its own, like generic 2026 AI-hype boilerplate — "agents are important, governance matters, more redesign needed." Nobody's publication department is going to cross-cite a competitor to point out the pattern. You only see the convergence if you read all five in the same week and aren't selling any of them.

## If I'm wrong

**Prediction, gradeable 2026-10-22 (90 days out):** by then, at least one of the major PE-focused surveys (Bain, KPMG, or a comparable Q3/Q4 2026 release) will show the real-time agent-cost-visibility number *still* under 40% across respondents — i.e., the governance gap won't have closed materially in one quarter, because it's a structural lag, not a temporary one. If that number instead jumps meaningfully above 50%, this thesis is wrong and the gap is closing faster than the convergent warning implied.
