# WOLF Brief — June 17, 2026

**The dark horse: what the expensive strategy research actually says this week**

---

## The Surprise

Bain just quietly declared that private equity's second consecutive recovery attempt is dead — killed by three shocks hitting simultaneously — and buried the lede: **the math of a PE deal now requires roughly 10–12% annual EBITDA growth to generate the same return a 5% growth deal produced a decade ago.** Nobody in LP land has updated their mental model yet.

---

## Paper 1 — The PE Thesis Has a New Math Problem

**Bain & Company: "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest — Private Equity Midyear 2026"**  
Published June 8, 2026 | https://www.bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026/

Bain landed three simultaneous shocks on the PE market in H1 2026: the AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" deflated software multiples just as buyers had gotten comfortable with SaaS-forward roll-up strategies; redemption stress in private credit (Blue Owl gated withdrawals in February) froze deal financing; and the Iran conflict spiked oil and reset risk appetite globally. The outcome: deal value collapsed 70% from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026. Thirty-three thousand unsold portfolio companies are now sitting in limbo with holding periods stretching past conventional fund cycles.

Bain's central reframe — **"12 is the new 5"** — is the thesis-changer. A PE deal that needed only 5% annual EBITDA growth to hit a 2.5x return a decade ago now needs 10–12%, because multiple-expansion tailwinds are gone and debt costs are structurally higher. The winners in this environment are firms that have built genuine AI-driven operational improvement capabilities inside their portfolio companies — not firms that deployed AI tools and called it transformation.

**Why most readers haven't seen this yet:** The PE press covered the headline ("recovery stalls again") and missed the underlying math shift. "12 is the new 5" recalibrates every valuation model, every fundraising pitch, every entry multiple conversation. It's not a headline — it's a new underwriting constant.

**What it means for The Construct:** Hartley Capital's roll-up thesis needs to be underwritten against the 12% EBITDA growth bar, not the legacy 5% bar. Any target acquisition that can't credibly show agent AI-driven cost compression or revenue velocity to hit that number is overpriced at current ask. The good news: it also means the competition for deals requiring genuine operational AI capability is temporarily thin — most PE firms are still in "deploy AI tools" mode, not "reshape operations with AI agents" mode.

## Paper 2 — The Hidden Tax on Agent AI Nobody Is Budgeting

**EY: "Agentic AI Enterprise Token Cost"**  
Published June 1, 2026 | https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/ai/agentic-ai-token-costs

EY's most important AI paper of the year is not getting the attention it deserves. The headline is that a simple AI interaction that cost $0.04 in 2023 costs $1.20 in an agentic workflow today — a 30x increase — but the more important finding is that **token cost is still only the visible fraction of total agentic cost.** EY identifies six cost buckets most enterprises aren't tracking: governance and audit overhead, organizational change cost, failure and hallucination recovery, regulatory risk provisioning ("AI taxes"), orchestration infrastructure, and Agent FinOps tooling itself.

The implication: enterprise agentic AI spend is far larger than IT budgets reflect, which means most "AI ROI" numbers in boardroom decks are dramatically overstated. EY's recommendation is to treat agentic AI as a new variable operating cost category and build Agent FinOps discipline — cost monitoring, usage governance, runbook automation — before scaling agent systems, not after.

**Why most readers haven't seen this yet:** CFOs are getting sold on token prices. The orchestration tax, the governance burden, and the failure recovery costs are invisible until they aren't. EY is the first of the Big Four to put a cost framework around the full picture.

**What it means for The Construct:** For Hartley Capital building agent AI into fund operations and portfolio monitoring: budget for the hidden six, not just the API bill. The 30x per-interaction number is real — it's also the reason to be selective about what actually needs agentic orchestration versus simpler automation. Getting this cost model right before scaling is the difference between AI as competitive edge and AI as budget leak.

## If I'm Wrong

**Prediction (grade in 90 days — September 17, 2026):** PE deal volume rebounds meaningfully in Q3 2026, driven by dry powder deployment pressure from LPs, and the "12 is the new 5" framing gets superseded by multiple expansion as macro stabilizes. If deal count is up >15% in Q3 vs. Q1 and software sector multiples recover to 2024 highs, the Bain framing was too doom-forward and the valuation model recalibration is unnecessary.

The falsifiable edge: if Bain's mid-year read is wrong about the structural math shift — and multiple expansion comes back because inflation rolls over and rates drop — then this brief was too bearish and the right Hartley move was to move faster on acquisitions now, not wait for the new math to force better prices.
