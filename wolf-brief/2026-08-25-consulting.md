# WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-08-25

The dark horse post: a daily synthesis of the strategy-firm and market research your CIO doesn't have time to read, filtered to one corner of the universe almost nobody else is watching — the intersection of PE roll-ups, family-office capital, and agent AI.

## The surprise

Buried in a Q2 data release from FINTRX — a family-office intelligence vendor, not a name you'll see cited by CNBC — is a number worth sitting with: of the 96 new family offices added to their platform this quarter, **92.7% want direct investments and 89.6% want private equity. Just 10.4% want hedge funds. 6.3% want private credit.**

That's not a mild preference. That's the widest gap FINTRX has ever recorded between what brand-new family-office money wants and what the existing, older cohort of family offices holds (hedge fund interest across the full database sits at 38.2% — still nearly 4x the new-cohort number). And the reason isn't subtle: 68.6% of this quarter's new family offices are first-generation, entrepreneur-origin wealth — people who built something by operating it directly, now allocating capital the same way. They don't want to hand it to a black-box fund. They want to run it, or co-run it, themselves.

## Why you haven't seen this

FINTRX publishes quarterly data-vendor blog posts, not glossy PDFs with a McKinsey masthead — it's the kind of release that shows up in wealth-management trade press (ThinkAdvisor, InvestmentNews) three days later, gets one paragraph, and disappears. Nobody at a hedge fund is forwarding this internally. But it's a direct read on where a specific, fast-growing pool of capital is headed, and it lines up with a quieter theoretical paper making the rounds in the NBER Handbook on the Economics of Transformative AI — Hadfield and Koh's "An Economy of AI Agents" — which argues the next wave of institutional design has to account for AI agents as semi-independent economic actors, not just tools bolted onto existing structures. Put the two together: the capital wants direct operational control, and the tooling to run direct operations at fund-scale without fund-scale headcount is arriving at the same time.

## What it means

For anyone building an operator-run PE roll-up instead of a traditional fund — courting family-office capital directly, with transparent operating metrics instead of quarterly LP letters — this is a tailwind that's easy to miss because it's not being reported as one. The smart money among first-generation family offices isn't just tolerating direct-deal structures; it's actively rejecting the alternative. Roll-up platforms that can show real-time operating data to a family office instead of asking them to trust a fund wrapper are underpriced relative to where this capital is trying to go.

## If I'm wrong

Falsifiable prediction, graded here on **2026-11-23**: if this is a durable structural shift rather than a 2026-specific liquidity artifact, FINTRX's Q3 2026 report should show new-cohort hedge-fund interest still at or below roughly 15% (versus 10.4% now). If instead it snaps back above 15%, treat the "family offices are structurally exiting commingled vehicles" read as premature — a rate-and-liquidity-cycle story, not a generational one — and discount the tailwind claim above accordingly.
