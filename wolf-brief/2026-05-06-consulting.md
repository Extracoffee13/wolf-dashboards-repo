## The Surprise

Roland Berger just named the thing nobody in the AI industry wants to admit: **companies have learned to make AI work, but they haven't learned to make AI pay.**

They call it "profitless prosperity." Massive AI investment. Measurable capability gains. Flat ROI.

The follow-on finding is the one that matters for The Construct: **as foundation models commoditize, the moat is no longer the AI — it's the data nobody else has.** Roland Berger is explicit about it. The firms that win will be those that own proprietary, industry-specific datasets that can't be replicated by a competitor who simply buys a ChatGPT license. Generic AI tools are becoming a utility, like electricity. You don't build a competitive advantage by being good at plugging things in.

Here's why most readers haven't seen this: Roland Berger is a second-tier strategy firm in the U.S. press. Their research rarely gets amplified the way McKinsey's does. But on AI strategy for mid-market operators — which is The Construct's exact universe — Roland Berger has consistently been sharper, less hedged, and more operationally grounded than the Big Three.

---

## Paper 1 — The One That Sharpens Our Thesis

**Roland Berger: "The AI Value Gap" (Profitless Prosperity in AI)**  
*Published: Early May 2026 | rolandberger.com*

The core argument: the competitive edge was never going to be the AI model itself. It was always going to be the data that makes the model proprietary. Roland Berger frames the strategic imperative as moving from "buyer" to "builder" — from licensing off-the-shelf AI tools to owning the intelligence layer that trains on data no one else can access.

**For Brand 9:** Every physical sign Brand 9 touches — monument, wayfinding, homebuilder community entrance — represents a data point about where people go, how they navigate, what communities are growing. That's a proprietary observation layer that no AI vendor can replicate. The question isn't whether to use AI in the signage business. It's whether Brand 9 is systematically capturing the data its physical footprint generates.

**For Hartley Capital:** Deal flow visibility, LP relationship data, sector-specific due diligence learnings — these are the orchestration traces of a PE operation. The firms that capture and structure this data now will train agents in 2027 that perform categorically better than competitors who started later. You cannot buy your way to this advantage. You have to build it.

## Paper 2 — The One From the Lab That Becomes Real in 12 Months

**arXiv 2605.02801: "Reinforcement Learning for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems through Orchestration Traces"**  
*Published: May 2026 | arxiv.org*

Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5 demonstrated something that will matter enormously in 18 months: you can train multi-agent systems on how they *coordinate*, not just what they *produce*. They ran Agent Swarms at 4,000 coordinated steps using Parallel-Agent Reinforcement Learning (PARL). The agents didn't just get better at tasks — they got better at working together.

The operational implication: every agentic workflow you run today is generating training data for tomorrow's agents — *if you're capturing the orchestration telemetry*. Most enterprises are not. The logs are the moat. Firms that start running agentic workflows now and instrument them correctly will compound an advantage that becomes structurally unassailable by late 2027.

For Hartley Capital's agent AI buildout: instrument everything from day one. The telemetry is not IT overhead. It's the asset.

## What It Means — One Sharp Takeaway

The strategy firms this week are collectively describing a bifurcation that's arriving faster than the market expects: between operators who own proprietary data + agent telemetry, and operators who buy commodity AI tools and wonder why the ROI doesn't materialize.

The Construct sits on both sides of the right side of that line — if it moves now. Brand 9's physical footprint is a proprietary observation layer. Hartley Capital's deal flow is an irreplicable training corpus. The risk is treating these as operational assets rather than strategic ones.
