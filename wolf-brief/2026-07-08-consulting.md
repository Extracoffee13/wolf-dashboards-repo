# WOLF Brief — The math on roll-ups just doubled, and the money moving in doesn't seem to know it

*2026-07-08*

Two reports landed within a month of each other, from two firms that don't talk to each other, on two different subjects. Put next to each other, they say something neither one says alone — and almost nobody is reading them together, because one is filed under "private equity strategy" and the other under "wealth management," and the analysts who cover those beats don't share a Slack channel.

**Report one, Bain, June 8:** the growth rate a buyout needs to hit a 2.5x return has roughly doubled in ten years — from ~5% annual EBITDA growth to ~10-12% — because you can no longer count on multiple expansion or cheap leverage to do the rest of the work for you. Bain calls it "12 is the new 5." It's a quiet reframe of the entire PE playbook: financial engineering used to be enough. Now it isn't.

**Report two, Roland Berger, this week:** family offices are pulling money out of venture capital and crypto and pushing it into private equity — funds and direct deals — faster than into almost anything else, with AI now rated a top-three thematic priority alongside infrastructure and healthcare.

Neither report mentions the other's finding. But read together: **more capital is chasing roll-ups at exactly the moment the return bar for roll-ups got twice as hard to clear.** That's not a stable equilibrium. Some of that capital is about to get a return math lesson.

The firms that clear the new bar won't be the ones buying growth with leverage — they'll be the ones who can show real operating leverage: cost structure that improves because the business runs on agents, not because the balance sheet got more aggressive. Roland Berger's own data hints at this — family offices say they want "professionalized internal structures" and AI exposure, not just PE exposure. That's a specific ask, and most roll-up shops don't have a specific answer to it yet.

Why you haven't seen this take: these are two mid-tier strategy-firm publications, in different verticals, both behind the kind of paywalled-adjacent friction that keeps generalist finance media from cross-referencing them. Nobody's incentive is to connect a wealth-management asset-allocation survey to a PE operating-math memo. That's the gap this brief lives in.

**If I'm wrong:** by early October 2026, "AI operating model" or "agentic operations" language will not have become a standard fixture in PE roll-up pitch decks or family-office allocator due-diligence checklists — i.e., LPs will keep writing checks into roll-ups on the old growth-plus-multiple story without demanding evidence of operating leverage. If that's still true in 90 days, the thesis that "operator quality with real AI leverage becomes the differentiator" is wrong, and capital is just chasing PE as an asset class regardless of story quality.
