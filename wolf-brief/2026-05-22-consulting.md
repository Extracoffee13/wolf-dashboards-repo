## The Surprise

**McKinsey just made the case that agentic AI in real estate is a capital-flow event, not a technology event.**

"Where AI Is Creating Real Value in Real Estate" (McKinsey, May 15) buries the most important number in the middle: coordinating agents across an entire leasing or operations *workflow* — not one step of it — produces 10–30% improvements in NOI, operating costs, and cycle times. That's not a productivity gain. That's the kind of number that changes underwriting assumptions.

The MGI labor productivity analysis underneath this puts $430–550 billion in annual value on the table globally across real estate, construction, and development. But the mechanism isn't AI magic — it's domain redesign. McKinsey's framing: stop deploying tools, start redesigning *domains* (leasing is a domain; operations is a domain; asset management is a domain). Each domain gets rebuilt end-to-end with human–agent partnership. The firms still running pilots are not wrong about AI — they are operating at the wrong unit of analysis.

**Why most readers haven't seen this yet:** The paper is categorized under McKinsey's "Explainers" series, which reads as introductory. It isn't. The NOI data and the $430B MGI figure are buried under consumer-facing prose. Most CRE operators scanning headlines will skim it as a 101 piece. The operators who read it carefully will have a 12-month head start on workflow redesign before their competitors realize this is a thesis document, not an explainer.

## The Second Paper Worth Your Time

**PwC just published the quiet counterargument to every AI transformation pitch deck you'll see in PE diligence this year.**

"Data and AI Readiness for PE Portfolio Companies" (PwC, updated May 19–20) says, bluntly: most middle-market portcos lack the operating infrastructure to run real AI. Fragmented systems, manual workarounds, no data backbone. The recommendation is not "go slower" — it is "favor reliability over novelty." Basic dashboards. External data feeds. Monitoring that operators actually use. That's where the returns are.

The exit pressure is new and real: PE buyers in 2026 are scoring portcos on AI-readiness in diligence. A portco that hasn't built even a minimal data backbone is going to take a valuation discount before it gets a chance to pitch its AI ambitions.
