## The surprise

Buried in a Roland Berger survey of 88 family offices: **geopolitical risk just overtook interest-rate risk as the #1 concern**, 88% vs. 65% a year ago. That's not a gradual shift, that's a regime change in how the most patient capital in the world perceives threat. And the money is already moving — into private equity and defensive hard assets, with infrastructure, healthcare, and AI as the sector priorities that survived the reshuffle.

Pair that with an Oliver Wyman Forum report out the same day: across transportation, logistics, and defense, only **8% of CEOs use AI at scale** — but the ones who do are seeing revenue gains above 20%. Everyone assumes AI adoption in old-economy industrials is basically done. It's 8% done. The gap between the 8% and the 92% is the trade.

Put those two together and you get a specific, unfashionable thesis: family-office capital is rotating toward PE structures at the exact moment a massive, underpriced AI-adoption gap has opened up in the unglamorous, capital-heavy sectors PE actually operates in.
