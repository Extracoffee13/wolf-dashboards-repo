# WOLF Brief — Consulting Pulse

**Beat:** Strategy firm intelligence filtered for PE roll-ups, agent AI, and signage/real estate operators

## The Surprise: Asset Managers Tripled AuM — and Made No More Money

BCG dropped its Global Asset Management Report on April 28, and most of the financial press headlined the growth story: $147 trillion in global AuM, nearly triple 2010's base. What they missed is the line buried three pages in — **profit margins have held flat at ~30% the entire time**. Every dollar of growth has been perfectly consumed by cost growth. Asset management built an empire on rising markets and ended up with the same economics it started with.

BCG's thesis: AI is the first force that can actually break this. Not incrementally — structurally. Firms redesigning their operating models (not just layering AI onto existing workflows) are projecting 25–35% cost reductions, 2–5x research coverage expansion, 3–5x client manager throughput. Early movers are already documenting it. The catch: capital is consolidating faster than most firms are acting. The top 10 US passive fund providers captured **more than 90% of net inflows** over the past decade. Private markets capital is gravitating to fewer, larger platforms. Firms still running AI pilots in mid-2026 are not just behind — they're operating on borrowed time.

**Source:** [BCG Global Asset Management Report 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/rebuilding-asset-management-for-an-ai-first-world) — published April 28, 2026

---

## Paired Signal: The Moat Is Not the Agent — It's the Governance Around It

On the same day, McKinsey published its "State of AI Trust 2026" data, and the sharpest number in the report is not the 62% of companies experimenting with agents. It's this: **high performers are 2.8x more likely to have formally defined human-in-the-loop validation processes** (65% vs. 23% of laggards).

BCG's companion piece on the strategy function adds the mechanism: AI agents are now doing parts of strategy autonomously — pulling data, detecting signals, triggering analyses before any human queries the system. The firms moving fastest are not those with the most capable models. They are those with the clearest intervention protocols. Trust architecture — auditability, defined escalation points, explainable intervention — is the actual competitive moat. It also happens to be what LP governance will eventually require.

**Sources:** [McKinsey State of AI Trust 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/tech-forward/state-of-ai-trust-in-2026-shifting-to-the-agentic-era) | [BCG Corporate Strategy Function in an AI-First World](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-corporate-strategy-function-in-an-ai-first-world)

## Why Most Readers Haven't Seen This

The BCG asset management report will be read by asset managers. The McKinsey agentic trust piece will be read by technology leaders. Almost nobody is reading both through the lens of a PE roll-up operator who is *also* building a multi-agent ops stack. That's the aperture this brief covers.

The structural insight — flat margins despite 3x scale, broken only by AI operating model redesign, in a market consolidating faster than most operators are moving — is the kind of thing that looks obvious in hindsight and actionable right now. For a platform like Hartley Capital, this reframes acquisition targeting: operators that haven't committed to an AI operating model are better candidates to acquire, not less attractive ones. The inefficiency is the opportunity. Buy the laggard, wire it for AI economics, exit into the consolidation wave that BCG documents is already in motion.
