### The Surprise

Real estate — the asset class that stores more American wealth than any other — is the *least* AI-adopted major industry in the United States.

BCG quantified it this week: only 25% of real estate firms qualify as AI leaders, versus 40% across industries. The sector invests roughly **half the cross-industry average in AI** — trailing pharmaceuticals, utilities, and manufacturing. This is not a startup sector fumbling with new tech. This is an industry that moves trillions of dollars annually, where margin is everything, and where the firms sitting at the bottom of the AI adoption curve are burning 400–700 basis points of margin they could recapture in development alone.

BCG's number: AI-first development companies are compressing project timelines by **~30%** and accelerating capital deployment cycles. That's not a rounding error. That's the difference between one project per year and 1.3.

**Why most people haven't seen this:** Everyone reads the BCG AI reports on tech, financial services, and healthcare. The real estate version dropped May 14 and barely registered. The sector's insularity — where deal brokers still run on Excel and Salesforce workarounds — means the research doesn't circulate. The people who would act on it aren't in the rooms where it's discussed.

### The Second Signal (Arxiv, May 6)

Buried in arxiv this week: a paper formalizing a security problem that every team building multi-agent AI systems is quietly accumulating.

**"Authorization Propagation in Multi-Agent AI Systems: Identity Governance as Infrastructure"** (arXiv 2605.05440) argues that when AI agents delegate tasks to other agents across organizational boundaries, classical security models (RBAC, ABAC, permissions-as-permissions) silently break. The problem isn't prompt injection — everyone knows about that. The problem is that a chain of authorized agents can collectively access and synthesize data that no single agent was authorized to see. The paper names this *aggregation inference* and derives seven structural requirements for fixing it.

Why it matters here: anyone building agent infrastructure for PE operations — deal sourcing agents, portfolio monitoring agents, research synthesis agents — is building exactly the architecture this paper is warning about. The fix is not expensive, but it must happen at the workflow design level, not bolted on afterward.
