# WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-16

*The dark horse: a daily synthesis of the strategy-firm white papers your CIO doesn't have time to read.*

## The surprise

Two reports landed this week from firms that don't talk to each other, and put side by side they say something neither one says on its own.

Roland Berger surveyed 88 family offices and found them re-weighting hard toward private equity — funds and direct deals both — as their strongest-growth allocation, with more than half planning to increase it further. Geopolitical risk, not rates, is now their top worry, and AI is one of their three named sector priorities.

BCG, in the same week, told CIOs they have a 6-to-12-month window to get agentic AI governance right before today's ad-hoc agent deployments harden into unmanageable tech debt — and framed it as a board-level problem, because most enterprises are still stuck running agent pilots, not agent operations. Oliver Wyman's parallel research on transportation, logistics, and defense firms put a number on it: only 8% of CEOs in those sectors say they're running AI "at scale."

Put those two findings together: the capital that funds roll-ups is actively moving toward more direct PE exposure right now, and the operating capability that would make a roll-up defensible — real, governed, at-scale agentic AI, not a pilot deck — is something fewer than 1 in 10 companies actually has. That's not two unrelated data points. That's a mispricing window.

## Why you haven't seen this take

Because nobody reads BCG's CIO-governance research and Roland Berger's family-office survey in the same afternoon. They sit in different verticals of the same firms' own websites, aimed at different buyer personas (tech leadership vs. wealth management), and neither report mentions the other's subject. The synthesis only exists if someone is deliberately reading strategy-firm output through a PE-and-agent-AI lens at the same time — which is exactly the corner of the research universe The Construct sits in and most readers don't.

## The takeaway

If you're raising or deploying capital from family offices into operating businesses right now, "we run this on agentic AI" is still a genuine differentiator, not a checkbox — because the base rate of real agent-ops maturity across the market is still under 10%. That won't stay true. The LP appetite is already ahead of the operator supply; the window is the gap between those two curves closing.

## If I'm wrong

Grading date: 2026-10-14 (90 days out). This thesis is wrong if, by then, Bain's next Global Private Equity report or KPMG's next Pulse of Private Equity shows "agentic AI operating maturity" or equivalent language showing up as a named top-3 due-diligence criterion in more than 25% of surveyed GPs. If that shows up that fast, the market caught up before the window closed, and the differentiator we're describing here was already priced in.
