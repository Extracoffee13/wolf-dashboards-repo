# The dark horse: four strategy firms just told you agentic AI hit a wall, and none of them said it to each other

**2026-09-01**

Nobody published a blockbuster paper in the last 48 hours. That's not the story — the story is what shows up when you stack four firms' *separate* 2026 output next to each other and read it as one signal instead of four.

McKinsey: agentic tasks burn roughly 1,000x the tokens of a chat task, and about 60% of that spend isn't the answer — it's the checking, repairing, and re-verifying that comes after. BCG: only 5% of companies are actually agent-first despite eighteen months of "the year of agents" messaging, and the firms that succeed spend 70% of the effort on people and change management, not model quality. Deloitte: production deployment is staying selective while governance maturity falls further behind autonomy every quarter. KPMG, most bluntly: nearly half of executives have already pulled back agent deployments because token-metered costs exceeded the benefit.

None of these four firms is talking to the others. Each arrived at some version of "the capability is ahead of the economics, and the economics is ahead of the governance" from a different survey, a different client base, a different quarter. That convergence — not any single paper — is the signal worth paying for.

**Why you haven't seen this framed this way:** each firm is publishing it as their own proprietary finding, wrapped in their own methodology, competing for the same "we called it" headline. Nobody in consulting has an incentive to say "we all found the same thing," because the whole business model is selling you their unique lens on it. Reading across the paywalls is the only way to see the wall is real and not firm-specific spin.

**What it means for The Construct:** if you're underwriting a PE roll-up thesis that assumes agentic AI lets you strip cost out of acquired operations fast, this is the data telling you to slow the clock. The firms that are actually winning with agents are the ones treating deployment as an 18-month change-management program with a hard cap on unmonitored token spend — not a six-month bolt-on. That reshapes how aggressively Hartley Capital should price agent-ops synergies into a deal model: discount the year-one number, not the thesis.

**If I'm wrong:** grade this in 90 days (by 2026-11-30). The falsifiable claim is that the *next* round of enterprise AI surveys (Q4 2026 KPMG Pulse, next Deloitte State of AI checkpoint) will show the agent-pullback number *holding or worsening* — not reversing — because the cost-per-verified-task problem doesn't get solved by better prompting, it gets solved by cheaper inference or better agent architectures, and neither is landing broadly by Q4. If instead the pullback number meaningfully improves (fewer than a third of executives reporting pulled-back deployments, with cost cited as the reason), this thesis is wrong and the wall was a 2026-H1 blip, not a structural ceiling.
