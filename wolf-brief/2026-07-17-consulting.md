# The dark horse: LPs and GPs are reading the same PE market and coming to opposite conclusions

Two reports came out of the strategy-consulting world this cycle that nobody is reading side by side, because they sit in different trade verticals and neither one made a mainstream business-press splash. Put together, they say something sharper than either says alone.

**Roland Berger** surveyed 88 family offices across the DACH region and found 55% of them planning to *increase* their private equity exposure right now — private equity is their single biggest growth vehicle for 2026, ahead of infrastructure, ahead of AI, ahead of everything.

**Bain & Company's** own midyear PE report, published a few weeks earlier, describes the opposite mood on the operator side: the "recovery" that PE firms were counting on for 2026 has stalled, hit by three shocks at once — an AI-driven collapse in software valuations, redemption stress rippling through private credit, and an oil-price spike from the Iran war. Bain's advice to GPs is basically triage: stop trying to time the cycle, make fewer bets, and make them disciplined.

**Why you probably haven't seen this juxtaposition:** the family office study is a DACH-region survey most English-language business press will never touch, and Bain's midyear report gets cited for its AI soundbite while the "three shocks stalled everything" framing — the actual news — gets buried under it in most write-ups.

**The takeaway:** the people writing the checks (LPs, family offices) are getting *more* bullish on PE at precisely the moment the people deploying the checks (GPs, operators) are telling each other to brace for impact. That gap is either a signal that LP capital hasn't caught up to a deteriorating market yet — or it's a signal that disciplined operators willing to buy into the current dislocation (distressed software sellers, credit-stressed sponsors needing to sell) are about to get very favorable terms from motivated counterparties while their overleveraged competitors are busy managing their own balance sheets instead of sourcing deals.

**If I'm wrong:** by mid-October 2026, lower-middle-market PE deal multiples will have held flat or *increased* rather than compressed further, and no notable "SaaSpocalypse" casualty will have surfaced in trade press as a distressed roll-up target. If either of those things happens, the dislocation-buying-opportunity read was wrong and the LP optimism was the accurate signal all along.
