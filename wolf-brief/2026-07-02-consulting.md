## The surprise

Three Yale/Rochester/LUISS economists just quietly proved that "AI exposure" is a priced, tradeable equity factor — not a vibe, not a multiple story, an actual number, backed by 380 trillion tokens of real-world AI usage data pulled straight from OpenRouter.

The paper is called ["AI Premium"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30583). Borri, Liu, and Tsyvinski build an "AI Factor" out of real token/dollar/user growth across 400+ models, estimate each public company's "AI Beta" from how its stock actually co-moves with that factor, and then just... trade it. Value-weighted, long the high-beta names, short the low-beta names. Result: 64.1 basis points per week. That's not a backtest built on sentiment scraping or narrative tagging — it's built on metered consumption data, the same kind of usage telemetry that used to be locked inside cloud vendors' billing systems and is now visible enough to factor-model.

Same week, BCG published a companion pair — ["Return on AI"](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-ceos-can-optimize-ai-token-costs) — telling CEOs and CFOs the exact same underlying fact from the other side of the ledger: token-metered AI billing is now hitting capex, opex, and COGS simultaneously, and boards need a "Return on Intelligence" metric or they're flying blind on AI spend. Read together, these two papers are the same story told from two sides of the same trade: the cost side (BCG, for operators) and the alpha side (academia, for allocators). Nobody at either publication seems to have noticed they're describing the same phenomenon.
