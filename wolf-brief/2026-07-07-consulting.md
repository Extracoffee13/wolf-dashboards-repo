# The $50k Slide Deck Just Described Our Stack

BCG published a paper yesterday telling Fortune 500 boards to build something we'd call, internally, a Tuesday. It's titled ["Decision Agents Bring AI into the Boardroom"](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/how-ai-decision-agents-transform-strategy), and it describes an AI system that sits in on executive committee meetings as a standing member — briefed in advance, updating its assessment live as discussion moves, running scenarios on demand, converting the room's conclusion into follow-up actions automatically. BCG's example failure mode: an exec asks "what happens if demand softens in Q3?" and the honest answer is a week of spreadsheet archaeology before anyone can say.

Here's the surprise: that's not a research preview of something coming. It's a description, with a consulting-firm price tag attached, of the pattern a lean AI-native operator can already run today for a fraction of what a BCG engagement costs to *design* it, let alone what it costs a Fortune 500 to build it internally with a platform team and a change-management budget. The gap between "documented in a strategy firm's flagship publication" and "operating in production at a two-person shop" has basically closed. Most people don't see this because the read is split across two audiences that don't talk to each other — BCG's paper lands on innovation-officer desks at companies large enough to retain BCG, while the people already running this pattern are small enough that nobody's writing the trend piece about them. The signal that matters — small AI-native operators are running what F500 boards are being told to build — is invisible from inside either reading list.

Layer in a second data point from the same week: [Roland Berger's family office study](https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Family-offices-adjust-asset-allocation-to-meet-evolving-challenges.html) finds family offices simultaneously increasing PE allocations and chasing AI exposure as a sector theme, in that order of magnitude. That's the fundraising environment for exactly this kind of platform, right now, not in some future fund cycle.

**Why it matters:** once a top-3 strategy firm names and blesses a pattern, the clock starts on it becoming table stakes. The window where "we run an AI decision agent" is a differentiator rather than a checkbox is probably 12-18 months, and it just got shorter by however long it takes a BCG-retaining PE shop to read this paper and greenlight a build. The move isn't to build the decision agent — most of that's arguably already built — it's to get the fundraising narrative in front of family offices *while* they're actively reallocating toward both PE and AI themes, not after the pattern is commoditized and every deck says the same thing.

**If I'm wrong:** grade this in 90 days (October 2026). The falsifiable claim is that at least one top-25-AUM PE firm publicly announces or is reported to have deployed an internal "decision agent" in investment-committee workflow, citing AI-augmented decision-making as a differentiator in an LP letter, conference talk, or press piece. If no such announcement surfaces by then, the "consulting-paper-triggers-copycat-adoption-within-a-year" thesis is wrong, and the real bottleneck is organizational inertia, not idea diffusion — which would mean the actual edge is patience, not speed.
