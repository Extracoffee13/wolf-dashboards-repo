# The Dark Horse Brief — 2026-07-13

## Nobody's underwriting the agent-cost line item, and that's the actual risk in every PE roll-up right now

Bain published a quiet piece in May that most people who follow AI headlines never saw, because it ran in the "opex modeling" corner of their site, not the splashy agentic-AI-is-here corner: agent and token costs are on track to replace **20–30% of headcount operating expense** at technology-forward companies. That's not the surprise. The surprise is the second half of the finding — Bain is explicit that there is **no smooth glide path**. It's "wildly nonlinear," with double-running costs (you're paying for the agent *and* the human during the handoff), organizational whiplash, and entire quarters where the unit economics just don't work.

Pair that with a KPMG data point from a month later that almost nobody cross-referenced against it: as of Q2 2026, the number of companies running *coordinated, multi-agent* workflows doubled quarter over quarter — from 9% to 18%. But only **26%** of those same organizations have real-time visibility into what their AI systems actually cost to run. Nearly three-quarters of the market is scaling agent orchestration faster than it can see the bill.

Put those two facts next to each other and you get the actual risk sitting inside every deal model in PE right now: operators and their boards are increasingly underwriting agent-driven EBITDA expansion — Bain's own separate midyear PE report says the era of multiple-arbitrage returns is over, so real operating alpha is now mandatory, not optional — while running the agents that are supposed to deliver it without the cost telemetry to know if they're actually profitable yet. That's a margin-expansion story built on a number three-quarters of the industry admits it can't currently measure.

Most readers haven't seen this pairing because these are two mid-tier releases from two different firms, three months apart, filed under "opex modeling" and "quarterly pulse survey" — neither one is a headline. The headline-grabbing consulting content this month is all "AI leaders outperform" (BCG, July 9) and "here's our agentic AI framework" (everyone). The unglamorous, unsexy data — the stuff about cost observability lagging deployment — is where the actual near-term blowups will come from.

**If I'm wrong:** by October 11, 2026 (90 days out), no PE-backed portfolio company will have cited an AI/agent token-cost overrun as a specific, named factor in a missed EBITDA target or guidance revision, in an earnings call, investor letter, or SEC filing. If that happens — if agent-cost line items start showing up as the explicit reason deals miss numbers — this thesis is confirmed. If a full quarter passes with agent-cost overruns invisible in any disclosed underperformance, grade this wrong and downgrade the urgency.
