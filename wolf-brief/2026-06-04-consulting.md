## The Surprise: Your AI Agents Cost 30x More Than You Think

EY published the first serious cost-of-ownership framework for enterprise agentic AI on June 1, 2026 — and it contains a number the AI enthusiasm cycle has been quietly burying.

A simple linear AI workflow cost $0.04 per interaction in 2023. A 2026-vintage orchestrated multi-agent system — the kind every PE-backed digital transformation is now pitching — costs $1.20 per interaction. **That's a 30x increase in two years, driven not by model pricing but by orchestration overhead: routing infrastructure, retrieval layers, governance controls, and human-in-the-loop review.** EY is calling the discipline that manages this "Agent FinOps," and their message is blunt: treat token spend like headcount — budget it, measure it, or lose control of it.

The backdrop: Gartner, cited in the EY report, projects that **more than 40% of enterprise agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027** — not because the technology failed, but because costs escalated beyond what the original business case modeled.

Most readers haven't seen this yet because it's buried in a cost-accounting framework piece, not a flashy AI strategy report. The firms pitching "agentic transformation" are not leading with this number. The firms paying for it will discover it on their cloud invoice.

### Why This Matters Here

WOLF is a multi-agent orchestration stack — research → synthesis → brief → signal → vault. That is exactly the architecture EY flags as living in the 30x cost zone. **Hartley Capital needs an Agent FinOps ledger before the stack scales, not after.** For PE due diligence: any portco presenting an agentic AI business case without a cost-governance layer is underwriting risk the model hasn't priced.

## The Sharper Find: Real Estate Is the AI Laggard — and That's the Opportunity

BCG benchmarked AI adoption across the full property value chain and found that only **25% of real estate firms qualify as "AI leaders"** — compared to 40% across all industries. The sector invests roughly half the cross-industry average in AI, lagging even utilities.

The margin opportunity for development companies (DevCos) that do move: **400–700 basis points of margin uplift**, with project timelines compressed by approximately 30%. The problem AI is actually solving: 66% of RE projects experience schedule overruns and 39% face cost overruns — chronic fragmentation that AI-driven procurement and site management directly addresses.

**For Brand 9:** The homebuilder and GC clients you serve are squarely inside the 75% of RE firms not yet AI-leading. That's not a threat — it's a positioning window. Signage and wayfinding is the visible, shovel-ready layer of an AI-first job site. While your clients' PMs are debating when to pilot construction AI, the physical world still needs to be labeled, navigated, and handed over. First movers get compressed timelines and faster close-outs; Brand 9 benefits when projects finish sooner.

Fewer than 20% of enterprise agentic AI projects will be canceled in 2027, and "Agent FinOps" will look like Y2K-era anxiety in retrospect.

If that's true: the urgency in EY's framework is overblown, and the right move is to build the stack now and let cost curves catch up. If it's wrong: every dollar of untracked agent token spend is compounding, and the 40% project cancelation rate will show up first in financial services and regulated industries — exactly Hartley Capital's territory.
