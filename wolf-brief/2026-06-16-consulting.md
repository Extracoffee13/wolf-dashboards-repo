## Strategy Intelligence: What the Consultancies Know That Nobody's Talking About

### The Surprise

Bain just released its PE Midyear 2026 report and buried the most important data point in a technical footnote: PE deals now require **12% EBITDA growth over five years** to hit a 2.5x return — up from 5% a decade ago. That's not a bad year. That's a structural repricing of the entire asset class. And it means the only PE strategy that still pencils at current entry multiples is the one most generalist LPs haven't underwritten: **AI-embedded mid-market buy-and-build**.

Roland Berger dropped the other shoe this week: **90% of companies report AI returns lagging investment**. Not because AI doesn't work — because firms are layering it on top of broken systems. The 10% who consistently capture ROI share one discipline: they pick one narrow use case, connect it to a measurable financial outcome, and rebuild the substrate to support it. Everyone else is running pilots that never graduate.

Put those two findings together and you get the only clear trade available in current PE: acquire mid-market businesses in fragmented verticals, embed AI at the substrate level (not the dashboard level), and underwrite the EBITDA improvement as a Day 1 plan — not a Year 3 aspiration.

### Paper #1: Bain — "Control the Controllable, Weather the Rest" (PE Midyear 2026)
*June 8, 2026 | bain.com/insights/private-equity-midyear-report-2026*

The broad PE recovery is dead — again. Three shocks hit simultaneously in H1 2026: an AI-driven software valuation collapse ("SaaSpocalypse"), redemption pressure in private credit, and an oil spike from the Iran conflict. But inside the wreckage, one sub-strategy is working: **mid-market buy-and-build** where deal headroom is widest and AI value-creation plans can move the underlying EBITDA in ways large-cap can't.

Bain's framing of AI in PE is no longer aspirational: "Inaction on AI is a strategic choice, not a neutral one." Firms that arrive at Day 1 without an executable AI plan are underwriting a thesis that won't pencil at current cost of capital.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:** The headline coverage focused on the "triple shock" macro narrative. The buried story — that the deal cost index is at an all-time high and the math only works with AI-driven margin improvement — hasn't broken through to LP conversations yet.

### Paper #2: Roland Berger — "The AI Value Gap" (Profitless Prosperity in AI)
*June 2026 | rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/Profitless-prosperity-in-AI.html*

200+ senior executives across three continents. 90% say AI spending outpaces returns. Roland Berger's diagnosis: the failure isn't capability — it's integration depth. Firms are buying Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini and bolting them onto legacy systems. When they try to deploy agents, the agents fail because **the underlying systems were never designed to be machine-navigable**. "AI agents can think logically but cannot reliably operate within company systems" is Roland Berger's precise language — and it's the exact failure mode you see in every enterprise AI pilot that stalls in month four.

The 10% who win: specific use cases, clean data, financial KPIs attached from day one. That's it. That's the whole formula.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:** The report is published in a niche B2B advisory slot that doesn't make LinkedIn trending topics. The data is from March 2026 and hasn't been picked up by the AI-enthusiasm press, which prefers coverage of what's possible over coverage of what's failing at scale.
