## The Surprise No One Is Acting On

BCG just documented the exact reason why most PE firms will waste the bulk of their AI investment over the next 18 months — and it isn't about which tools they choose.

The finding, in "AI-First Enterprise Operations" (June 15, 2026): **organizations that redesign processes end-to-end with agentic AI achieve 60%+ cost reductions. Organizations that add AI to existing processes achieve less than 20%.** That's not a performance gap — it's a structural ceiling. The firms getting 60%+ have rewired process ownership to agents and restructured human roles around exception handling. The firms getting less than 20% are running AI alongside their existing workflow. They've added a lane to the highway but haven't changed the speed limit.

The conventional PE playbook right now is "deploy AI tools across the portfolio." That is the task-level approach. It gets you sub-20%. BCG's agentic deployment data shows the jump to 60%+ requires saying "this entire process no longer works the way it used to" — and making that decision at acquisition, not 18 months post-close after the management team has built institutional attachment to the existing workflow.

**Why most readers haven't acted on this:** The headline ("AI delivers cost savings") is everywhere. The hidden wedge ("only end-to-end process redesign unlocks the majority of that value") lives in an executive perspectives PDF that requires you to already believe process architecture is the real unit of transformation. Most PE operators are not there yet. That's the window.

## The Exit Math Has Quietly Broken

Bain's PE Midyear Report (June 8, 2026) is more alarming below the fold than the headlines suggest. The triple shock — AI SaaSpocalypse in software, private credit stress, Iran oil spike — has left 33,000 portfolio companies with no near-term exit. But the structural issue is worse: generating a 2.5x return over a 5-year hold now requires **~12% annual EBITDA growth**, up from ~5% a decade ago.

That number should be on every deal memo. Roll-up targets underwritten at 5% organic growth are going to miss — not because the deal was bad, but because the return math has changed. The ones that work are the ones where agentic process redesign can generate genuine EBITDA growth, which loops directly back to BCG's finding.

There is also a buyer's opportunity in the wreckage: 33,000 stuck portfolio companies means distressed bolt-on targets at compressed multiples, available to anyone with dry powder and an operating playbook already built.
