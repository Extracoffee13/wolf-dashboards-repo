## The Surprise

## Top Papers This Cycle

### 1. McKinsey — "How Agentic AI Can Reshape Real Estate's Operating Model" (Mar 4, 2026)

McKinsey's Global Institute pegs $430–550B in annual value at stake across real estate, construction, and development from agentic AI automation. Early live deployments are showing 30%+ time savings on maintenance, 3–7% lift in tenant renewal rates, and lead response times accelerating by 90%+.

The finding most people skip: McKinsey explicitly says value doesn't come from pilots. It comes from redesigning entire operational domains so agents work inside systems of record, not alongside them. Leasing, maintenance, construction coordination, finance, tenant experience — five domains, all requiring end-to-end workflow redesign, not point-solution overlays.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:** It was published in March but the GRI Institute is surfacing it again this week, which means institutional real estate is only now stress-testing the thesis against their own ops. The window between "McKinsey said it" and "your competitor has done it" is shrinking.

**What it means:** If you work with FL homebuilders or commercial real estate operators, the lead response time finding alone is the pitch. A 90% acceleration in lead response — via agentic AI — is a competitive advantage that compounds. The operators who redesign their leasing and maintenance workflows around agents in 2026 will have structural cost and velocity advantages by 2027 that are very hard to reverse-engineer.

---

### 2. Deloitte — "AI Agents Are Scaling Faster Than Their Guardrails" (2026)

Only 21% of organizations have mature governance for agentic AI. 74% expect to be running agents at scale within two years. The top risks? All governance-layer: data privacy, legal/IP/compliance, oversight capability, model explainability.

The implicit argument: the firms that build governance infrastructure *now* — before enterprise procurement demands it — end up with a moat, not just a compliance checkbox.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:** Everyone is focused on capability ("can the agents do the task?"). Deloitte is focused on the layer below: "do you have the audit trail, the role-scoped permissions, the escalation protocols to deploy agents safely at scale?" That question is only starting to get asked in board rooms.

**What it means:** For any multi-agent deployment — whether it's a hedge fund automating due diligence or a roll-up platform running agents across 12 portfolio companies — governance infrastructure built before you scale is the competitive variable. Build it now while most competitors are still in pilot mode.

## The Takeaway

Both papers are saying the same thing from different angles: **the value in agentic AI is structural, not incremental.** It doesn't live in the pilot. It lives in the decision to redesign the operating layer and govern it before your competitors do.

The firms that figure this out in 2026 will look, by 2028, like the firms that moved to SaaS in 2012 — not because the technology was magic, but because they restructured the work around it while everyone else was still debating the ROI.
