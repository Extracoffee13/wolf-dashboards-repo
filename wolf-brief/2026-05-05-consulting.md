## The Surprise

McKinsey just handed anyone building an agent ops stack the most useful single data point published this quarter: **62% of organizations are experimenting with AI agents. Fewer than 10% are scaling them in any given function.**

That gap isn't a technology problem. McKinsey says it plainly — it's an *infrastructure* problem. Organizations that close it first will see infrastructure transform from a cost center into the orchestration platform for how all work gets done. Those that don't will spend 2–3× more on IT by 2030 with flat budgets, running the same pilots in year three that they're running today.

Most readers haven't seen this framing because it's buried in a tech-practice paper targeted at CTOs, not investors. But the investment signal is louder than the operational one: **the 62%→10% gap is a moat that closes fast.**

**Source:** McKinsey, *Reimagining Tech Infrastructure for Agentic AI* (~April 2026)  
https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/our-insights/reimagining-tech-infrastructure-for-and-with-agentic-ai

## The Follow-On

BCG published a piece in March that reframes the whole real estate industry in one sentence: **"Real estate is no longer won in capital markets — it is won in operations."**

The argument: buildings must become platforms — adaptive, continuously optimized, integrated into digital ecosystems. AI, industrialized construction, and next-gen facility management are the mechanisms. The tell: BCG reports most real estate firms plan to spend less than 1% of revenues on AI. That's not conservatism. That's the gap that active operators exploit.

Why most readers haven't seen this: it's a strategy-firm report addressed to institutional real estate players, not homebuilders or signage firms. But if buildings are becoming platforms, *everything embedded in those buildings* — wayfinding, signage, spatial identity — moves from specification to infrastructure.

**Source:** BCG, *Future of Real Estate: Ecosystems and Active Ownership* (March 13, 2026)  
https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/future-of-real-estate-ecosystems-and-active-ownership

## What This Means

Two theses, confirmed in the same week from two different directions:

**For Hartley Capital:** The agent ops bet is correct but the infrastructure layer is the actual position. Experimenting is table stakes; scaling is the game. Build the foundation before the gap closes and it becomes commodity.

**For Brand 9:** The BCG brief is a sales tool. When a homebuilder or commercial developer pushes back on intelligent wayfinding as a premium line item, the answer is BCG's own thesis: the developers who win in 2026-2030 are the ones who treat their buildings as platforms. Brand 9 is the interface layer of that platform. That's not a signage conversation — that's an ops conversation.

## If I'm Wrong

**Falsifiable prediction, grade in 90 days (August 4, 2026):**

If the infrastructure moat thesis is wrong, we'll see the agent-scaling percentage jump from <10% to 20%+ by Q3 2026 as hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google) commoditize agent infrastructure fast enough to erase the first-mover advantage. In that world, the moat shifts entirely to data and governance — not the stack. We'd need to reposition Hartley's agent ops thesis away from infrastructure and toward proprietary data assets and agent governance frameworks as the defensible layer.

Watch the McKinsey State of AI survey (typically drops Q4) for the first empirical update to the 10% scaling figure.
