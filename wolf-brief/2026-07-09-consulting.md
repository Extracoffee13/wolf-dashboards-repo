## The surprise

While every strategy-firm blog this week is running some flavor of "AI will change work, but calm down, jobs aren't disappearing," the sharpest thing published in the last 72 hours on autonomous agents didn't come from McKinsey, BCG, or Bain at all. It came from a 22-page arXiv paper out of UCL's Institute of Finance & Technology, and it's not about whether agents take your job — it's about what happens once agents start *paying each other*.

## Agent-to-Agent Finance: the paper nobody in your feed is reading

Hui Gong's ["Agent-to-Agent Finance: Blockchain Payments and Trust Infrastructure for Autonomous AI Agents"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00245) makes a simple but underrated claim: AI agents are graduating from analytical tools to transacting counterparties. They negotiate with other agents, call external tools, and increasingly initiate payments or on-chain transactions without a human in the loop for each one. If that's true, markets need infrastructure for four things agents don't currently have: verifiable identity, payment rails built for machine speed, reputation systems, and audit trails that hold up when nobody was watching the transaction happen.

**Why most readers haven't seen this yet:** it's a systematization paper on arXiv's q-fin board, not a press release — no PR team, no LinkedIn push, filed under a category most finance and consulting readers never browse. The Big Four are still publishing skills surveys. The actual infrastructure debate is happening in a venue they're not reading.

## Runner-up: KPMG's family business report just handed PE a sourcing signal

[KPMG's Global Family Business Report 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ai-and-technology/global-family-business-report.html) surveyed 1,927 leaders across 41 jurisdictions and found family enterprises expect to be mostly board-governed and professionally managed by 2035 — but most admit their governance, AI-readiness, and talent capability aren't keeping pace with that transition. Read plainly: that gap is a target list. Family businesses that can't professionalize fast enough on their own become the acquisition candidates roll-up shops are built to find.

## What it means

Two threads, one implication: the infrastructure for agents acting as economic counterparties is being drafted right now, in low-visibility venues, while the operating businesses that will eventually run on that infrastructure — including the family enterprises PE firms are courting — are openly telling surveyors they aren't ready for it. That gap is where the next 18 months of deal flow and platform-building both live.

## If I'm wrong

**Falsifiable prediction, graded 2026-10-07:** by then, none of the Big Four or MBB firms will have published a report specifically on agent-to-agent payment/identity/trust infrastructure (as opposed to general "agentic AI in the enterprise" content). If McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, PwC, EY, or KPMG puts out a dedicated agent-to-agent commerce infrastructure report before that date, this call — that the real infrastructure conversation is happening outside the consulting majors' radar — is wrong, and we'll say so.
