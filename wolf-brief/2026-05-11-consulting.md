## Paper 2: The Coordination Problem Nobody Is Solving

**arxiv 2605.03310 — "Coordination as an Architectural Layer for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems"**  
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03310  
Published: May 2026

Multi-agent AI systems fail in production at rates between 41% and 87%. The cause is almost never the AI model. It's coordination — the architecture layer that determines when agents spawn, how they hand off work, and when they stop. Every major agentic AI framework (LangChain, AutoGen, IBM Watsonx Orchestrate, Google ADK) currently conflates coordination with agent logic, making failures nearly impossible to diagnose systematically. This paper proposes separating coordination into its own configurable layer — and provides empirical evidence that doing so dramatically improves reliability.

**Why most readers haven't seen this:** It's an arxiv preprint from two researchers — no marketing budget, no press release, no consulting firm amplification. But the 41–87% production failure statistic is the most important number in enterprise agentic AI right now.

**What it means for The Construct:** Before Hartley Capital signs any agentic AI vendor contract or deploys agents in fund operations, this paper is required reading. The coordination-layer framework gives you the right due-diligence questions: how does this system handle delegation? how does it aggregate results across agents? can you inspect and tune coordination independently of model performance? Vendors who can't answer these questions haven't solved the hard problem.
