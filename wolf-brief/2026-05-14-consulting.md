# WOLF Brief — Consulting Pulse
### 2026-05-14 | Strategy Firms + arxiv

---

## The Surprise

The consulting industry just handed agentic AI a $64 trillion justification — and framed it as infrastructure, not software.

EY dropped a report this week co-authored with Cambridge and NVIDIA arguing that agentic AI isn't a tool you deploy inside a system. It's an **intelligence layer** that sits above your existing fragmented data systems and acts continuously — routing, synthesizing, flagging — without requiring you to rip and replace. The $64T number is the global infrastructure funding gap; EY's argument is that agentic AI is the unlock that makes the existing capital go further by eliminating the 15% cost drag from rework and coordination failure.

This matters beyond infrastructure: it is the clearest consulting-grade articulation yet of what "agentic ops at scale" actually looks like in practice. EY even built it internally for audit workflows and published the case study alongside the report. Not a whitepaper about future potential. A blueprint with a production deployment behind it.

---

## The Kicker

Roland Berger published complementary data that should unsettle anyone running AI "pilots": **90% of companies surveyed report AI returns lagging investment.** Financial services — hedge funds, asset managers, the entire Hartley capital stack universe — ranks as the worst-performing sector. The winners (they call them "Industrializers") all share one trait: they treat AI as an operational capability to be engineered, not a project budget to be spent. The losers are indistinguishable from winners on ambition, spend, and technology access.

Most readers haven't seen this because Roland Berger is smaller than McKinsey or BCG and its AI work gets buried under the louder voices. But the 200-company, three-continent dataset here is rigorous, and the "Industrializer vs. Stalled vs. Observer" taxonomy is more useful for operator-level decision-making than anything the big three have published on AI ROI this cycle.

## Why Most Readers Haven't Seen This Yet

EY's infrastructure report landed in the Romania/Netherlands/UK newsrooms rather than the US feed — it's indexed under newsroom subdomains most aggregators miss. Roland Berger's full methodology ran in German financial press before the English summary surfaced. Neither made the AI Twitter digest circuit this week.

## What It Means for The Construct

**For Hartley Capital:** The Roland Berger framework is a diligence lens. Any target company pitching AI capability should be tested against the Industrializer criteria: Is AI a line item or a capability? Is it in production or in pilots? Financial services is the sector with the most "Stalled" firms — which means the spread between AI-native and AI-lagging fund ops is about to widen fast.

**For Brand 9 / Built Environment:** The EY "intelligence layer" framing is the argument for why a signage or wayfinding platform operating on large-scale development projects should be talking to the GC's agentic AI stack, not just the architect's drawing set. Spatial data, wayfinding logic, and project sequencing are exactly the siloed, high-coordination-cost data flows this report is describing.

---

## If I'm Wrong

**The prediction:** By August 14, 2026, at least two institutional GPs or family offices with >$1B AUM will publicly disclose that they are restructuring their AI investment/operations programs around a "capability" model (dedicated team, infrastructure budget, ongoing training) rather than a project-by-project procurement model — and will cite the Roland Berger or EY frameworks by name or proxy.

**How it gets falsified:** If the next 90 days show continued proliferation of one-off AI pilots at major allocators with no structural shift in how AI is resourced, and if the "Stalled" cohort stays stable or grows in Roland Berger's follow-on data, the thesis that consulting frameworks are changing operator behavior is wrong. The money is moving; the operating models may not follow on the timeline asserted here.
