# The Consulting Pulse: Nobody published the memo that matters this week

**2026-09-02**

Here's the surprise: I scanned ten strategy firms' publications feeds and this week's arXiv postings for anything touching PE roll-ups, agent AI, or hedge fund/family-office ops. In the 24–48 hours before this ran, none of them published anything on that lane. What they did publish — climate resilience for CROs, European retirement systems, back-to-school retail spend — is the normal exhaust of a research machine that isn't currently pointed at the question that matters to a PE roll-up shop right now.

That's not "nothing happened." It's that the real signal isn't a single headline — it's three separate reports from the last month, from three different firms, quietly converging on the same claim without citing each other:

- **Oliver Wyman** (July): 49% of PE-backed CEOs now name "falling behind on AI deployment" a top-3 threat to the business — more than double the rate for public-company CEOs.
- **KPMG US** (August): AI-native workflows are now a stated *top priority* for leading PE sponsors, not a pilot-stage curiosity.
- **Bain**, Global PE Report 2026: the return bar has moved — "12 is the new 5" — meaning today's deals need EBITDA growth roughly double what got a deal done five years ago, and multiple expansion isn't there to cover for weak integration anymore.

Nobody wrote the sentence connecting those three dots, so here it is: **the EBITDA bar went up at exactly the moment operating margin has to come from somewhere other than headcount, and AI agent deployment is the only lever left that scales with add-on count instead of against it.** That's not a forecast, it's already priced into how PE-backed CEOs describe their own fear. The research shops are treating it as three unrelated data points because none of them owns the "roll-up + agent ops" beat end to end — which is exactly the gap a firm like Hartley Capital's engine is built to sit inside.

**Why you likely haven't seen this framed this way:** each of the three source reports lives in a different firm's PE practice, aimed at a different reader (Oliver Wyman → PE-backed CEOs, KPMG → PE sponsors' ops teams, Bain → LPs and GPs on returns), and none of them is trying to make the cross-firm argument. Nobody's job is to sit across all three and notice the pattern — that's the corner of the universe this brief exists to cover.

**If I'm wrong:** grade this in 90 days (by 2026-12-01). The prediction is that at least one major strategy firm (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, or a Big Four) publishes a report explicitly framing AI agent deployment as *the* mechanism for hitting higher post-LBO EBITDA growth targets on add-on-heavy roll-ups — not just "AI helps PE" in general, but the roll-up-specific version of the argument. If nothing that specific ships by then, the connection was mine to make, not an emerging consensus, and I was reading three unrelated data points as a trend that wasn't there.
