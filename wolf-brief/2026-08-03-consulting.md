# The dark horse: PE operators are more scared of AI than public companies are

*2026-08-03 · WOLF Consulting Pulse*

Everyone's narrative for mid-2026 was supposed to be "AI ROI skepticism sets in." That's not what's showing up in the actual consulting research this week.

Oliver Wyman just ran a 415-CEO survey with the NYSE and found something that should be the headline and isn't: 49% of private-equity-backed CEOs name falling behind on AI as a top-three threat to their business — more than double the 22% of public-company CEOs who say the same. PE operators aren't hedging. Finite hold periods and concentrated ownership mean they don't get the luxury of "let's see how this AI thing shakes out" — they're on a clock, and they know it.

Pair that with BCG's number from two days later: companies that are winning on AI are spending roughly 1.7% of revenue on it, versus 0.8% for the ones falling behind. That's not a rounding-error gap. That's the difference between treating AI as core infrastructure and treating it as a tool you bolted on.

**The sharp takeaway:** the "smart money is getting cautious on AI" story is backwards. The smart money — the operators with the shortest time horizons and the most skin in the game — is the *least* cautious cohort in this data. If you're structuring a PE roll-up, running a family office allocation, or evaluating an operator's AI story in diligence, "we're being thoughtful about AI" is starting to read less like prudence and more like a lagging indicator.

**Why you probably haven't seen this:** this is sitting on Oliver Wyman's and BCG's gated advisory-insights pages, not in TechCrunch or the WSJ AI beat — which is busy writing the "bubble" story. Nobody's cross-referencing the PE-CEO urgency data against the enterprise investment-gap data in the same breath, because they're two different firms' reports published two days apart in the same news cycle nobody's reading end to end.

**If I'm wrong:** by November 1, 2026, deal-level data (PitchBook or Preqin Q3 2026 releases, or CIM/teaser language from sub-$100M-revenue roll-up deals) should show a measurable valuation or multiple premium tied to demonstrated agent-AI capability in PE-backed operators. If that premium isn't showing up in actual deal terms by then — if this is still survey sentiment with no capex or multiple data behind it — I'm wrong, and this was noise, not signal.
