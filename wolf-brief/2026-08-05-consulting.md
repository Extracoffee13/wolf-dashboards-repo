# The Dark Horse — 2026-08-05

**The tool PE firms are most scared of missing is the same one that just clipped their recovery.**

Oliver Wyman's cut of the 2026 NYSE CEO Agenda survey — 415 CEOs, roughly 10% of global market cap, PE-backed vs public sliced apart — found PE-backed CEOs are more than twice as likely as public-company CEOs to name "falling behind on AI deployment" a top-3 existential threat (49% vs 22%). AI oversight is climbing PE board agendas faster than public boards too. Read alone, that's the standard 2026 headline: private equity is going all-in on AI as the growth lever.

Read next to Bain's midyear PE report, published a month earlier and largely ignored outside deal circles, it looks different. Bain names the thing that braked H1 2026's PE recovery a "triple shock" — and one leg of it is an AI-driven "SaaSpocalypse" that compressed software multiples hard enough to widen bid-ask spreads and stall exits across the industry. Same technology, same six-month window: the thing PE boards are racing to deploy is also the thing that just took a chunk out of the sector's software-adjacent portfolio value.

**Why you haven't seen this framed together:** Oliver Wyman's report reads as a workforce/AI-adoption story; Bain's reads as a macro/dealmaking story. Nobody's publication calendar puts them on the same page, and neither firm has an incentive to point at the other's finding. But if you're underwriting a roll-up with any AI-augmented SaaS-adjacent asset in it, both are describing the same market at the same moment: AI is the thing you can't afford to skip and the thing that can still reprice you overnight.

**If we're wrong:** by November 3, 2026 (90 days out), if no PE-backed roll-up with material AI-augmented SaaS or agent-tooling exposure has taken a public down-round, write-down, or delayed exit citing multiple compression traceable to this "SaaSpocalypse" dynamic, this thesis is dead and we'll say so.
