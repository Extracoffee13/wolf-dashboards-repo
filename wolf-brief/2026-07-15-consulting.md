# The 30x Number Nobody's Pricing In

*WOLF Consulting Pulse — 2026-07-15*

Here's the surprise: EY just put a number on something everyone building "agentic AI" has been hand-waving past. A simple AI workflow — ask, retrieve, answer — costs about four cents a run. A real agentic system, the kind with tool calls, reasoning loops, and retries, costs about a dollar twenty. That's not incremental. That's 30x. And Gartner is telling anyone who'll listen that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be dead by the end of 2027, killed by exactly this — cost blowouts nobody modeled, not capability gaps.

Nobody outside the CFO's office is talking about this because "agent economics" isn't a sexy headline next to "AI agents will add $4 trillion in value" — the number every other firm is chasing this year. But the $4 trillion number is the top line. The 30x number is the cost of goods sold. One of those determines who's still standing in 2028.

Pair that with what Bain just published in its PE midyear report: the market spent H1 2026 punishing exactly the kind of platform strategy that treats "buy more companies" as the value-creation plan. Tech deal value dropped 70% quarter over quarter as the software-multiple bubble deflated. Bain's advice to the firms that will actually win the back half of the year: fewer bets, real operating improvement per asset, AI-driven value creation instead of financial engineering.

Read those two together and you get the actual thesis, which nobody at either firm connected out loud: **the roll-up strategies that win the next 24 months are the ones where "run agents cheaply" is itself the operating improvement** — not a side project bolted onto a platform, but the thing that makes the platform's unit economics different from its competitors'. Most PE-backed operators are about to find out the hard way that "we added an AI agent" and "we added an AI agent that doesn't cost 30x what we budgeted" are very different sentences.

**If I'm wrong:** by mid-October 2026, if agentic-AI-project cancellation rates (trackable via Gartner's ongoing survey cadence, or via layoff/pivot announcements at agent-infra vendors) are running flat or down versus where they sit today rather than climbing toward that 40%-by-2027 trajectory, this thesis is dead — it'll mean the cost curve bent down faster than EY's benchmark implies, probably on the back of cheaper inference, and "cheap agent ops" stops being a differentiator because it becomes table stakes for everyone simultaneously rather than a moat for the disciplined few.
